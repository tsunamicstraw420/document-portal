Part 1: The 7 Skills Worth Building

Skill #1: LinkedIn Carousel Content System
    What you’re really selling: An authority machine. The appearance of a thought leader who
    publishes consistently — without the thought leader spending 10 hours a week doing it.

    What it does: Takes a topic, a brand voice brief, and a color palette. Generates a complete
    branded LinkedIn carousel as production-ready HTML — 8 slides, each one designed with the
    hook, body, and visual hierarchy that the LinkedIn algorithm rewards. Every slide renders at
    1080×1080, screenshottable, uploadable. Delivered as a full month of content: 4 carousels, 4 short
    posts, ready to schedule.

    Who pays for it: B2B companies investing in executive personal branding. Marketing agencies
    running LinkedIn for C-suite clients. Any brand where the founder or CEO is the face of the
    company and LinkedIn is a pipeline channel.

How to Build It
    • Run a 20-minute voice intake: how does the client write naturally? Pull 5–10 existing posts,
    emails, or talks. Feed them to Claude and extract a voice profile.

    • Build a master carousel template: 6 slide types (hook, problem, insight, example, proof, CTA) that
    get populated per topic.

    • Define Claude’s design rules for this client: brand color, accent, font weight, whitespace.
    Document once, reuse every month.

    • Generate your first carousel, screenshot every slide, drop into a single PDF. That PDF is your
    sales asset.

    • Add short-form posts: for every carousel, Claude generates a companion short post and a
    question post to drive comments.
   How to price it: $2,000–3,500/month per client.

   Why it sells: LinkedIn is the single highest-ROI organic channel in B2B right now. You close the
   ‘CEO on LinkedIn’ gap in one call.


Skill #2: Branded Email Template System
   What you’re really selling: Brand consistency at scale — and the ability to ship a campaign email
   in hours instead of days.

   What it does: Generates a complete HTML email template library, platform-compatible with
   Marketo, HubSpot, Salesforce Marketing Cloud, or Klaviyo. Covers every email type: product
   announcement, newsletter, onboarding, event invitation, promotional campaign, re-engagement.
   Every template is modular.

   Who pays for it: B2B SaaS companies with a marketing ops function. E-commerce brands
   running weekly campaigns. Director of Email Marketing, Head of Demand Gen, or Marketing
   Operations lead.

How to Build It
   • Get the client’s brand kit: logo files, hex codes, font names, and any existing email HTML.

   • Build the master template first: header, hero image zone, headline, body copy, CTA button,
   footer.

   • Generate each template variation from the master — same structure, different hierarchy.

   • Test every template in Gmail, Outlook, and Apple Mail. Check dark mode. It cannot break in
   Outlook.

   • Deliver: HTML files, 1-page usage guide per template, and a style guide.

   How to price it: $4,000–7,000 for full library build + $1,000–2,000/month for ongoing additions.

   Why it sells: A broken or off-brand email going to 100,000 subscribers is a brand incident. You’re
   selling risk elimination and speed.


Skill #3: Event & Campaign Asset System
   What you’re really selling: A coordinated visual moment — every touchpoint of a campaign
   looking like it came from the same brain, delivered before the agency has finished the kickoff deck.

   What it does: Takes a single event or campaign brief and generates the complete asset kit: email
   invitation, confirmation email, event page header, social posts (LinkedIn, Instagram, X), countdown
   graphic, speaker cards, post-event recap, and thank-you email.
   Who pays for it: Corporate events teams running 6–20 events/year. B2B companies launching
   products. Agencies managing event marketing. Events Manager, Head of Field Marketing, or
   agency account director.

How to Build It
   • Define the campaign visual language upfront: one primary color, one accent, one headline font,
   one photography direction.

   • Build email assets first — they have the longest lead time and most technical constraints.

   • Generate social assets at platform-correct dimensions: 1200×627 for LinkedIn/X, 1080×1080 for
   Instagram.

   • Build the countdown graphic as an animated HTML component — a $1,500–3,000 add-on that
   takes 20 minutes in Claude.

   • Package: zip file of all assets, 1-page asset map, and deployment checklist.

   How to price it: $3,000–5,000 per event kit. Monthly webinar retainer at $2,500/month. Annual
   conference packages $8,000–12,000.

   Why it sells: Event teams are always behind. You deliver in 3 days what takes an agency 6
   weeks.


Skill #4: Animated HTML Explainer & Product Demo
   What you’re really selling: Motion that communicates — not just motion that looks pretty. A
   product demo without a $20,000 motion studio invoice.

   What it does: Takes a product brief and generates an animated HTML explainer using CSS
   transitions and JavaScript. Scroll-triggered animations, SVG path draws, and sequenced reveals.
   Works as a standalone page, embeds in a website, or exports as video via screen capture.

   Who pays for it: SaaS companies needing a product explainer. Startups raising seed round.
   Marketing agencies building campaign microsites. CMO, Head of Product Marketing, or agency
   creative director.

How to Build It
   • Map the narrative first: Problem → insight → solution → how it works → outcome. The animation
   serves the script.

   • Start with structural HTML: each narrative beat is a section. Build static version first.

   • Layer in animations: CSS keyframe                    entry   effects,   scroll-triggered    JavaScript
   IntersectionObserver, SVG path animations.

   • Add the product demo layer: HTML replica of key UI screens with animated user flow.
   • Test on mobile — CSS animations behave differently on iOS Safari.

   How to price it: $2,500–6,000 per explainer. $1,500/month retainer for quarterly updates.

   Why it sells: Every SaaS company needs a product explainer. Most have a 2-year-old
   placeholder. You offer the same output at a fraction of the price.


Skill #5: Ad Creative System — IAB Banner Set
   What you’re really selling: A production-ready display ad campaign — HTML5 banners that
   animate, perform, and go straight to the media buyer’s trafficking sheet.

   What it does: Takes a campaign brief and generates a complete IAB-standard HTML5 banner ad
   set across all 8 standard sizes: leaderboard (728×90), medium rectangle (300×250), wide
   skyscraper (160×600), half page (300×600), large rectangle (336×280), mobile banner (320×50),
   large leaderboard (970×90), and billboard (970×250).

   Who pays for it: In-house media teams. Performance marketing agencies. Paid Media Manager
   or Performance Marketing lead.

How to Build It
   • Lock the creative brief: one hero headline (under 8 words), one supporting line (under 12 words),
   one CTA (under 4 words).

   • Build the master banner first — always start with 300×250. It becomes the visual reference for
   every other size.

   • Generate each size as a separate HTML file. Claude adapts layout per size.

   • Animate with a 3-beat CSS sequence: beat 1 (0–2s) hook, beat 2 (2–4s) solution, beat 3 (4–6s)
   CTA.

   • Test every banner: clean animation loop, legible text at small sizes, under 150KB file weight (IAB
   standard).

   How to price it: $1,800–3,500 for a full 8-size set. $500–800 per additional A/B variant.

   Why it sells: Display advertising doesn’t stop because creative production is slow — it just runs
   the same tired banners. You fix that.


Skill #6: Mobile App UI for Local Business
   What you’re really selling: A real app experience — not a wireframe, not a mockup. A clickable,
   tappable HTML prototype that looks and feels like a native app.

   What it does: Takes a local business type and generates a complete set of mobile app screens as
   HTML at iPhone dimensions (390×844). Onboarding, home dashboard, service menu, booking
   flow, profile, notifications. Every screen is clickable — tap a button and it navigates to the next
   screen.

   Who pays for it: Local business owners quoted $15,000–40,000 for a custom app. Franchises
   prototyping loyalty apps. Digital agencies pitching app development.

How to Build It
   • Map 6 core screens per business type (e.g., restaurant: splash, home, menu, item detail, order
   confirmation, loyalty tracker).

   • Apply the brand: business name, logo colors, hero image or CSS gradient placeholder. Takes 10
   minutes.

   • Add bottom tab bar navigation with 4–5 icons that highlights active tab.

   • Wire tap interactions: onclick JavaScript show/hide system. No framework needed.

   • Deliver as a single HTML file, hostable on Netlify in 2 minutes so clients can tap through on their
   phones.

   How to price it: $2,000–4,000 for a 6-screen prototype. $500 per additional screen.
   $800–1,200/month retainer.

   Why it sells: A $2,500 prototype breaks the $30,000 mental barrier and starts a development
   relationship.


Skill #7: TikTok Carousel Generator
   What you’re really selling: Scroll-stopping visual content at machine speed — a week of TikTok
   carousels designed, formatted, and ready to post.

   What it does: Generates TikTok carousel posts as production-ready HTML at 1080×1920 (9:16),
   with bold typography and high-contrast color blocks. Each carousel is 5–8 slides. Claude
   generates 4–6 carousels per session, covering a full week of content.

   Who pays for it: DTC brands building organic TikTok. Agencies managing 5–20 social clients.
   Creators and personal brands. Social Media Manager or Head of Content.

How to Build It
   • Study the format: hook slide with bold statement; middle slides with one punchy idea each; final
   CTA slide.

   • Build the visual system: oversized headline, dominant background color, small brand mark. Less
   is more.

   • Define brand variants: primary (most slides), accent (hook/CTA), neutral (quote slides).
    • Generate in batches of 4–6: give Claude the week’s content calendar and have it produce all
    carousels in one prompt.

    • Add a caption system: TikTok caption under 150 characters, hook, 3–5 hashtags, and CTA for
    every carousel.

    How to price it: $1,500–2,500/month for weekly carousel package. $500–800/month add-on per
    client for agencies.

    Why it sells: TikTok rewards consistency. Brands need 4–5 carousels/week. Most can’t execute
    at that volume. You make it a part-time job.
