Totally doable — here's the razor method, step by step. The one thing to burn into memory: "Ripple Delete" is what closes the gap and keeps everything in sync. Plain Delete just leaves a hole.

Per bad spot:

  1. Find it and zoom in. Scrub to the flub (spacebar to play, or J/K/L to shuttle). Zoom the timeline in with the slider (or Cmd +) and watch the waveform — a flub usually looks like: words → a flat gap (the breath) → the same words again. You want to remove the first take plus that gap.
  2. First cut. Park the playhead at the start of the bad part and press Cmd+B (Split Clip at Playhead). That's the razor — cleaner than the blade tool because it cuts exactly on the playhead. (If you prefer the actual blade: press B, click the clip, then press A to get back to the arrow.)
  3. Second cut. Move the playhead to the end of the bad part and press Cmd+B again. The flub is now its own little chunk.
  4. Remove it and close the gap. Click that middle chunk to select it, then right-click → Ripple Delete (shortcut Shift+Delete). Everything after it slides left to fill the gap.
  5. Repeat for each flub.

Why not lock the video: leave V1 unlocked. Ripple Delete pulls the audio and the video above it left together, so they stay in sync and the episode just gets a couple seconds shorter. If you cut only the audio, the words would drift off from the pictures — so let it ripple both.

Even faster if you want to skip the razor: park on the flub, press I (mark in) at the start and O (mark out) at the end, then Shift+Delete. That ripple-deletes the whole marked range in one shot — no blading. Same result.

Handy: N toggles snapping (helps the playhead grab clip edges), Cmd+Z undoes any cut that lands wrong.
