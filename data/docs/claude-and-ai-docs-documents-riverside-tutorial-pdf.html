Why Riverside?
Editing is the single biggest thing that stops people from starting a podcast — most beginners don’t want
to wrestle with complicated software. Riverside.fm solves this by combining recording, editing, and
promotion in one browser-based tool. For roughly 80% of podcasters it can be the only software you
need. It pairs well with tools like Descript or Premiere Pro for advanced work, but you can go a long way
on Riverside alone.

1. Getting Oriented: The Dashboard
After logging in, you’ll see three main tabs on the left side:

    • Studio — your recording rooms. You can keep separate studios for different shows, or create a reusable
    1-on-1 “meeting room” link to replace Zoom.
    • Schedule — book recording sessions and invite guests directly inside Riverside.
    • Home / Projects — where recording starts and where finished recordings live for editing.

Scheduling a session
Go to Schedule → Create new session. Name the session, set the day and time (with time zone), enter your
guest’s email, and add an agenda in the description field. This lets you invite people without needing Google
Calendar.
2. Setting Up a Recording
From the Home tab, click Record. It does not start recording immediately — you get a setup screen first.
Configure these before joining:

Setting                 Recommendation

Headphones              Choose “I am using headphones” whenever possible (even AirPods). This keeps the recording
                        raw and full; you can add background removal later if needed. Only pick “not using headphones”
                        if you must — it turns on echo cancellation to stop speaker feedback.

Camera                  Pick your webcam (FaceTime/internal, a DSLR, or a phone via a tool like Camo). External
                        cameras show up here if connected.

Microphone              Select your actual mic / audio interface (e.g. a Focusrite Scarlett). You CANNOT change this
                        mid-recording without stopping.

Speaker                 Headphones if wearing them; internal speakers are fine otherwise.

Join role               Join as Host, or join as Producer — an “invisible” participant the host and guest hear but the
                        audience does not. Great for guiding nervous first-time hosts.

Desktop app             Optional “join via Riverside Desktop app” can run smoother, but the browser version works fine
                        for most people.

  Plug your computer into a wall outlet for maximum quality, and consider a USB hub/dongle if you’re juggling a webcam,
  mic, and laptop ports.
  Once you hit Record you can’t change camera/mic/speaker without stopping — double-check them first.



3. Inside the Recording Studio
After joining the studio, here’s what you’re looking at and the key controls.

Layout of the screen
    • Right side — Recording info: shows live quality (e.g. 720p) vs. your max enabled quality (up to 4K).
    Host and Producer see all individual tracks and can adjust each mic.
    • Center — the stage: guests can wait in a waiting room (like Zoom) or enter automatically. For solo
    episodes, just close the empty guest panel to go full-screen.
    • Settings icon: choose audio-only vs. audio+video (always record video, even if you only plan to publish
    audio — it enables clips and better eye contact).

Key studio settings (under “All settings”)

Tab                  What to do

General              Toggle the waiting room (recommended on, so you can get settled before letting the guest in). Set
                     studio name and your display name.

Design               Upload your logo, change colors/branding and background — like a branded Zoom room.
Recording               Confirm video + audio. Leave noise reduction off by default and apply it per-person only if needed
                        (you can add it in post). Set audio to 48 kHz; choose the Advanced / up to 4K option for best quality.

Transcript              Pick your language — Riverside generates the transcript automatically, in-app, included with your
                        plan.

Countdown               Keep the 5-second countdown on so you and your guest can settle before recording starts.

Live stream             Optional: connect YouTube, LinkedIn, Facebook, etc. to stream while recording.

Advanced                “Low data mode” hides everyone’s live video to preserve bandwidth on bad Wi-Fi — video is still
                        recorded.


Handy in-studio features

Feature                      What it does

Script / Teleprompter        Paste questions or a sponsor read; display it as a teleprompter with adjustable size and scroll
                             speed. A small top strip keeps your eyes near the camera.

Screen share                 Share your or a guest’s screen — recorded as a SEPARATE track for editing flexibility.

Chat / Whisper               Private side-chat between host, producer, and guest. Tell guests it exists so they know to switch
backstage                    to it.

Text overlay                 Drop lower-thirds call-to-actions (e.g. “Subscribe”) live; they fade out on a timer.

Media / Sound effects        Play drum rolls, laughter, applause or custom sounds live — saved as a separate track.

Layout                       Grid (everyone equal), Speaker split, or Speaker full-screen (great for focus). Choose Fill (use
                             all space) or Fit (leaves room for a background).

Marker (press M)             Hit M anytime during recording to bookmark a moment — it becomes the start of a clip. Guests
                             can do it too.



4. Ending the Recording (Don’t Skip This)
When finished, click Stop / Leave → End session for all. Riverside records locally on each device and then
uploads to the cloud. There is usually a short “wrapping up” upload after you stop.

  Both you AND your guest must leave the browser tab open until uploading completes (often up to ~30 minutes).
  Closing too early can delay your edit by days or weeks while you chase the guest. Warn guests in advance.
  If someone did close early, send them to the Riverside upload-recovery link (riverside.fm/upload). They reopen the tab,
  it finishes uploading, then they’re done.

Importing outside recordings
The Import tab lets you bring in recordings made elsewhere (Zoom, Premiere exports, even a published
YouTube link) so you can use Riverside’s editing and Magic Clips on them too.


5. After Recording: Files, Transcript & AI Show Notes
Open the episode under the Projects tab. The folder holds the full recording plus any clips and markers you
made.

Sharing with an editor
Click the Share icon (top right) → Copy link. This gives an editor or collaborator access to everything — all
individual tracks, clips, and markers — without exporting to Google Drive. Far fewer clicks.

Recording files you’ll use
    • High-quality aligned / raw video — the main files; tracks are time-aligned even if speakers’ lengths
    differ.
    • Cloud backup — a lower-resolution safety copy in case of recording problems (like Zoom’s cloud file).
    • Transcript — download it! It’s free with your plan. Feed it into an LLM (Claude, ChatGPT) to find clip
    moments, pull quotes, titles, and refined show notes.

AI-generated show notes
Riverside auto-generates a full content kit from the episode. Compare it with what an LLM produces, and refine
in your favorite tool:

Asset                  How to use it

Keywords               Ready-made tags for YouTube and other platforms.

Summary                Drop straight into your episode description (refine in an LLM if you like).

Takeaways              Each bullet is a potential tweet, thread, carousel, graphic, or even a whole new episode.

Titles                 Several options; double-check in an LLM for angles that are compelling but not clickbait.

Soundbites             Direct pull quotes — short ones (5–7 words) make great thumbnail copy.

Chapters               YouTube chapters — must start at 00:00 to auto-generate. Help viewers jump to and preview
                       sections.

Social snapshots       Auto-generated still and animated social graphics, including device mockups.



6. Generating the Episode & Magic Clips
On the project page you get three options: Magic Clips, Full Episode, and Edit from scratch. Generate both
Magic Clips and the Full Episode — even if you tweak or discard them, it’s useful to see what the AI flags as
strong moments.

Clip generation options
    • Length: under 30s, 30–60s, 90s+, or Auto (recommended first pass).
    • Focus on speaker: bias clips toward you, your guest, or Auto (best of both).
    • Keywords: enter a remembered word/topic to steer the AI toward a specific moment.
  Tip: Re-run clip generation — a second pass often surfaces different clips. Volume of options is the real benefit; you
  can always trim later.
7. The Editor (Text-Based Editing)
Click Edit on the AI-generated full episode (or any clip) to open the editor. Riverside edits like a document —
you edit the transcript and the video follows.

Core editing moves

Action                   How

Set aspect ratio         16:9 for YouTube/Spotify; 9:16 for vertical clips.

Delete a section         Highlight the words and delete — easily restored if needed.

Correct a word           Click a word → Correct to fix spelling (e.g. names). Fixes show up in captions.

Add media (B-roll)       Highlight text → Add → insert image, video, music, or text — layered without touching the
                         timeline.

Make a new clip          Highlight a moment → the plus / “create new edit” button turns it into its own clip.

Split (press S)          Cut the timeline at the playhead.

Search                   Find every mention of a word — great for hunting an awkward moment to remove.

Chapters sidebar         Jump quickly to chapter timestamps.

Color-coded tracks       Each speaker has a color so you can see who’s talking at a glance.

Add a hook               Upload or build an opening hook and slot it to the very front of the episode.



8. AI Producer, Audio & Branding
The right-hand sidebar holds the polish tools.

AI Producer
    • Remove pauses — cut silences; set the threshold in 0.5s steps (e.g. cut anything over 1.5s, or 0.5s for a
    tight, retention-focused solo episode).
    • Remove filler words — strip hundreds of “ums” in one click.
    • Remove for clarity — AI suggests sections to cut, with reasoning you can review or skip.
    • Magic audio, layouts, eye contact — extra one-click enhancements.

Audio & tracks
    • Tracks — adjust each speaker’s volume across the whole episode (waveforms update live).
    • Magic audio — auto cleanup; hit-or-miss, can over-process and clip words, so test it.
    • EQ presets — Default, Crisp, Clear, etc. to round out your voice.
    • Sync / drift fix — correct remote audio sync issues without leaving Riverside.

Brand kit (set once, reuse forever)
Upload intro/outro screens in every aspect ratio, set default caption styles, fonts, color palette (use exact hex
codes), and backgrounds. Riverside remembers these across every future clip and edit — a big time-saver.


9. Layout, Captions & Stock Media
    • Layout: Full-screen (recommended for full episodes — all eyes on the speaker), Grid, Picture-in-picture,
    or Smart layouts that auto-switch to reduce visual fatigue. Keep Fill on unless using a background.
    • Music: built-in licensed tracks, or upload your own intro/outro music.
    • Captions: trending presets (including the karaoke / Instagram-reels style); customize size, position, font
    and style, or pull from your brand kit.
    • Images & video: royalty-free stock B-roll built in, with smart suggestions drawn from your transcript — no
    separate Storyblocks/Envato subscription needed.
    • Text: lower-thirds and presets like “subscribe” or “check the links below.”


10. Exporting Your Episode
Go to the Export tab (top right). The Share icon also gives a preview link so a guest or teammate can review
before the file is fully rendered.

Option                            Guidance

Resolution                        Bump to 4K if your source supports it and you want it in the final output.

Remove watermark                  Turn this on (if your plan allows) to drop Riverside’s branding.

Normalize audio levels            Recommended — evens out loud vs. quiet speakers so listeners aren’t riding the
                                  volume.

Remove background noise           Optional; can over-correct and sound over-edited. Test before relying on it.

Audio format                      WAV = highest quality; MP3 = smaller file.

Timeline export (Premiere /       Use ONLY if you’ll finish editing in Premiere Pro or Final Cut — exports a project file for
Final Cut)                        a seamless handoff. (Descript is no longer an export option.)

  Pro move: export multiple test versions (e.g. plain, normalized, noise-removed) and put the setting in the filename so
  you can compare and pick the best.

After export, wait a few minutes; a download icon appears on the finished file in the Edit section. Download,
rename, and upload to your host (YouTube, Spotify, etc.). If connected to Spotify you can publish directly, but
downloading the file first is the safe default.


Final Advice
Repeat the export process for every clip you want. Don’t only make short vertical clips — an hour-long episode
usually hides a 5- or 10-minute segment that can be republished as its own upload and may outperform the full
episode. Mix short, medium, and long-form clips into your strategy.
Riverside is no longer limiting the way it once was. For most beginners it can be the only software you need for
a long time — and the skills transfer to more advanced editors when you eventually need them.
