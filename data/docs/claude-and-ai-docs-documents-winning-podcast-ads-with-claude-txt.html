# The Awareness & Angle Diagnostic

**When to use it:** Before you write a single line. This tells you which of the six winning structures fits your product and where your buyer’s head is at.

**Why it works**: The fastest way to kill a podcast ad is picking a hook built for the wrong awareness level. A discovery hook lands flat on a buyer who already knows the problem. A compliment hook feels random to someone who doesn’t yet believe change is possible. This prompt makes Claude diagnose first and recommend second.


Here is my product:
[PRODUCT NAME + ONE-LINE DESCRIPTION]
[WHAT IT DOES / THE MECHANISM]
[WHO IT'S FOR]
[THE MAIN PROBLEM IT SOLVES]
[PRICE POINT]

Here are 5-10 real customer reviews:
[PASTE REVIEWS]

Do three things:

1. Tell me the awareness level of my coldest profitable buyer (unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware). Explain your reasoning in 2 sentences.

2. Recommend which of these six podcast-ad structures fits best, and why:
- "I just found..." (discovery, for unaware buyers)
- "It blows my mind that X still..." (industry-norm contrast)
- "So you're telling me..." (misconception flip)
- "They say it takes..." (sticker-shock, better-way)
- "Haven't you noticed..." (pattern recognition)
- "Your [result] is so good..." (outcome-led, for buyers who know change is possible)

3. Give me the single sharpest opening line for that structure, in spoken language, under 12 words.

Do not pitch the product. The opening line should make a stranger lean in.
How to run it: Run this first. Keep the structure name it gives you. You hand that to Prompt 2.

# The Winning Script Generator

**When to use it:** Once you know your structure. This writes the full two-voice script on the beat skeleton that actually scales.

**Why it works**: It bakes in the rules that hold every winner together. The first 10 to 15 seconds never sell, and the product enters around the 28 to 33 second mark. It writes two real people having a conversation. Most AI hands you a monologue with a second voice added on top, and that is the version that fails.


Product:
[PRODUCT + MECHANISM + PRICE]
Coldest buyer & awareness level:
[FROM PROMPT 1]
Real customer language (pull phrases from these):
[PASTE REVIEWS]

Use this structure: [STRUCTURE NAME FROM PROMPT 1]

Write a 45-second script as a two-person dialogue. Voice A is the operator/explainer. Voice B is the audience proxy who asks the questions a real skeptical customer would ask.

Hard rules:
- Seconds 0-3: the hook. No product, no brand.
- Do not sell or name the product before the 0:28 mark.
- Voice B must push back at least twice with a real reaction line ("Wait, what do you mean?", "That sounds expensive", "No way").
- Use words from the reviews, not marketing language.
- Put a research or authority proof point in the back half.
- Close on one simple action, not a hard pitch.

Format the output with rough timestamps every few lines so I can hand it to actors.
How to run it: Paste the reviews in the same message. If the dialogue feels one-sided, run Prompt 3 on the weak lines.

# The Listener Reaction Line Engineer

**When to use it**: When the script reads like one person talking and a second person nodding along.

**Why it work**s: A line like “Wait, what do you mean?” or “That sounds expensive” is doing almost all the persuasion. It gives the explainer a reason to go deeper, and it mirrors the exact doubt sitting in the viewer’s head. Generate ten, keep the two that sting.

[PASTE SCRIPT]

The second voice (Voice B) is too passive. Rewrite Voice B's lines so they sound like a real, slightly skeptical listener who keeps interrupting with the doubts a real buyer would have.

Give me 10 alternative reaction lines I could drop in at the key turns. Each should:
- Be under 8 words
- Sound like spoken language, not ad copy
- Voice a real objection, surprise, or disbelief (price, "is this even legal", "that can't be true", "how", "no way")

Then mark the 2 you'd bet on and say why in one line each.

# **The Hook Variation Machine**

**When to use it**: When a script wins and you want to scale it. Same body, ten new front ends to test.

**Why it work**s: The hook decides the hook rate. The fastest growth lever you have is keeping a proven body and testing fresh openings across the six structures.

[PASTE WINNING SCRIPT]

Keep the body and the product reveal exactly as they are. Write 10 new opening hooks (first 3 seconds, spoken, under 12 words) that could replace the current one.

Spread them across these angles:
- discovery ("I just found...")
- industry-norm contrast ("It blows my mind that...")
- misconception flip ("So you're telling me...")
- sticker shock ("They say it takes...")
- pattern recognition ("Haven't you noticed...")
- outcome envy ("Your [result] is so good...")

For each: label the angle and the awareness level it targets. No product or brand name in any hook.

# **The Shoot-Ready Actor Brief**

**When to use it:** After the script is locked, before the studio day. This stops a great script dying on set.

**Why it works**: Actors have to be spontaneous and briefed on the persona, not the product. If they look like they are recalling the next line, the take is unusable. This prompt turns the script into a brief built around that one requirement.

[PASTE FINAL SCRIPT]

Produce:
1. A persona for each actor (who they are, their energy, how they speak). Brief them on the persona, not the product.
2. The emotional beat for each section (curious, skeptical, surprised, convinced).
3. Pacing notes: where to interrupt, where to pause, where the back-and-forth should speed up.
4. A "do not" list (no reading off-screen, no recalling lines, no salesy tone, no looking at camera).
5. The one moment in the script that has to feel completely real or the ad fails, with a note on how to direct it.

Keep it to one page an actor can read in two minutes.

How to run it: Pair the brief with a two-camera setup, a 50 to 85mm lens, and actors at a 45 degree angle. The brief plus that setup is what makes it read as a real clip.
