Why "a function of time" matters
Nothing plays on its own. There are no timers and no CSS animations running in the background. Frame
372 always looks exactly the same, so a slow laptop can never drop a frame, and 60 fps just takes longer.
It's also why the sound lines up perfectly: the page hands over a list of every pop and swoosh with its

03 · THE STORY



One conversation. Every screen change has
a reason.
My first version of this format jumped across eight screens and two chats. Nobody could
follow it. These are the rules that fixed it.
1. One thread from start to finish. One person, one problem, one chat. No second storyline.
2. Every app switch is caused by the beat before it. Stuck on a reply, so screenshot. Screenshot, so
   open the app. Copied, so paste. The viewer never has to work out where they are.
3. Show the pain with the sound off. Three replies typed and deleted says "I'm stuck" faster than any
   caption.
4. The app is the answer inside the flow, not a detour to a product page.
5. Use real language. I searched real dating openers first. "Two truths and a lie" tested best, so it became
   the hook.
6. End on a payoff notification. A calendar invite or a "good morning" text. It's the reward, and it's on
   screen in half a second.

The Rizz ad, beat by beat (39 s)

TIME         SCREEN                  WHAT HAPPENS


0–2 s        Lock screen             Notification: "Sofia: two truths and a lie. go 😏"

2–7 s        Dating app              Chat, then her profile: the taco prompt gets highlighted

7–12 s       Dating app              "Your turn" pulses. Three replies typed and deleted

12–14 s      Screenshot, home        White flash, thumbnail shrinks, swipe home, tap the app

14–22 s      The AI app              Upload, "Reading the conversation…", three reply cards, copy one

22–30 s      Dating app              Paste, send, she replies, "deal. thursday?"

30–35 s      Notifications           Calendar invite, then next morning: "good morning taco guy ☀️"

35–39 s      End card                Icon, promise, rating, Download button

0 5 · W H AT W E N T W R O N G



Five things that broke, so you can skip
them.
1          Too many screens
           Eight screens and two chats in 40 seconds read as chaos. One conversation, and a reason for
           every switch.



2 Emoji rendering as blank boxes
  Web emoji fonts load one character at a time, so anything that appears later in the video can render
       invisible. Fix: put every emoji and late-appearing word in a hidden block on the page so it loads up
       front.



3         Chat bubbles showing through other apps
          A bubble set to "visible" shows even when its whole screen is hidden. Tell Claude: bubbles use
          visibility "inherit", never "visible".



4          A long pasted message covering the keyboard
           Anchor the message box to the bottom so it grows upward, like a real phone.



5         Trusting it without looking
          Every bug above was caught on a contact sheet, not in the code. Make Claude look at one after
          every render.

06 · HOOKS AND TWEAKS



Test hooks, not whole ads.
Only the first two seconds change, so every new hook is a free re-render. Five that work
for app ads:

HOOK                             EXAMPLE


The notification                 Lock screen at 11:47 PM: "Sofia: two truths and a lie. go 😏"

The deleted draft                Open on "hey haha" being backspaced letter by letter

The badge                        "Your turn" pulsing over a message from two hours ago

The search                       A search bar typing "what do i reply to two truths and a lie"

The late-night lock screen       "Sofia: you're quiet… 👀" at 1:14 AM


Change anything in plain words
   "Use hook 3 and re-render."
   "Make the chat text bigger and slow the replies down."
   "Swap the dating app for a generic one with no brand."
   "Cut a 20-second version: hook, the app, the payoff."
   "Same structure, new app: [link]. Start from Prompt 2."


   Before you run it as a paid ad: show other companies' apps only in a generic style, use photos
   with a licence that covers ads, and keep the fine print. Organic posts are far more forgiving than ad
   review.
