  Behind the ad: why it holds attention for 58 seconds
  The ad is a comedy that sells without feeling like an ad until the product shows up. It
  works because of four choices:

   1. A genre hook, not a product hook. It opens as a breaking-news zombie outbreak.
      Nobody scrolls past a zombie apocalypse, and nothing tells you it's a coffee ad yet.
   2. The problem is the joke. The "zombies" turn out to be ordinary tired office workers at 3
      p.m. Every viewer who has had an afternoon crash sees themselves in it.
   3. An outsider who can't tell the difference. A small zombie with a clipboard walks the
      office and genuinely can't tell the coworkers from his own horde. The comedy carries
      the problem so the ad never has to lecture.
   4. One person who isn't a zombie. Maya is calm, sharp and awake, with a Ryze pouch on
       her desk. The contrast does the selling before a single claim appears.

  The claims only arrive once you already want to know what Maya has. Then a punchline
  pays off the joke, and the end card closes the sale.

  Three rules keep it working:

       Hold the product back until beat 4. If it appears in beat 1, viewers know it's an ad and
       leave.
       Give every line room. A full spoken line needs about 3-4 seconds. Rushed lines look
       fake on screen.
       Only use claims the brand already makes. The story creates belief, so the copy
       doesn't need to stretch.


  Fill-in template: turn your product into a story ad
  Answer the four questions first, then fill in the six beats. It takes about 15 minutes.

     FOUR QUESTIONS
     1. What does your customer look like when they have the problem?
        (tired, bloated, stressed, broke, messy, invisible...)
     2. Which famous genre already looks like that?
        (zombies, heist, nature documentary, courtroom, horror, sports
     commentary...)
     3. Who could mistake your customer for that genre?
        (a zombie, a detective, a narrator, an alien, a referee...)
     4. What are your product's 2-3 claims, word for word from the brand?


     SIX BEATS
     Beat 1, genre hook: Open on [GENRE] at its most dramatic. Headline: "
     [DRAMATIC LINE]"
     Beat 2, the flip: Reveal it's really [CUSTOMER] dealing with [PROBLEM]. Line:
     "[RELATABLE LINE]"
     Beat 3, the outsider: [OUTSIDER] can't tell [CUSTOMER] apart from [GENRE
     CREATURE]. Show [PROBLEM MOMENT].
     Beat 4, the exception: [HERO] doesn't have the problem. [PRODUCT] is visible
     on their [DESK / BAG / COUNTER].
     Beat 5, the mechanism: Clean product shot. On-screen: "[CLAIM 1]" / "[CLAIM
     2]" / "[CLAIM 3]"
     Beat 6, payoff + close: [PUNCHLINE THAT FLIPS THE GENRE JOKE]. End card: "
     [GENRE-THEMED CTA]" + [GUARANTEE] + [ACTION]


  Keep the end-card line in the genre. "Don't join the horde" works because it's still the
  zombie joke while it asks for the sale.
