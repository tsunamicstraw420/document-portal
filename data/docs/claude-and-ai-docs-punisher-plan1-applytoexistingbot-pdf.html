1. Source recap — what the article actually claims
Punisher (@0x_Punisher) publishes a Polymarket bot at polymarket.com/@pbot-6 claimed at $100k PnL on
May 9 2026. He frames the playbook as 8 steps. The dense, useful chunks are: (a) a 6-layer WebSocket
hardening system, (b) the win-rate-versus-entry-price math, (c) the Type 1 / Type 2 strategy split, (d)
adverse-selection filtering, and (e) the recurring claim that market-maker and ladder bots earn more than
directional bots.

Verbatim claims worth banking

●       Type 1 (90% of working bots): bet at window open from external signals (Binance direction, mean
        reversion, candles). AI-backtestable on strike + close + signal.
●       Type 2 (more profitable): mid-window trading, GTC bids, stops, order-book modeling. Requires
        self-recorded tick data.
●       Entry-price math: enter at 57c → need >57% win-rate to break even. Enter at 40c → need >40%. Rule
        of thumb: +5c break even, +10c profit.
●       Test both entries: window open AND 60s in. Segment by US vs Asian hours, weekday vs weekend.
●       Adverse selection: bidding 10c below ask at 40c often fills on the losing side. Filter for it.
●       Stop losses: no native stops on Polymarket; sometimes no-stop beats stop on short windows. Best on
        BTC with strict filters; avoid betting blindly above 85c.
●       Survivor strategies: 99 of 100 ideas die. The survivors are usually market-maker / ladder bots.
        Directional strategies work but are least profitable overall.

4. Phased plan of attack
Six phases, sequenced so each one validates before we spend on the next. Effort is engineer-days for one
person; cost is real-money exposure on the wallet. Phases A and B are blocking precursors; phases C-F can be
reordered once the data is flowing.

Phase A — Tick recorder (precursor for everything Type-2)
Why: He says 18+ months of self-recorded tick data is the biggest single edge. Without it, Type-2 strategies
(market making, ladder, mid-window) cannot be backtested honestly.

What it actually is:

●        Standalone Python service running on the same Vilnius VPS, separate Docker compose service.
●        Subscribes to Polymarket CLOB book WebSocket for every active BTC/ETH 5-min and 15-min market.
●        Persists every tick (bid, ask, sizes, mid, timestamp) to compressed daily files. Rotate at 00:00 UTC.
●        Parallel Binance WS recorder for the same window so we can replay both feeds aligned.
●        Goal: 30 days of recordings before any Type-2 strategy is considered production-worthy. We start
         banking data NOW even before any strategy work begins.

Phase B — WebSocket hardening (his 6-layer system, applied to our 3 feeds)
Why: Our bot has been dropping ticks and misreading status fields. His 6 layers describe how serious
operators handle exactly this. We do not need 100-300 replicas, but we do need the cheap layers.

What it actually is:

●        Layer 1 (warmup & quality gate). Subscribe 15s before each new 5-min market opens; in the final 5s
         require ≥3 ticks and no >5c jump; otherwise skip the window. Direct port from his text; no infrastructure
         cost.
●        Layer 3 (stale-tick guard). Reject any tick with >15c delta from the warmup midpoint; log 'STALE TICK
         REJECTED'. One conditional in the tick handler.
●        Layer 4 (first-tick skip). Drop the first tick after any reconnect — almost always a cached snapshot.
         Trivial.
●        Layer 5 (staggered startup). Spread reconnects evenly over 1s rather than reconnecting all
         subscriptions on the same millisecond. One sleep schedule.
●        Layer 2 (parallel replicas) and Layer 6 (jitter EMA + reaper). Defer. They assume 100-300 WS
         connections per market. Only worth it after we measure that our single-connection per market is the
         bottleneck.

Phase C — Entry-price discipline (audit + retune)
Why: He insists that win-rate must beat entry-price-in-cents. Our edge thresholds were tuned to theoretical
probability, not to achieved win-rate. We have never audited this.

What it actually is:

●        Backfill the last 30 days of bot trades from the wallet CSV (already locally for May 4, 11, 13).
●        Group by (asset, duration, entry-price-bucket) and compute realized win-rate.
●        Produce a grid: rows = entry-price 35c/40c/45c/50c/55c/60c, cols = duration 5/15min, cells = win-rate
         count and edge.
●        If any bucket sits below entry-price-in-cents, raise the edge threshold for that bucket. If a bucket is well
         above, we are leaving size on the table.
●        Add a permanent diagnostic line to the digest: 'today's fills: avg entry 47c, win-rate so far 51%, edge
         ratio +4c'.

Phase D — Adverse-selection filter
Why: He claims bids 10c below ask at 40c fill disproportionately on the losing side. Our bot bids ASK+5c
(above the ask, not below), so the literal pattern does not apply, but the mechanism (we get filled when
better-informed makers think the price is wrong) does.

What it actually is:

●        Define a logged metric: for each fill, was the side that filled us the one that ultimately won? Compute %
         of fills where the maker would have wanted out.
●        If >55% of fills are adverse-selected, we are being picked off. Add a filter: skip BUYs when ask > mid +
         N c (book is moving away too fast).
●        Skip BUYs when the ∆30s Binance signal has weakened in the last 2-3 ticks (common cause of
         adverse-selected entry).

6. Sanity rails — what NOT to copy
Not every claim in the article should be accepted at face value. The post is partly a marketing piece for his
Telegram channel; we keep skepticism on the bigger claims.

●         $100k PnL profile — cited without time-frame, drawdown, or fees. Treat as 'this is possible' not 'this is
          the median.'
●         '100-300 parallel websockets per market' — almost certainly only useful if you are competing for the
          same fills as he is. We are an order of magnitude smaller. Layers 1, 3, 4, 5 first; revisit 2/6 when we
          have evidence of a bottleneck.
●         'Market makers and ladder bots dominate' — true but they are also Type-2-only and require the tick
          data and a sophisticated execution engine. That is the basis for Plan 2, not this plan.
●         '1-7 days per idea' — sound principle, but for OUR scale 2-4 weeks per idea is more realistic given
          sample-size constraints.
●         'Never deploy unproven logic' — keep. Our cancel-race fix is itself an example of an
          empirically-verified change. Maintain that bar.


7. Success criteria for Plan 1 as a whole
●         Tick recorder produces clean, replay-able daily files for at least 30 consecutive days.
●         WebSocket layers 1/3/4/5 are live; STALE TICK REJECTED counter is visible in the digest.
●         An entry-price × win-rate grid is published in the repo; at least one edge threshold tuned from it.
●         Adverse-selection metric is logged on every fill; a filter is enforced once data justifies it.
●         Time-of-day exclusion config exists and is informed by ≥20 trades per bucket.
●         Stop-loss A/B has a written conclusion for 5-min markets.
●         Daily PnL trend over the period is flat or up; drawdown does not exceed the existing 5% risk limit.
