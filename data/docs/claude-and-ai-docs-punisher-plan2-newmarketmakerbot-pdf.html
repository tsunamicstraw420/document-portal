                         Build a separate Polymarket market-maker / ladder bot

1. Thesis — why a brand-new bot, not an upgrade
Our current bot is a directional Type-1 bot: predict the next move, take one side, exit at expiry. Punisher
explicitly states: 'directional strategies work but are the least profitable overall' and that 'the survivors are
usually market-maker or ladder bots, the most common and profitable big bots on the platform.' Those are
Type-2 strategies and they require fundamentally different architecture: order-book modeling, paired bid/ask
quoting, inventory management, and self-recorded tick data for honest backtests. Bolting them onto the existing
bot would compromise both. A separate codebase, separate process, and (eventually) a separate wallet is the
right shape.

Three reasons not to merge:

●      Different risk profile. The directional bot loses on adverse selection; the market-maker relies on a
       bounded-inventory model that survives adverse selection. Cross-contamination of risk parameters is the
       most common reason these get fused-then-killed.
●      Different latency budget. Directional latency-arb wins by being first. Market making wins by being
       patient. Tuning one in the codebase of the other will degrade both.
●      Different wallet semantics. Market makers carry inventory across windows (carry risk). The directional
       bot does not. Co-mingling on one wallet makes risk limits ambiguous.


2. What the article says about market-maker and ladder bots
He does not give a step-by-step market-maker recipe — he keeps the high-edge code to himself. But across
the 8 steps he leaks enough to reconstruct the shape:

●      Type 2 is the profitable family. Mid-window trading, GTC bids, stops, order-book modeling.
●      Self-recorded tick data is non-negotiable. Order-book depth, fill rates, slippage. Public strike/close
       prices are insufficient for Type-2 backtesting.
●      Win-rate vs entry-price math. Same rule applies to market making: a maker who quotes a bid at 38c
       on a market that resolves 50/50 needs >38% win-rate to break even. Lower bids = lower required WR
       but slower fills.
●      Adverse selection is the dominant risk. Quoting 10c below ask at 40c gets filled disproportionately
       on the losing side. Filter for it; do not just collect spread.
●      Stop-loss thinking on short windows. Sometimes no-stop beats stop. For a market maker, the
       analogue is 'when do you flatten a one-sided fill before resolution?'
●      Execution quality first. The 6-layer WebSocket system applies to any Type-2 strategy.
3. Specification — what this bot actually does

3.1 Strategy archetype
Single-window market maker on BTC and ETH 5-min Up/Down markets. For each active market, quote one bid
on YES and one bid on NO at a configurable distance from the mid. When one side fills, the bot is holding
inventory; it then either places an offsetting offer on the same token at a tighter spread (capturing the
round-trip), or it lets the market resolve if the position is small and time is short.

3.2 Ladder extension (phase 2)
Once the single-quote version is profitable, extend to a ladder: 3-5 resting bids on each side at progressively
wider spreads. As the price walks, deeper bids fill, and the bot rolls offers to capture each step.

3.3 What it does NOT do

●       No directional prediction. No Binance momentum signals. No Chainlink late-window settle.
●       No taking liquidity at ask+5c (that is the existing bot's job).
●       No carry across resolution boundaries. Every quote is for a specific 5-min market window only.
●       No participation in markets above 85c or below 15c (Punisher's stated rule, ported here).

5. Required precursors (must finish before live deploy)
●    30+ days of self-recorded tick data. Plan 1 Phase A delivers this. Until it exists, backtests are
     guesswork and the bot is not deployable. This is the longest pole.
●    WebSocket 6-layer system, cheap layers at minimum. Plan 1 Phase B delivers this. Market makers
     need cleaner tick streams than directional bots.
●    A funded separate Polymarket wallet. Small starter ($50-100). User-side action.
●    A replay-driven simulator. Strategy is not tunable without it. Built alongside Phase A's recorder.
6. Phased plan of attack

Phase A — Tick recorder + replay simulator (shared with Plan 1)
Why: Without recorded book ticks, every strategy decision is a guess. The simulator turns those ticks into a
replayable environment so we can A/B parameters without spending real USDC.

What it actually is:

●        Recorder: subscribes to Polymarket CLOB book WS for all active BTC/ETH 5/15-min markets. Persists
         every book update (bid, ask, sizes, mid, timestamp) to compressed daily files.
●        Simulator: takes a folder of recordings + a strategy function and walks the bot through the data tick by
         tick. Tracks would-be fills against historical bid/ask. Reports PnL, fill rate, inventory time-series,
         adverse-selection metric.
●        Both shared with Plan 1; no duplicate work.

Phase B — Quote engine + offline backtest
Why: Decide the math before risking USDC. Punisher's playbook says 99 of 100 ideas die at this stage.

What it actually is:

●        Implement quote_engine as a pure function: (book_state, inventory, risk_caps) -> (bid_price, bid_size,
         ask_price, ask_size, lifetime_seconds).
●        Start with a textbook Avellaneda-Stoikov-shaped model adapted for binary outcome markets:
         reservation price = mid - inventory_skew, half-spread tuned to volatility and time-to-resolution.
●        Run it against 30 days of recorded ticks. Tabulate PnL by (asset, duration, time-of-day, half-spread,
         max-inventory). Punisher's '2-hour multivariate sweep with 3-fold CV' approach is the right shape.
●        Hold-out validation: tune on 21 days, validate on 9.

8. Top risks specific to this plan
●        Adverse selection. Punisher's most-emphasized warning. Market makers who do not filter for it lose
         money slowly to better-informed flow. The simulator's first job is to surface this.
●        Fishy 1-cent resolutions. Punisher describes these as real and recurring on Polymarket. Market
         makers carry inventory into resolution; a wrong resolution is a 100% loss on that inventory. Mitigate with
         the same cross-check (Binance, Coinbase, on-chain) and a rule to flatten before resolution if the market
         is close to 50/50.
●        API rate limits. Quoting many markets means many cancels. Existing bot already lives near
         Polymarket's limits; running both will require throttling and likely a coordinated scheduler.
●        Time-of-day asymmetry. Punisher mentions US vs Asian hours; market-making PnL is even more
         sensitive than directional PnL. Allow per-hour enable/disable from day one.
●        The 'Punisher edge is gone by the time you copy it' risk. If 175k people have read the same
         playbook, the easy edges are already crowded. Assume the playbook is a starting point, not a recipe.


9. Decision gates (kill-switches between phases)
●        Gate A → B: 30+ days of clean ticks on disk; simulator replays without errors. If not, fix the data layer;
         do not write a strategy yet.
●        Gate B → C: Backtest PnL positive on the 9-day validation hold-out, adverse-selection < 55%. If not,
         the parameters are wrong; iterate.
●        Gate C → D: Dry-run fill rate matches simulator within 3%. If not, the simulator has a bug; fix it before
         any real money.
●        Gate D → E: 5-7 days of single-market live trading shows positive PnL net of fees and the live fill
         behavior matches the simulator. If not, debug; do not scale a losing strategy.
●        Hard kill: any week where drawdown exceeds 20% of starting wallet, regardless of phase, the bot stops
         and we go back to the simulator.
