                  Introduction

Hey there!

Thanks for downloading The Seedance 2.5 Playbook.

Seedance 2.5 is incredibly powerful - but great results
take more than writing a prompt and hitting generate.

The right techniques let you edit, extend, refine, and
direct your videos with far more control.

After extensive testing, we’ve collected 10 practical
tricks to help you get better results and push
Seedance 2.5 further.

Some are simple. Some are easy to miss. But each one
can change the way you work with the model.

           #1 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
              Omni-Reference




Seedance 2.5 supports up to 50 reference materials.

Each scene is made up of three main components:

1. Environment - the location or setting of the scene

2. Characters - the people or figures appearing in it

3. Props - the objects or items they interact with




                                                        4
Upload all elements to Seedance 2.5 as image
references and bind each character, prop, and
environment to its reference image using @tags.

If you can name your images then label them for
clearer and more organized prompting.




“@Steve is sitting on a @sofa in the living room,
watching TV.
[cut] @Ricky comes in holding a @tire and says: “Hi
Steve, look what I’ve got!”
[cut] Steve looks at Ricky, frustrated, and says: “What
the hell, Ricky?”
[cut] Steve says: “It’s literally just a tire.”
[cut] Ricky says: “Yeah, but imagine if I find the rest of
the car!”

          #2 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
         “In-Between Technique”




What if you already know how your story starts and
ends, but you’re not sure what should happen in
between?

Seedance 2.5 can help you build the missing part of
the sequence.

Just upload your first and last frames and use the
following prompt:

“Show me what happens in between. Use multiple
camera angles.”

         #3 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
       Editing Video with a Prompt




You can make changes to your video simply by
describing exactly what you want to change in the
prompt - whether it’s an object, the environment, the
lighting, or the time of day.

For example, if you want to change the season in your
video, upload the reference video and use the
following prompt:

“Change the season in the video to winter.”

          Original video            Changed video




           Summer                       Winter


You can also make multiple changes at once:

1) Object + Time of the day

Prompt:

“Change the bike crossing the road in the @video to
an ice-cream truck crossing the road in front of the
man. Change the day time from night to early
morning.”




          Original video          Changed video




        Evening + bike        Morning + ice-cream truck

Instead of trying to recreate the same sequence with a
new character, use your existing video as the base and
swap only what needs changing.

Upload the video to Seedance 2.5 together with the
character image as a reference, and use this prompt:

“Change the X in the @video to the X in the @image.”

            #7 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
                Change Audio




You can edit dialogue, language, voice, music, and sound
effects separately. Just specify what you want to change
and what should stay the same:

“Edit @video. Remove only the
original background music. Keep
the character dialogue, lip sync,
ambience, and action sound
effects; preserve the visuals,
camera treatment, and editing
rhythm from @Video.”


You can also adjust the accent in
the video:

“Edit @Video 1. Change the
policeman’s spoken language to
natural American English while
preserving the dialogue content
and speaking times. Keep all
other     character      voices,
background music, ambience,
and visuals from @Video 1.”                           21
           #8 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
         Speak in Different Languages




You don’t need to translate the dialogue yourself.
Just write it in English and specify which language
each character should speak.




 The result should be the same as if you had written
 the dialogue directly in the target language.

          #9 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
        Editing Video with a Sketch




You can edit your video using a sketch by drawing
directly on a specific frame instead of describing the
change only with words.

Your drawing is then used as a visual reference for
that exact moment in the video.

                         The instructions below are

                         choose Sketch.

          #10 Seedance 2.5 Trick -
               Storyboards




A storyboard helps Seedance understand the overall
story, shot order, and composition of each scene.

It doesn’t need to copy every detail exactly - think of
it as a visual guide for how the sequence should
unfold.

For best results, keep the storyboard simple and easy
to read, with no more than 15 panels and minimal
text.




                                                     27
Upload the storyboard to Seedance 2.5, then describe
what should happen in each shot in your prompt:

“Shot 1: Cowboy slowly raises the beer mug, takes a long
drink, then lowers it back onto the bar.

Shot 2: Cowboy suddenly pauses, shifts his eyes toward
the entrance, then sharply turns his head as if hearing
something outside.

Shot 3: Cowboy immediately pushes away from the bar
and strides quickly toward the exit, his coat swinging
behind him.

Shot 4: Cowboy mounts his horse, grabs the reins, kicks the
horse forward, and accelerates away.

Shot 5: Cowboy gallops at full speed while three masked
riders race after him. Dust explodes beneath the horses as
the distance between them gradually closes.

Shot 6: Cowboy leans forward
and urges his horse faster
while the pursuing riders
remain close behind. His coat
whips violently in the wind as
the chase continues.

Keep all movement fast,
continuous, and physically
connected between shots.
Preserve    natural    horse
galloping, dust movement,
coat and fabric motion, and
environmental ambience.”

   NOW PUT THESE TRICKS TO WORK!




You’ve got 10 more tricks in your Seedance 2.5 toolkit.

The biggest takeaway?

You don’t always have to regenerate and hope for the
best.

A character isn’t right? Swap it.

A scene needs fixing? Edit it.

Your story needs another shot? Extend it.

The more you understand Seedance 2.5, the less it
feels like generating - and the more it feels like
directing.

Now take these tricks, test them, combine them, and
make something worth watching.
