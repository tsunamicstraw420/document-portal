Part 1: What CPA actually is

CPA stands for cost per action.

Here's the logic from the brand's side, because once you see their side, the whole model stops feeling too good to be true. A company like Walmart or a big sweepstakes brand wants new customers and email signups. They could spend millions running their own ads or they could say to thousands of independent marketers: "bring us signups, and we'll pay you per signup." They only pay for results, so they love it. You only need traffic, so you love it. The visitor gets something free, so they're fine too.

The action can be as small as:

  • Submitting an email
  • Filling in a short form
  • Signing up for a free trial or rewards program
  • Entering a sweepstakes

Payouts range from around $8 for a simple email submit up to $120+ for deeper actions like deposits or trials, depending on the offer.

The platform this entire system runs through is Glitchy it's the network that connects you to the offers, gives you your tracking links, and pays you. You pick an offer, build content that points people to it, and you earn every time someone completes the action.

That's the full model. No inventory, no shipping, no refunds, no angry customers. Your only job is attention getting the right offer in front of the right people.

Part 4: Picking your first offer (this decides more than anything else)

Log into Glitchy and open the offer library. Now the beginner mistake is sorting by highest payout and grabbing the biggest number. Wrong move. A $120 offer that nobody completes earns less than an $8 offer that converts constantly.

As a beginner you're hunting for one specific combination: brand recognition + a fresh angle.

Brand recognition because trust does half your work. When the offer involves a name people already know Walmart, Amazon, Target, Costco, Sam's Club, McDonald's the viewer doesn't have to be convinced the brand is real. Sweepstakes and gift card offers around household names, often paying a flat $6-10 per email submit, convert at rates that shock people precisely because there's zero trust barrier.

A fresh angle because saturation kills offers. Here's how to check before committing: open YouTube and TikTok and search the brand name plus words like "rewards," "gift card," "program." Look at what already exists. If fifty videos are all running the same framing that lane is crowded and the audience is numb to it. Either find a different framing or a different brand.

The offer that makes you $10,000 a month is almost never the most popular one on the network. It's the one with proven demand and the least competition the fresh angle nobody's running yet.

Part 5: Content that converts (without lying to anyone that part matters more than you think)

No camera needed for any of this. The formats that work: faceless commentary videos (long-form or Shorts), AI avatar channels, niche-dedicated pages.

Now, the psychology. The content that converts on cold traffic all does the same thing: it creates curiosity before the offer ever appears. By the time the offer shows up, the viewer isn't reacting to a sales pitch they're following a story that happens to lead somewhere useful.

Hook framings that do this honestly:

"Most [brand] customers have no idea this program exists"

"I found this three months ago and I'm annoyed nobody told me sooner"

"There's a way to get rewarded for shopping you already do here's how it works"

"Things about [brand]'s rewards program nobody explains properly"

One thing I need to say directly, because you'll see other affiliates doing it: you'll come across hooks like "I worked at [brand] for 3 years and they told us never to mention this." If you didn't work there, do not run that. I'm not saying this to be preachy I'm saying it because false claims about real companies are exactly what gets videos taken down, accounts banned, and affiliate payouts frozen for fraud. Brands monitor this stuff. Networks cut affiliates over it. The curiosity hooks above convert nearly as well and can't be used against you. Build the version of this business that's still standing in a year.

Same rule for your content's promises: if the offer says "up to $750 in rewards," say "up to" don't turn it into "you're owed $750." The conversion difference is small. The account-safety difference is everything.

Here's what actually works, converts genuinely well, and can't be taken from you:

Post your real proof. Your first payout screenshot from Glitchy even if it's $32 is worth more than a hundred fake comments, because it's verifiable. Post it in the community tab, in a pinned reply, in your next video. Real small numbers beat fake big ones every single time.

Reply to every comment fast. An active, responsive comment section signals "real person" louder than anything else on the page. Most people check comments before finishing a video give them a live one.

An empty comment section is a trust killer, that part is true. The fix is being genuinely present in it, not manufacturing it.

Part 7: The testing framework (how winners are actually found)

Burn this into your brain: you will not find your winning angle on the first post. Probably not the fifth. The affiliates making $10k months tested 15-20 angles before finding the one. The difference between them and everyone else isn't talent it's that they treated flops as data instead of verdicts.

The framework:

  • Take one offer and write 3 genuinely different angles for it
  • Post each angle across your channels and give it 24-48 hours
  • Under 5,000 views everywhere - the angle is dead. Cut it without emotion. Move on.
  • One version crossing 5,000 - make more variations of that specific framing
  • Multiple videos of one angle crossing 10,000+ - that's your proven winner. Scale it hard more variations, more frequency, everything behind it.

Then read the data with total honesty. The angle you love means nothing. The angle the data loves is the business.

A worked example of what this looks like in practice: say you're testing a Walmart-adjacent rewards offer with three angles a generic awareness angle ("Walmart has a rewards program most people don't know about"), a dollar-amount curiosity angle ("most customers never claim what they're eligible for"), and a discovery-story angle ("I found this after months and wish someone told me sooner"). You post all three, wait 48 hours, and the numbers come back: 900 average views, 4,800, and 16,000. The decision makes itself. Kill the first, give the second one more shot with a stronger hook, and pour everything into the third. That's the entire skill run the test, believe the numbers, feed the winner.

Part 8: EPC and the landing page where the same clicks become 5x the money

Time for the concept that separates people who understand this business from people who just post links.

EPC means earnings per click the average money you make every time someone clicks your link. If 100 clicks produce $8, your EPC is $0.08. If 100 clicks produce $40, your EPC is $0.40. Same traffic, 5x the income. Every serious affiliate obsesses over EPC, because raising it means earning more without needing a single extra view.

And the single biggest EPC lever is the landing page.

Most beginners send traffic straight from the video to the raw offer link. That click hits a generic network page, the visitor feels the spam energy, and most bounce. A clean landing page in between changes everything: it explains what the offer is, who it's for, and exactly what to do so by the time they hit the offer page, they're warmed up and they finish the flow.

What a high EPC lander looks like:

  • One offer. One button. No menus, no clutter, no six links competing for the click.
  • A clear headline matching the video they just came from (message match matters if the video said rewards, the page says rewards)
  • Simple numbered steps: "1. Tap the button. 2. Enter your email. 3. Complete the steps on the next page."
  • Honest framing describe the offer as what it is. Some offer flows genuinely involve completing multiple deals to qualify; if so, say it plainly ("this program requires completing several partner deals to qualify here's what that involves"). People who know what they're walking into complete the flow at much higher rates than people who feel tricked halfway through and networks pay you reliably for informed conversions, while tricked ones get clawed back as fraud. Honest landers aren't just safer. They genuinely convert better over time, because the traffic completes instead of abandoning.

Part 11: Niches that work for this (and why each one works)

The niche decides who's watching, and who's watching decides what converts. The best CPA niches share one trait: the audience is already in a money-adjacent mindset. Here's the breakdown:

Frugal living / money-saving hacks. The perfect match. Your viewers are actively trying to save money, so rewards programs, gift card offers, and cashback signups aren't an interruption they're literally the content. Costco, Walmart, and Sam's Club offers feel native here.

Deals, couponing & freebies. Same energy, even more direct. An audience that follows a freebies page exists specifically to claim things. Sweepstakes and free-reward offers convert here at rates other niches can't touch.

Side hustles & making money online. Viewers here are hungry for any legitimate income angle, and low-friction CPA offers ("get paid to sign up") fit naturally. Bonus: this audience follows accounts loyally, which builds your long-term asset.

Personal finance for everyday people. Budgeting content, "stop wasting money" content, paycheck-stretching content. Slightly more trust-driven, slightly older, completes forms reliably. Gift card and cashback offers do well.

Student life & budgeting. Broke-by-definition audience, extremely receptive to free money angles, very high engagement. Freebie and discount offers are the fit.

Gaming rewards. Younger audience, but massive volume and completely comfortable with sign-up flows (they've done a hundred of them). Game-adjacent CPI offers (cost per install) work here.

Shopping hauls & retail content. People watching Walmart/Target haul content are the exact demographic for those brands' reward offers. The brand match is literal.

Sweepstakes & giveaway content. The niche where the offer is the content. Highest conversion intent that exists the viewer clicked specifically because they want to enter things.

Pick the one closest to content you can produce consistently, then match offers to it using the "would this viewer genuinely want this?" test. If the answer isn't an obvious yes, wrong pairing.

Month 3: roughly $3,500-$5,500. Proven angles rotating. You now know what converts for your audience instead of guessing. Decisions get faster and better.

Months 4-6: $5,000-$15,000+. Multiple winning angles running simultaneously. Possibly a VA handling uploads while you focus on finding the next angle. Batch production. This is where the "$40k months" people talk about become mathematically visible: more proven angles × more distribution × higher EPC, compounding.

Two honest notes on those numbers. First, they assume consistent posting this curve belongs to people producing daily, not weekly when motivated. Second, the top end assumes you eventually treat it like an operation: scaling what wins, cutting what doesn't, reinvesting time into new angles. The people who make it to month four are never the ones who had the best start. They're the ones who kept posting through the month where it looked like nothing was working.

And that's really the whole secret hiding under all the tactics: the first month's job isn't to make money. It's to buy data. Every flopped angle tells you something.
