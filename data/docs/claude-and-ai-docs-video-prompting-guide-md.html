VIDEO PROMPTING GUIDE — grounded motion, no floating / morphing

What this fixes: clips where the character floats, hovers, "flies in place," never actually crosses the frame, whose feet don't touch the floor, whose face morphs mid-motion, or that jitter / melt — and clips where the model ignores the action entirely and just wobbles the keyframe.

Our exact pipeline (every rule below is written for it): still KEYFRAME first (Nano Banana / Imagen, 16:9, graphic-novel painterly style) → that still becomes the first frame of an image-to-video clip → Kling 3.0 (also Kling 2.x; principles port to Runway Gen-3/4, Pika, Luma, Sora, Hailuo/MiniMax) → a 5–10s, 720p clip animating ONE hero action or one subtle held beat.

Three hard project constraints baked into every template:

  1. Never animate the mouth — lips closed, no speech (voiceover is separate).
  2. Face/identity stays on-model — no morphing, no warping.
  3. Keep the graphic-novel style.
The single biggest lever is the KEYFRAME POSE, not the clip prompt. A video model does not invent a new action — it continues the pose it is handed. Hand it a mid-stride or leaping pose frozen in the air and it will hover that pose ("flying in place"). Hand it a grounded, weight-loaded, about-to-move pose and it will complete the move. Fix the free keyframe first, then spend clip credits. (This is exactly what burned 700 credits on the SC-18 "Thais bolts" clip — a terse prompt over an airborne-reading pose.)

0. The two-prompt split — READ THIS FIRST (it's where people go wrong)

We write two different prompts with opposite rules about the words "no / not":

KEYFRAME prompt (Nano Banana / Imagen — the still)CLIP prompt (Kling image-to-video — the motion)
JobDraw a grounded first frame in graphic-novel styleDescribe the full motion arc beat-by-beat
NegationsPOSITIVE ONLY. Never write "no/not/avoid." Naming a thing to forbid it seeds it in Nano Banana ("NO border" → drew a border). Describe the grounded pose as a fact: "both feet flat on the floor, weight forward."The anti-float / anti-morph / anti-jitter words go in Kling's separate NEGATIVE-PROMPT FIELD — not smuggled into the positive description.
FaceWire the character's reference anchor; don't re-describe their face in words (it overrides the anchor).Keep motion restrained enough that the face survives; let camera hide the face during big moves.

So: grounded pose is engineered in the KEYFRAME (positively). "Floating / morphing / jitter" are banned in the CLIP's NEGATIVE FIELD. If an engine/API has no negative field, append a short negative clause to the end of the positive prompt instead.

Sources for the split: Nano Banana negation-backfire is our own logged failure (magnific-keyframe-style-and-anchors memory); Kling's negative-prompt field is documented at atlascloud.ai and veed.io.

1. PROMPT FORMULA — character-action clip (fill-in-the-blanks)

Kling's official order is Subject + Subject Movement + Scene + (Camera + Lighting + Atmosphere) (kling.ai text-to-video guide, via atlascloud.ai). We extend it with an explicit body-mechanics arc and a grounding clause, because that arc is what stops the float. Our house format keeps the camera block verbatim from camera_movement_prompts.md and adds the scene separately (clip-camera-action-prompts memory).

MAIN (positive) prompt — paste the camera block first, then fill the Scene:

Scene: <WHO> in <graphic-novel style reminder>, in <WHERE / set>.
Opening frame: <exact starting pose — grounded, weight-loaded, both feet in contact>.
Motion arc: <beat 1 — first body mechanic> then <beat 2> then <beat 3 — where they END UP
in frame>. Ground contact: <heel-to-toe / weight transfer / visible foot-to-floor impact
on every step>, the body physically TRANSLATES <direction> and gets SMALLER/LARGER in frame
as distance changes.
Secondary motion: <hair sways, coat/dress trails, chair rocks back, curtain stirs>.
End frame: <where the subject and camera finish>.
Lips remain closed and still for the whole clip; the only motion is the camera move above,
the body action described, and the secondary motion.

NEGATIVE-PROMPT FIELD (Kling): paste the anti-float / anti-morph / anti-jitter list from §5 (trimmed — see the "keep it short" note there).

Duration / intensity: single hero action → 5s, moderate motion intensity (~0.5). See §6.

2. PROMPT FORMULA — "held" / subtle emotional beat (fill-in-the-blanks)

For a near-still beat (a couple leaning together, a held reaction), a locked or barely-moving frame will freeze into a photo unless you seed ambient motion. Give the camera a slow push and name small living details (atlascloud.ai; clip-camera-action-prompts memory).

<CAMERA BLOCK VERBATIM — Shot 05 slow zoom in OR Shot 08 dolly in
(or Shot 01 static ONLY if you add ambient motion below)>

Scene: <WHO>, <graphic-novel style reminder>, in <WHERE>.
Held pose: <what they hold — leaning in, hand on hand, eyes meeting>; bodies stay planted
and stable.
Micro-motion: <a slow breath raising the shoulders, one slow blink, hair drifting, candle
flame flickering, curtain and dust motes stirring, fabric settling> — subtle, small-amplitude,
continuous.
End frame: <same composition, slightly closer>.
Lips remain closed and still for the whole clip; the only motion is the slow camera push and
the small ambient details above.

NEGATIVE FIELD: anti-morph + anti-jitter list from §5 (you can drop the locomotion terms like "moonwalk/sliding feet" here — nobody's walking).

Duration / intensity: 5s and low intensity (~0.3) — a held beat over 10s tends to drift the face or invent unwanted movement.

3. PRE-FLIGHT CHECKLIST — run before every clip

Keyframe (the still) first:

  • [ ] Pose is GROUNDED — both feet in contact with a visible floor, weight over the feet, not airborne / mid-leap / tip-toe. (Airborne pose → guaranteed hover.)
  • [ ] Pose is the START of the action, not its peak — she is rising, not already sprinting; he is stepping in, not mid-lunge.
  • [ ] Face is turned to hide warp on big moves — three-quarter or from-behind for anything with real body travel; face-forward only for near-still beats.
  • [ ] Character reference anchor wired; face not re-described in words.
  • [ ] Style preamble present; no negation words anywhere in the keyframe prompt.
  • [ ] A visible floor + distinct background so the model has depth/ground to read (atlascloud.ai; kling.ai i2v guide).

Clip prompt (the motion):

  • [ ] Camera block pasted VERBATIM from camera_movement_prompts.md (not paraphrased).
  • [ ] ONE action only — one motion per clip.
  • [ ] Full motion-arc spelled out beat-by-beat (opening pose → mechanics → end state).
  • [ ] Ground-contact clause present (heel-to-toe / weight transfer / visible foot impact).
  • [ ] Real translation stated — subject moves across frame and changes size, doesn't stay put.
  • [ ] Secondary/ambient motion named.
  • [ ] Lips-closed line present (positive phrasing).
  • [ ] Negative field filled from §5 (short list).
  • [ ] Duration/intensity chosen (§6): 5s hero action, 5s low-intensity held beat.
  • [ ] Prompt is genuinely hyper-descriptive, not terse (terse = freeze/hover = wasted credits — video-prompts-hyperdescriptive memory).

5. NEGATIVE-CUE LIST — for the CLIP's negative field (NOT the keyframe)

Keep it short — an over-long negative prompt makes motion stiff (atlascloud.ai). Pick the bucket(s) that match the risk; don't paste all three every time.

Anti-float / ground (for any locomotion): floating, hovering, flying in place, levitating, weightless, gliding, sliding feet, moonwalk, skating feet, feet not touching the ground, no foot contact, drifting

Anti-morph / identity (for any shot with a face): face morphing, face warping, distorted face, changing face, melting features, identity drift, deformed hands, extra fingers, extra limbs, warping body

Anti-jitter / stability (always fine to include a couple): jitter, flicker, stutter, shaking, wobbling, melting, boiling texture, blurry, background warping

Mouth (our hard rule — reinforce it here too): talking, mouth opening, lip movement, speaking

A solid default short set for a walking character: floating, gliding, sliding feet, moonwalk, face morphing, warping, extra limbs, jitter, talking (Kling's own baseline is similar: "blurry limbs, distorted joints, extra fingers, unnatural movement, morphing body parts" — atlascloud.ai.)

6. MOTION STRENGTH & DURATION — 5s vs 10s

Duration. Kling 3.0 supports 3–15s; longer clips give the model more frames to drift the face, loop, or invent movement (atlascloud.ai). Our rule of thumb:

BeatDurationWhy
Quick hero action (a bolt, a turn, a step-in, a door exit)5sEnough for one arc; cheaper; least drift/morph. video-prompts-hyperdescriptive memory: quick actions only need 5s.
Sustained locomotion that must cross the whole frame, or a held beat that needs to breatheup to 10sOnly when the action genuinely needs the time — accept higher morph risk and higher cost (~700 credits/10s when unlimited doesn't cover it — magnific-clip-economics memory).

Motion intensity/strength. Where the slider exists, start at moderate (~0.5) for a walk/bolt and low (~0.3) for a held beat, then adjust (atlascloud.ai). Too low → the keyframe barely animates and reads as a floating photo; too high → limbs and face melt. If the character floats, the fix is almost never "raise intensity" — it's a grounded keyframe + ground-contact wording; raising intensity on a bad pose just makes it hover faster.

One motion per clip. The most reliable single setting there is. Splitting "stands up AND walks AND opens door AND exits" across separate clips beats cramming it into one (atlascloud.ai).

7. WORKED EXAMPLES

7A. THE BOLT — corrected (centerpiece)

Shot: "a woman (graphic-novel style) bolts up from a dining chair and rushes out the drawing-room door, moving away from camera." This is the SC-18-type shot that hovered.

Terse version that FAILS (do not use):

A woman bolts up from her chair and rushes out the door. Dynamic motion.

Why it floats: no starting pose, no foot contact, no translation, no camera, no lips rule — the model just jitters the keyframe in place and burns credits.

KEYFRAME to generate first (Nano Banana — positive only, grounded):

Graphic-novel illustration in a semi-real painterly style, deep dramatic cel shading and crisp
inked linework, high-contrast cinematic lighting; one single fully-illustrated scene that fills
the entire 16:9 frame edge to edge:
A woman seen from behind at three-quarter back angle, caught in the first instant of rising from
a dining chair: she is pushing up off the seat, one hand flat on the dining table taking her
weight, both feet flat on the wooden floor directly beneath her hips, knees bent, torso leaning
forward toward an open drawing-room doorway several steps ahead of her. The chair is tipped
slightly back behind her knees. Warm lamplight from the left, a dim hallway visible through the
open door. Her face is turned away toward the door. Take her face and hair strictly from the
wired character reference.

(Grounded, weight over the feet, back-to-camera to hide any face-warp, START of the action — not airborne. No negation words.)

CLIP prompt (Kling 3.0 — camera block VERBATIM = Shot 17 follow shot from behind):

follow shot from behind. Movement: move behind the subject along their route at shoulder height.
Speed: match the subject's pace. Framing: keep the back, shoulder or head as the foreground guide
while the route ahead stays readable. End: continue following with the subject leading the frame.

Scene: a woman in graphic-novel painterly style, in a warm lamplit drawing room with an open
doorway ahead.
Opening frame: she is mid-rise from the dining chair, one hand pushing off the table, both feet
planted on the wooden floor, weight coming forward.
Motion arc: she straightens up and pushes off the table, plants her left foot then her right in
two firm accelerating strides, her body clearly TRANSLATING forward AWAY from camera and getting
smaller in frame as the distance opens, and she reaches the doorway and crosses through it into
the dim hallway. Ground contact: each step lands heel-to-toe with a clear weight transfer and a
visible foot-to-floor contact, the floorboards taking her full weight on every step.
Secondary motion: her hair and the hem of her dress trail behind the sudden movement, the chair
rocks back slightly as she leaves it, the doorway light shifts across her as she passes through.
End frame: she has passed through the doorway into the hall, small in frame, camera still trailing.
Lips remain closed and still for the whole clip; the only motion is the following camera, her
body rising and running through the door, and the trailing hair, dress and rocking chair.

Negative field: floating, hovering, gliding, sliding feet, moonwalk, feet not touching the ground, face morphing, warping, extra limbs, jitter, talking Duration/intensity: 5s, intensity ~0.5.

7B. Man strides into a hall (toward camera)

Keyframe: man one step inside a doorway, front foot landed flat on the floor, back foot pushing off, weight forward, coat mid-swing, face three-quarter — grounded, start of the stride. Camera: Shot 18 reverse tracking (verbatim). Scene: "he walks straight toward camera, left then right foot each landing heel-to-toe with visible weight transfer on the stone floor, body growing larger in frame as he closes distance, coat swinging with each stride; camera retreats at his pace." Lips-closed line. Neg: float/slide/morph/jitter set. 5s, ~0.5.

7C. Couple leaning together (held beat)

Keyframe: the two seated close, shoulders touching, hands clasped, both stable and planted, faces forward and evenly lit. Camera: Shot 05 slow zoom in (verbatim). Scene / micro-motion: "bodies stay planted; a slow shared breath lifts their shoulders, one slow blink, her hair drifts, the candle flame flickers and dust motes stir in the lamplight." Lips-closed line. Neg: morph/jitter set only (drop the walking terms). 5s, intensity ~0.3.

7D. Reaction turn (quick beat)

Keyframe: subject seated, torso already beginning to rotate toward a doorway, feet planted, weight settled — grounded. Camera: Shot 01 static with ambient motion, or Shot 05 slow zoom in (verbatim). Scene: "she turns her head and shoulders toward the door over about four seconds, a smooth controlled rotation, feet and hips staying planted; hair swings with the turn, curtain stirs behind her." Slowing a head turn to ~4s reads smoother (atlascloud.ai). Lips-closed line. Neg: morph/jitter set. 5s, ~0.35.

9. WHEN NOT TO USE GENERATIVE MOTION — use Ken Burns instead

Generative motion is the risky, expensive option. For many beats a still + an editor Ken Burns move (slow push-in / pan) in DaVinci Resolve is more reliable, frame-accurate, free, and can't morph a face (reelmind.ai; styleframe.ai).

Prefer Ken Burns when:

  • The beat is emotional / near-still — a reaction, a portrait, a couple holding — where the

only "motion" you want is a slow push or drift. Generative adds morph risk for zero gain.

  • The shot has no locomotion — nobody needs to cross the frame.
  • Face fidelity is paramount and the face is front-and-center (close-ups). Ken Burns keeps

the on-model face pixel-perfect; a clip risks warping it.

  • Clip credits are being charged and the beat doesn't earn a generated clip

(magnific-clip-economics memory: stage real clips only on the ~8 hero beats; the rest = free stills with motion added in the edit).

  • A generated attempt already floated/morphed twice — stop re-rolling; hold the good

keyframe as a still and Ken-Burns it.

Reserve generative motion for the hero beats that genuinely need real body locomotion or translation — a bolt, a stride-in, an exit through a door — where a static push can't tell the story. Everything else: still + Ken Burns. (This also matches our editing law that every still gets motion in Resolve — episode-timeline-reuse-rules / match-visuals-to-narration memories.)
