Before you start

When to reach for it

  • Still images: keyframes, thumbnails, posters, product shots, character sheets.
  • Video clips on Kling, Seedance or Happy Horse: camera moves, dialogue, chaining clips into one sequence.
  • Picking a model for a new kind of job.
  • A render looks plastic, posed or overly glossy. It names the cause and the words to change.
  • An image is almost right. Stop re-rolling and make a targeted edit.

How to call it

Ask in plain words, for example write a Kling prompt for… or why does this image look fake? Claude loads the skill on its own when the request matches.

Write the prompt

Prompt order

Image

Subject→Action→Setting→Lighting→Composition→Style

Video

Shot→Camera move→Subject→Action→Setting→Lighting→Style→Audio
  • Image-to-video: describe only the motion and the camera. The frame already supplies the subject.
  • Put the shot first. The model reads the first line first.
  • Repeat product text word-for-word in every image and every clip.
  • Explore cheap, deliver sharp: test at low resolution, re-run the keeper at 2K–4K.

Mood words spread across the whole image

Write "dewy" or "sensual" anywhere, even about the light, and the whole picture shifts: clothing slips and expressions turn pouty. Counter it in plain words, for example straps stay neatly in place; lips closed and relaxed.

Standard portrait negatives

smilingglamour makeupjewelryplastic smooth skindramatic rim lightcolored backgroundsoft focus

In your pipeline these go in Kling's negative box, never in a Nano Banana keyframe prompt.

Paste-in lines

Film-look line Photoreal only
soft cinematic lens, shallow depth of field with creamy focus roll-off, gentle contrast with soft highlight roll-off, fine uniform film grain, no oversharpening, no digital look
Skin realism Photoreal only
Natural skin texture: realistic pores, fine hairs, subtle veining, a faint highlight. Tactile and real, not retouched plastic.
End of every clip prompt with sound
No music. No subtitles.
Surgical edit: name the change, pin the rest
Change the headline text only, to 'FOCUS'. Keep typeface, size, colour and position.
Beat-by-beat timing
6-second product shot, one continuous take. 00:00 the watch lies flat on black stone, still. 00:02 it rises and rotates slowly to face camera. 00:04 it settles as a rim light sweeps across the dial. SFX: low hum, soft click. No music. No subtitles.

Words that work

Use two or three per prompt, never all of them. Stacked style words weaken each other.

Light

Composition and focus

generous negative spaceslight three-quarter angleleading linesbackground melts into soft bluronly the label in focus

Combos that tested well

  • shadowplay + cinematic + fine film grain
  • muted palette + faded blacks + negative space
  • practical light only + atmospheric haze

"Shadowplay" was the most reliable single word in testing. Lens numbers like "50mm" or "f/1.8" barely register, so always describe the visible effect too.

Text inside images

  • Put every word in quotes, exactly as it should read, and say where it goes.
  • Keep it to a few text elements in one or two sizes. Paragraphs turn into squiggles.
  • If a word keeps coming out mangled, spell it out: spelled F, O, C, U, S. Fix it with an edit, not a full re-roll.
  • Best at text: Seedream and GPT Image 2. Longest text: Recraft.
  • Zoom in on the label before you ship.

Editing an image

  • One plain sentence: what changes, and what stays exactly.
  • Label references and refer to them by name: Keep the product from @image1 and its shadow exactly; replace the background…
  • Nano Banana 2 is the everyday editor. Use Pro only when the edit needs extra care.
Direct the shot

Twelve camera moves

One move per clip, always. Add "slow" to almost everything, because slow moves hide what the model can't render cleanly.

Scene rules

  • Action is intent plus a named move (spinning back kick connects), not body mechanics.
  • Emotion is physical signs (jaw clenches, nostrils flare), not labels like "angry."
  • Only what can be seen or heard. Smells and thoughts render as nothing, so show the evidence: cedar shavings on the workbench.
  • Once someone leaves the frame, they're gone for that take.
  • Three tracked characters at most across cuts, or faces start swapping bodies. Keep extras generic.
  • Shoot around mirrors, never into them.
  • After every cut, restate who is where and which way they face. Keep the camera on one side of the action.
  • On-screen text: leave a clean area of the frame and add the type in the edit.

Dialogue

  • Quote the line, name the speaker, describe how it's said.
  • One speaker per beat keeps lip-sync clean.
  • End with "No subtitles."

Your show doesn't animate mouths, so this applies to other projects.

Characters

  • Character sheet before anyone appears twice: three panels on white (front head-and-shoulders, side profile, full body). Every later prompt carries it.
  • Changing the look? New wardrobe or haircut means a new sheet first. Keep only one current sheet per character.
  • Audition a talking character: 2–3 short self-tape takes, a voice recipe of three qualities at most (controlled, hurt, almost warm). Attach the winner to every later scene.

Continuity habits

  • Work from what actually rendered, not the plan. Grab the real last frame and write the next opening to match it.
  • Repeat the product description word-for-word in every clip.
  • Keep each clip to 30 seconds or less.
  • Start/end frames control the moment: where one shot opens and lands. References control identity: who and what appears. Use both together.
  • Rewrite the first frame: take frame one of a clip and edit it in Nano Banana Pro. Then re-render in Seedance with that frame as the start and the original clip attached as the motion reference.
  • Cover a location: start from one still and ask for a slow fly-through that stops for a beat at each named angle. Every stop becomes a usable frame with matching light.
