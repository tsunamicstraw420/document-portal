  What this guide covers
  YouTube changed its rules without telling creators. It stopped being a subscriber platform and became a
  topic platform: your video now competes with every other video on the same topic (including big creators),
  not just for your subscribers' attention. The new ranking signal is viewer satisfaction (likes, comments,
  shares, and especially people returning to watch more) — not click-through rate or subscriber count. This
  guide shows you how to diagnose why the algorithm can't place your videos, and the concrete fixes to
  make it start recommending them.

1 Diagnose — the 3 signs YouTube doesn't know who to recommend your videos to.
2 Fix — the 4 actions that tell the algorithm exactly who your video is for.

                  STEP 1 · DIAGNOSE
          1       Check for the 3 signs the algorithm is lost                                   ■ 4:33–5:34


     If your videos and packaging are good but views are stuck, it usually means YouTube doesn't know
     who to recommend them to. Look for these three signs on your channel:

     1.       Random content — you post different, unrelated topics (AI one day, weight loss the next). The
              algorithm can't build an audience for a channel with no consistent topic.
     2.       Traffic only from subscribers — in YouTube Studio, check your traffic sources. If views aren't
              coming from Suggested, Browse, or Search, the algorithm isn't recommending you.
     3.       No returning viewers — people watch once and never come back. That tells YouTube your
              content isn't satisfying viewers.

                 STEP 2 · FIX
         2       Do these 4 things so the algorithm can place you                                               ■ 5:34–7:31


    Consistency alone only gets you in the game; packaging and structure get you recommended. Apply all
    four:

    1.       Create content clusters. Make ~10 videos around one topic (e.g. for a YouTube-growth channel:
             subscribers, watch hours, views). Stop jumping between topics.
    2.       Study the homepage competition. Before posting, search your title on YouTube and look at the
             thumbnails already ranking. Ask: would mine earn the click next to these? If not, redo the
             thumbnail.
    3.       Write curiosity-based titles. Instead of “How to get more views,” try “I changed this one feature
             and my views doubled.” The title tells the algorithm who to show it to.
    4.       Interlink your videos. Use end screens, cards, and pinned comments to send viewers from one
             video to the next. These viewing sessions are what trigger recommendations.
    ■ The formula she repeats: a bad video with a great title gets clicks; a great video with a poor title gets
    ignored. Packaging is what gets you noticed.



 Quick checklist

    ■ Confirmed my channel sticks to one core topic (no random posting)
    ■ Checked traffic sources in Studio (aiming for Suggested / Browse / Search)
    ■ Planned a cluster of ~10 videos around one topic
    ■ Compared my thumbnail against the ones already ranking for the title
    ■ Rewrote the title to spark curiosity
    ■ Added end screens, cards, and a pinned comment linking my other videos



   Bottom line: YouTube isn't broken — the rules changed. Subscriber count no longer decides
   recommendations; viewer satisfaction does. A better strategy, not more uploads, is what gets you unstuck.
