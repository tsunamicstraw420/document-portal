The 3 New Algorithm Changes (and the
     Dashboard Feature to Exploit Them)
     Why subscriber count matters less — and how to reach YouTube's own audience



    Video: “The New Algorithm Might Be the Reason You Are Not Getting Views”
    Creator: Chiamaka Okoli (Coach Chaka) • Length: 8:13 • Link: youtube.com/watch?v=uaQn0tHxY8E




  What this guide covers
  The creator argues YouTube quietly changed three things that now favor small creators. The theme: your
  subscribers matter only briefly after you publish; after that, the video sinks or swims on its own value and
  gets pushed to YouTube's own audience (everyone who watches your topic). This guide explains the
  three changes and walks you to the dashboard feature that shows exactly what that audience wants.

                  UNDERSTAND
          1       The 3 changes that level the field                                             ■ 0:35–6:02


     These weren't announced, but they shape who gets views now:

     1.       Audience rule — your subscribers only influence a video for its first ~2 hours. After that, YouTube
              decides whether to push it to its own topic audience. Big channels no longer coast on subscriber
              count.
     2.       Subscriber-notification rule — YouTube largely stopped notifying all subscribers, so videos rise
              on value, not on a big subscriber base getting pinged.
     3.       Recent-performance rule — YouTube judges each individual video's performance, not your
              channel's history. A brand-new channel can blow up one strong video.

                  DO THIS
         2        Use the hidden dashboard feature                                                              ■ 6:38–8:10


    There's a feature that shows what YouTube's own audience in your niche is watching right now — use
    it to make videos that get picked up.

    1.       On your phone, open Chrome and go to youtube.com/dashboard (it must open the desktop
             version).
    2.       Click Analytics.
    3.       Click Audience, then scroll down to “What your audience watches.”
    4.       Pick a topic shown there, watch the top video, and note the value it delivers.
    5.       Make your own version that adds extra value (your experience, a better example), then optimize it
             well and upload.



 Quick checklist

    ■ Understood that subscriber count mostly matters only the first ~2 hours
    ■ Accepted each video rises or falls on its own value
    ■ Opened youtube.com/dashboard in desktop mode
    ■ Found “What your audience watches” under Analytics → Audience
    ■ Chose a hot topic and planned a higher-value version
    ■ Optimized the video before uploading



   Bottom line: the game is no longer about how many subscribers you have — it's about earning the attention of
   YouTube's own topic audience with each individual video.
