Desk Robot — 5-Second Intro Storyboard & Build Recipe

The intro that plays at the very start of each video (right after your cold-open hook, not before it — never make people wait to get to the content). Keep it under 5 seconds. Same every time; YouTube explicitly allows a repeated intro, and repetition is what builds recognition.

You already have every asset you need: the transparent character, the pose sheet, and the brand colors.

The storyboard (5 beats, ~4.5 sec total)

TimeOn screenMotionAudio
0.0–0.6sCream background, emptyTwo teal circuit-lines quickly draw in from the cornerssoft "whoosh"
0.6–1.6sCharacter pops into centerBounces up from bottom with a slight squash-and-stretch, antenna wobbleslight "boop" / pop
1.6–2.6sCharacter does its wave (or thumbs-up)Small idle bob; eyes do a quick blink—
2.6–3.8sDESK ROBOT logo slides in beside/under the characterText scales up from 90%→100% with a tiny overshoot1-note chime

Design rules: cream background the whole time (keeps it light and on-brand), the character never leaves center, motion is bouncy but fast — nothing eases slowly. The whole thing should feel like a friendly stamp, not a movie title.

Build recipe A — CapCut (free, ~30 min once)

CapCut has the keyframe tools to do this and it's free. One-time build, then reuse the export forever.

  1. New project, canvas 1920×1080, background fill #F5F0E6.
  2. Drop in deskrobot_master_transparent.png. Scale to ~55% height, center it.
  3. Pop-in: at 0.6s set a keyframe with the character at 0% scale, position slightly low; at 0.9s keyframe it to full scale, centered; at 1.0s a tiny overshoot (103%) then settle. That overshoot is what makes it feel alive.
  4. Text: add a text layer "DESK ROBOT" in Poppins Bold — "DESK" in #0E3A3F, "ROBOT" in #F7931E. Keyframe it from 90%→100% scale + fade in over 2.6–3.0s.
  5. Circuit lines / sparkle: optional — add a simple animated sticker or a small shape draw-on. Skip if short on time; the character pop alone is enough.
  6. Sound: grab a short "pop" + "chime" from CapCut's free sound library or YouTube Audio Library.
  7. Export 1080p. Trim to ≤5s. Done — this clip is now the head of every video.

Build recipe B — Canva (free, even simpler)

Canva's free plan does this with built-in animations:

  1. Create a 1920×1080 design, background #F5F0E6.
  2. Upload the transparent character, center it, apply the "Pop" or "Baste" entrance animation.
  3. Add "DESK ROBOT" text (Poppins), apply a "Rise" or "Pop" animation, delayed to ~2.5s.
  4. Set page duration to 5s, add a sound effect from Canva's audio (check it's licensed for monetized use — many Canva tracks are not; safest is to add the sound later in CapCut from the YouTube Audio Library).
  5. Download as MP4.

The one thing that matters most

Whichever tool: the character's bouncy pop-in (beat 2) is 80% of the effect. If you only nail one animation, nail that. A static logo fade reads as generic; a character that springs into frame reads as a brand with personality. Everything else is polish.
