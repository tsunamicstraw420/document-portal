1. What changed now that I know your real capacity

I built the first kit around 5 hrs/week. At 21 hrs/week you're at near-part-time capacity, which changes three things:

Old plan (5 hrs/wk)Revised (21 hrs/wk)
Cadence1 long-form/week2 long-form/week + 1–2 Shorts as funnels
Builder pillar1 in 6 videos1 in 4 — a named, recurring series
Runtime5–7 min6–9 min once templates are built

**The one thing that does not change: an SMB owner and a Claude Code user are different audiences, and YouTube reads your back-catalog to decide who to recommend you to. More hours doesn't dissolve that — mixing them too hard still muddies the signal. So the structure is SMB as the spine, builder as a clearly-labeled series inside the same channel** (its own playlist, its own thumbnail treatment), and we only spin the builder pillar into a second channel if it clearly outgrows the series slot. Two channels from day one would split your effort and your algorithmic identity before either has traction.

On "incompatible with scale" — what I actually meant: fewer people search "MCP tutorial" than "AI for my small business," so a pure builder channel tops out at a lower total view count. But each of those views pays 3–5x more (see the CPM data below), and with your capacity you can serve both. So the builder pillar isn't a reach problem anymore — it's a high-value wing of a broader channel. You were right to push.

2. Competitive landscape — the findings that matter

(Sourced. Subscriber counts conflict across trackers; treat as approximate.)

The niche is saturated at the top by on-camera personalities, and nearly empty in your exact lane. The big players — Matt Wolfe (~970K), Nate Herk (~830K), The AI Advantage, Skill Leap AI, Sabrina Ramonov (1.4M, AI for solopreneurs) — are all on-camera. The genuinely large faceless channels (AI Revolution ~557K, AI Explained ~350–580K) survive on the AI-news speed race, which is a bad moat and a low-CPM game.

Three specific gaps the research confirmed:

  1. AI for small business, faceless + animated, organized by function or trade — no incumbent. Sabrina Ramonov owns the on-camera version; nobody owns the animated-brand version. And B2B/business-buyer AI content commands $15–28 CPM vs. $4–12 for generic AI news — 2–4x. This validates the SMB spine on both competition and revenue.
  1. Claude-specific education — there is no dedicated large Claude channel. Claude content is scattered inside general channels. Wide open.
  1. MCP / agents explained for non-experts — the voices here (IndyDevDan, Cole Medin, AI Jason) are all on-camera and pitched at developers. Nobody owns "MCP and agents, explained simply, as a branded animated series." That's your builder pillar's exact opening.

On the animated-character bet — the research supports it, with a caveat. YouTube's 2026 "AI slop" crackdown judges whole channels, and it uses absence of a human face as a rough proxy for "AI-generated," which caught some innocent human creators. But what survives is content showing "a recognizable persona, point of view, or character" and "deliberate creative choices." Kurzgesagt (25M+) and Fern ($80K+/mo) are both explicitly cited as "the opposite of low-effort AI content." A recurring stylized character is the strongest available faceless hedge — it reads as authored and branded, not machine-templated. The caveat: because the face-absence proxy is blunt, you should actively signal original production — occasional behind-the-scenes, "made by a human, AI used as a tool only" in your About page, and real logged results in your test videos. Don't rely on the character alone to clear the filter.

What the recent fast-growers did (Nate Herk: 0→830K in under 2 years; Sabrina Ramonov: 0→1.4M in ~a year): narrow buildable niche, shipped usable artifacts (templates, real dashboards, not just talk), a recognizable brand, long-form authority with Shorts only as funnels. That's the playbook. Copy the structure, not the on-camera part.

Avoid, per the research: "top 10 AI tools," "5 prompts," ChatGPT-basics, generic news roundups, slideshow+TTS, and Shorts-first. These are the slop archetypes and the lowest-CPM tier.

3. Claude is moving fast — the current-state facts you need

(Sourced from Claude release notes and corroborating timelines, mid-2026. A few items flagged as less certain.)

This matters because half your content is "how to use X," and X changed a lot in the last 6 months. Highest-demand topics are the newest features, where competition hasn't caught up:

  • Pricing: Free $0 · Pro $20/mo · Max $100 or $200/mo · Team ~$25/seat. (Official pricing page was down during research; these are third-party-confirmed — verify before quoting exact numbers on camera.)

The single biggest under-served content vein: non-developer tutorials for the newest products — Cowork on web/mobile, Skills, connectors, the Memory redesign. Demand is rising, competition hasn't arrived. Weight your first two months here.

6. Real pain points to mine for titles (straight from users)

These are recurring, verified frustrations — each is a video where the search demand already exists:

  • "Which model do I use?" — the single most common beginner question, now worse with the Fable/Mythos naming.
  • Usage/rate limits — the most frequent complaint across all paid tiers; people don't understand the weekly caps.
  • "Is it free / which model am I actually getting?"
  • No image generation, refusals feel over-cautious, Claude.ai vs API confusion.
  • Builders: MCP servers failing to connect despite "correct" config, token-cost blowups on large codebases, debugging workflows, team/multi-dev setups. All named as explicit YouTube content gaps.
  • SMB owners: the blocker isn't capability, it's "where do I even start" and connecting AI to the tools they already use (QuickBooks, Gmail, Canva). Address that directly and you're solving the thing that actually stops them.

7. Repurposing the "Ethereal Explorer" channel — do it, here's how

Yes, repurpose it — since you never followed through, there's nothing to lose and it saves you setting up a fresh channel. Two checks first:

  1. Delete or unlist any existing videos on unrelated topics. A mismatched back-catalog is the one thing that actually confuses your recommendations.
  2. Name and handle can each change only twice per 14 days — get it right in one pass.

Paste-in values (if you go with Desk Robot):

  • Name: Desk Robot
  • Handle: @DeskRobot (check availability; fallbacks @DeskRobotAI, @AskDeskRobot, @DeskRobotHQ)
  • Avatar: replace the astronaut with your character once designed. Interim: a simple flat robot icon on your accent color.
  • Description: Practical AI for people who run a business — and for the ones who like to build. One job, one workflow, one short video. No hype, no jargon. New videos every week. Independent tutorials; not affiliated with or endorsed by Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google.

Keep the same Google account and channel — only the public identity changes, and there's no penalty for renaming.
