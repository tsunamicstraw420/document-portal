(MUST WATCH) The 5 Step AI Video Workflow

Module: AI Video Creation · Instructor: Anthony · Length: ~19 min Lesson URL: https://www.contentcreator.com/products/ai-creator-course/categories/2157469701/posts/2194080911

Summary

This is the foundational overview lesson. Its core argument: there is no single "one-click" AI tool that turns an idea into a finished, high-quality video. Tools that claim to do this produce generic "AI slop" that won't stand out or get views. Instead, treat content creation as a hybrid human + AI workflow where you stay in control. The five fundamental steps of making any video don't change with AI — only how you execute each step changes.

The 5 steps

  1. Idea (the "seed"). The most important step. A great video built on a weak idea still fails, no matter how good the visuals. If you already have a strong idea, you don't need AI here. The course's AI Content Planning & Writing section shows how to use AI to generate ideas or make an existing idea more viral/clickable.
  1. Script. Always write a script — even for ads (voiceover), short films (character dialogue), or silent videos (what's happening on screen). Anthony uses a back-and-forth method: write a rough outline himself, feed it to AI to expand into a full script, then edit it back in his own trained voice. This is usually the longest step. The course teaches how to train AI to write like you, research, and give feedback on drafts.
  1. Storyboard. The visual map of what is shown (the script is the map of what is said). Crucial for AI video because the best approach is image-to-video: generate and refine a still image first (cheaper/faster than video), then animate it. Tools like Nano Banana create consistent characters and environments across images. Organize storyboard frames chronologically (e.g., in Google Slides). Talking-head videos need less storyboarding; visual-heavy ads and narrative films need it most.
  1. Video creation. Falls into three categories:
  • Human–AI hybrid: real footage + AI effects or AI-generated B-roll (e.g., filming your real hand, then adding an AI fire effect; or a real dog-trainer interview with AI-generated B-roll of dogs).
  • Image-to-video: each clip generated individually from a storyboard image, then edited together. Clips typically cap at ~5–10 seconds, but that's rarely limiting — the average movie shot is only 2.5–4 seconds.
  • Long-form talking head: tools like HeyGen, Hedra, Lip Sync (on Fal AI), and ElevenLabs for longer talking content. HeyGen can study a video of you to build an accurate clone. A cost-saving trick: generate only short HeyGen segments (intro/middle/outro) and fill the rest with cheaper AI voiceover + B-roll.
  1. Editing. Where it all comes together — as simple or advanced as you like. Simple: arrange generated clips on a timeline, add AI-generated music and sound effects. Advanced: layer the AI effect over the original clip, mask and keyframe it (e.g., growing flame) so your real face still shows. The course teaches free editing software for beginners.

Key takeaways

  • Planning/pre-production (idea, script, storyboard) matters more than the generation step most beginners fixate on. "If you don't plan, you will almost always fail."
  • Don't chase a magic all-in-one tool; you'd lose control of the details that make content unique.
  • The course is deliberately organized tool-by-tool as an ever-growing database — watch every lesson rather than hunting for one shortcut video.
