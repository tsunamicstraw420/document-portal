Summary

A direct continuation of the image-to-video lesson. Anthony recommends most people start with image-to-video since it gives far more control (you refine the image first). With text-to-video there's no starting image, so everything rests on your written prompt. As always, the specific tool matters less than how you use it — he demonstrates with Kling on PromptEdit. A companion prompt worksheet is included in the description to copy, edit, and take notes on.

Why prompting is everything here

The three core components from the previous lesson — subject action, camera movement, and emotional mood/tone — matter even more now, because the AI can't infer them from an image that doesn't exist. You must supply all the context yourself.

Required prompt elements

  1. Subject(s) — who or what is in the scene (a person, animal, talking tree, robot, multiple subjects, a crowded city street, two cats fighting, "ants trying to take over the moon"). Be as creative as you like, but define it.
  2. Shot composition / framing — close-up, wide, medium (roughly waist-up), long shot, side angle, macro (fine detail close to the lens), drone/aerial, or something unique like an over-the-shoulder shot. You can use plain words if you don't know the cinematography terms, or ask ChatGPT how it would frame the scene.
  3. Environment / setting — where the subject is (jungle, train, mountaintop).
  4. Visual style — real human vs. CG cartoon vs. Studio Ghibli vs. Marvel-comics style. With an image the AI knows the style; with text you must state it.

Optional enhancement elements

  • Lighting style — describe in plain terms ("cozy feel") or technical ("book light"); note direction and intensity (backlight, side, overhead; bright midday vs. night streetlights).
  • Light color — white daylight vs. orange/neon.
  • Time of day — sunset, sunrise, midday, blue hour.
  • Atmosphere / environmental effects — fog, rain, snow, wind, smoke, dust, embers.
  • Color palette — vivid/saturated, black-and-white film-noir, or reference a known film's look (e.g., Blade Runner's iconic palette), which large models can often interpret.

Don't overwhelm yourself — prompts don't need to be a novel; one sentence per point is plenty.

Power tip

Copy the entire framework text from the description into ChatGPT and say: "Following this framework, write a text-to-video prompt for [brief scene description]." ChatGPT produces a full, detailed prompt; then tweak individual details rather than accepting it blindly. This gives you a proven structure, offloads the writing, and keeps you in control — saving significant time.
