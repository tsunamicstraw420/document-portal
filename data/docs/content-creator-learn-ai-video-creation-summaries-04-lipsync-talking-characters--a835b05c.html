How to Lipsync & Create Talking Characters with HeyGen

Module: AI Video Creation · Instructor: Anthony · Length: ~14.5 min Lesson URL: https://www.contentcreator.com/products/ai-creator-course/categories/2157469701/posts/2192666653

Summary

How to make realistic talking-head content — yourself as an AI clone or an entirely new character — using either your own voice or a typed script read by a preset voice.

Why HeyGen

Most cinematic AI video tools cap at 5–10 second clips (fine for most video, since the average movie shot is under 5 seconds). But for a character speaking to camera for much longer than 10 seconds, HeyGen is the industry leader. It was long subscription-only and pricey; now it's available pay-as-you-go on marketplaces like PromptEdit (no subscription).

Basic workflow on PromptEdit

Video → HeyGen (the avatar / image-to-video model). Upload an image (his were made in Nano Banana Pro — clone images of himself and of aliens). HeyGen animates that image to talk realistically. Two voice options:

  1. Type a script + choose a preset voice (preview each). Talking style: Stable (calm/neutral — his preference), Expressive (very dynamic, lots of hand movement), or Default (in between).
  2. Upload an audio file — record yourself or a voice actor; HeyGen animates the image to that audio. He used this for most of the intro because the human voice adds realism.

Then pick resolution (higher = more credits; 720p looks good, he used 1080p for the intro) and aspect ratio (16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for TikTok/Reels, 1:1 square). Hit Generate.

Using your own voice via a voice clone

To use your own voice without re-recording each time, make an ElevenLabs voice clone (PromptEdit → audio → ElevenLabs TTS → "Use your own voice"): name it, then upload an audio sample or record directly via your mic. Avoid the "remove background noise" option — better to start with a clean sample, since that processing reduces clone accuracy. Select your clone, generate, download the audio, and upload it into HeyGen.

  • Clone quality varies by person. To improve it: upload a longer voice sample (longer = more accurate), and adjust the Stability slider (default 0.5 — lower = more expressive, higher = more consistent). Experiment to find your best setting.

Key caveats & tips

  • Backgrounds: HeyGen animates the talking character well but is weak on backgrounds (e.g., fire smoke drifting backwards on longer clips; background animations can loop). Use simple backgrounds. For cinematic scenes use Kling/Veo instead — HeyGen isn't for that.
  • Best of both worlds (cinematic scene + controlled dialogue + consistent voice): generate scenes in Kling/Veo (they let you define what a character says, but the voice/accent varies clip to clip). Edit together, then run an AI voice changer to replace all voices with one consistent voice (even your own clone). Covered in dedicated course lessons.
  • Longer videos: don't generate a 5-minute clip in one pass — a single small error forces a full re-run. Break it into smaller segments, each its own generation.
  • Engagement: generate multiple talking-head clips, each in a slightly different (sometimes comical) scene — example shown of "Nick" as a real estate agent across varied scenes. Assemble on a timeline and export.
  • Save money: for a 10-minute talking-head video, use HeyGen only for key segments (first 30s, a minute mid-way, last 30s) and fill the gaps with cheaper ElevenLabs voiceover (or your own recorded voice) plus affordable AI B-roll/photos/graphics — which also keeps visuals changing for better retention.
