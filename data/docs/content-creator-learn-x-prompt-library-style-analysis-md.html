The big picture: it's always a two-stage pipeline

Almost every banger on his feed is one thread of 3 posts:

  1. The clip — the finished 15-second video (the hook post).
  2. The image prompt — a GPT Image 2 "character bible sheet" that locks the characters' look. Comes with the generated sheet as a still.
  3. The video prompt — a Seedance 2.0 / 2.5 prompt that animates those locked characters through a beat-by-beat action scene.

The insight for us: he never asks a video model to invent characters on the fly. He first builds a reference sheet as an image, then feeds that locked look into the video model. Consistent characters = the whole reason his clips look pro instead of morphing. That's the single most copyable move.

His genre is a specific look he repeats every time: "cinematic cel-shaded 3D anime," Unreal-Engine-grade, bold outlines — famous movies (Bad Boys, Rush Hour, Pulp Fiction) restaged as stylised anime action.

Every buddy-duo sheet follows this exact order:

  1. Identity lock — Create a premium cinematic character bible sheet for [A] & [B]. Use uploaded character sheets as strict visual reference for both characters. Do not change either's appearance.

→ This line is why his characters stay consistent. He uploads a reference and forbids drift.

  1. LAYOUT: — Split screen partner format. Two halves divided by a bold dramatic dividing element in the center.
  2. LEFT SIDE — [NAME]: — a colour-coded watercolour splash + big brushstroke name + tiny role label (DETECTIVE / MIAMI PD) + one massive cropped hero image from mid-thigh up, with expression, head-to-toe wardrobe, and pose.

STAGE 2 — The Seedance (video) prompt — where the motion comes from

This is the part worth studying hardest. His video prompts run 3,000–3,700 characters and are almost identical in structure every time.

His skeleton

  1. One-line premise — `Cinematic anime short film clip, 15 seconds. [SETTING — specific

environment + specific lighting]. He always states duration and nails the location detail (Miami street, bright Florida sunshine, palm trees, colorful Art Deco buildings`).

  1. CHARACTER 1 — [NAME]: and CHARACTER 2 — [NAME]: — build + expression + **full

wardrobe + a behaviour signature.** The behaviour line is his secret sauce: he gives each character a consistent motion personality, and they contrast:

  • Moves with total cool precision — never rushed, never rattled. (calm one)
  • Loud and reactive — shouting constantly, panicking occasionally, always getting the job done. (chaotic one)
  1. Antagonist block with a distinctness rule — `Thugs in casual street clothes only —

jeans hoodies t-shirts baseball caps. No suits.` (so the model never confuses heroes and enemies).

  1. AUDIO: — `High energy funky [genre] beat loud from frame one, every shot and impact

synced to the beat, [name]'s voice audible shouting throughout, never dropping.`

  1. The beat-by-beat timecode arc — the core. Eight 2-second beats:

0:00–0:01, 0:01–0:03 … 0:13–0:15. Each beat is one line of explicit choreography.

  • He always opens "Already mid-action" — never a static establishing shot. (This is the

single biggest anti-freeze trick; it matches the START+END-keyframe rule in our video guide.)

  • He ends every clip the same way: Hero moment. Done.
  • Camera directions live inside the beats: Wide shot showing full parlour, `close on

Lee's calm focused face, overhead looking straight down at the spinning clearing the room`.

  1. Closing style stack — the render signature again + `film grain, [genre] beat dominant

throughout, camera angles switching rapidly every action, [char 1 behaviour] throughout, [char 2 behaviour] throughout, [antagonist rule].`

Why his clips actually move (cross-ref to our VIDEO_PROMPTING_GUIDE)

  • "Already mid-action" opening — kills the frozen/hover-in-place start.
  • Every 2-second beat has a start pose and an end state — that's an implicit keyframe pair

per beat, which is exactly what forces locomotion instead of a held pose.

  • Constant forward locomotion refrain — keep walking forward, both advancing — travels

the characters through space instead of animating them on the spot.

  • Ground + environment contact — slides onto the polished floor, `vaults over a lounge

chair, never touching the floor, uses chairs as a continuous obstacle course` — physical contact with the set reads as real weight and motion.

  • Beat-synced audio — every impact synced to the beat gives the edit rhythm.

His negative-prompting habit

He doesn't use a separate negative field — he bakes negatives inline: not cartoon not Disney not Pixar, NO SUITS, never rushed, never dropping. → For Kling / Runway (which have a negative-prompt box): move these to that field — cartoon, Disney, Pixar, floating, sliding feet, static pose, morphing faces, still frame.

Second content lane — his "Simply replace [X]" template prompts

Separate from the movie-duo clips, he posts single-image viral templates people can reuse by swapping one word. Captured in the library under role prompt_other. Recurring types:

  • Isometric miniature scale-models — `Ultra-detailed photorealistic isometric miniature

scale-model diorama of [LANDMARK/MOVIE/ANIME]`.

  • 3D infographic travel posters — A hyper-realistic 3D travel guide infographic poster for [CITY].
  • Macro fabric / product hero shots — Extreme macro shot of woven cotton shirt fabric….

Format: a one-line Simply replace [TOKEN]: instruction, then the prompt in quotes, often Upload your reference image for enhanced accuracy. Good for high-reach single stills, not the animation pipeline.

How to use this to make a clip "on par with his"

  1. Pick two characters / a movie scene.
  2. Stage 1: fill the character-sheet template → GPT Image 2 (or Nano Banana / equivalent) →

get a clean split-screen bible sheet. Upload real reference images and keep the Do not change appearance lock.

  1. Stage 2: fill the Seedance template, reusing the exact wardrobe + behaviour words from

Stage 1 so the video matches the sheet. Keep the 8-beat timecode arc, the "Already mid-action" open, and the closing style stack.

  1. For Kling/Runway instead of Seedance: keep the beats and behaviour lines, but lift the

inline negatives (not cartoon…, NO SUITS, never rushed) into the negative-prompt box, and pair a START keyframe (mid-stride) with an END keyframe per our VIDEO_PROMPTING_GUIDE.
