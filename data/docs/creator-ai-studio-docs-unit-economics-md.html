ShowKit — Unit Economics & Pricing Model

Credit-based reseller of third-party image/video generation (fal.ai / Replicate / OpenRouter), marked up on a subscription. Compiled August 2026. Every wholesale price below traces to a dated search result listed in Sources, not memory. All figures are pre-tax, pre-salary, pre-marketing operating economics unless stated.

1. The credit unit and markup strategy

Recommended credit price: $0.02 per credit (list / pay-as-you-go rate).

The design rule is simple and auditable: each generation is priced at credits = round_up(wholesale ÷ $0.005) — one credit is backed by at most half a cent of wholesale COGS. Selling that credit at $0.02 is a 4× markup = 75% gross margin on the list rate, before Stripe. Cheap models round up slightly, so they land a touch higher (85% on a 1-credit Flux Schnell image).

Two prices for the same credit:

  • Top-up packs (pay-as-you-go): $0.02/credit — full list rate, 75% gross.
  • Subscription credits: ~$0.013–$0.016/credit effective — a "member" discount (you get more credits per dollar for committing), which is ~3× COGS, so ~62–67% gross if fully consumed. In practice breakage (subscribers rarely burn 100% of an allowance) lifts realized subscription margin back to ~72–76%.

Why 75% list / mid-60s subscription: after Stripe (2.9% + $0.30) the net contribution margin needs to survive refunds, churn, and infra and still fund CAC. Stripe is a bigger drag than it looks on small charges — on a $9 charge, 2.9% + $0.30 = 6.2% effective; on $49 it's 3.5%. That alone argues for nudging annual billing and larger top-up packs.

3. Subscription tiers, top-ups, and free trial

Three tiers, priced to anchor at the middle. Subscription credits carry an 18–35% bonus over the $0.02 list rate (bigger bonus at higher tiers), which is the reward for committing.

Creator$191,300$0.0146~160 mid images, or ~18 clips
Studio$493,800$0.0129~475 mid images, or ~54 clips

Rollover rule: unused credits roll over for one billing cycle only, capped at 1× the monthly allowance (a Creator can bank at most 1,300 rolled credits). This kills two problems at once — it removes "use-it-or-lose-it" anxiety (a top churn driver at incumbents) while preventing a user from hoarding six months of credits into a single COGS-spike month of Veo clips.

Top-up packs (pay-as-you-go, no bonus, never expire while subscribed):

  • 500 credits — $10
  • 1,500 credits — $28 (small pack discount)
  • 5,000 credits — $90

Top-ups are the pressure-release valve: when a power user drains the allowance, they buy more at the full $0.02 list rate (75% margin), which is better for us than the discounted subscription credit. Over-consumption converts into our highest-margin revenue.

Free trial: 100 credits, no card, expires in 14 days, restricted to image models + budget/standard video (Veo/Runway locked). Max possible trial COGS ≈ 100 × $0.005 = $0.50 — a tightly capped CAC. Enough to produce a full set of thumbnails and a couple of clips, i.e., a real "aha," without funding a free video farm.

4. Profit scenarios (100 / 1,000 / 10,000 paying users)

Assumptions (blended across all paying users, steady-state active payers):

  • Tier mix: 55% Starter / 35% Creator / 10% Studio → subscription ARPU ≈ $16.50.
  • Top-ups: ~12% of users buy a $10 pack/mo → +~$1.00/user. Blended ARPU ≈ $17.50.
  • Credits granted: ~1,137/user/mo; utilization ~75% (breakage) → ~850 credits consumed.
  • Usage mix: image-heavy — roughly 50 images + 7 clips per active user/mo; blended COGS ≈ $4.25/user/mo (effective ~$0.0047/credit consumed).
  • Stripe: 2.9% + $0.30 per charge; ~1.1 charges/user/mo → ≈ $0.85/user/mo.
  • Churn: ~8–10%/mo (AI apps churn fast; niche + rollover aims to beat the ~12% category norm). Scenarios are snapshots of active payers, so churn shows up as a growth/CAC problem, not in these margins.

5. Sensitivity — the two things that move margin

(a) Wholesale prices fall ~30% (they will). Aggregator prices have only fallen; the companion brief already flags this. If COGS drops 30%, per-user COGS falls ~$4.25 → ~$3.00, and if we hold credit prices, gross margin rises from ~76% to ~83% and net at 1,000 users climbs to ~$12,300/mo. The reseller benefits from deflation — as long as we don't cut prices faster than costs. The disciplined play is to keep credit prices flat and instead add credits to tiers (Starter 550 → 650) as a retention lever, banking part of the saving and passing part along. Danger only appears if a competitor forces a price war before we've built the workflow moat.

(b) Power users over-consume the allowance. This is the real threat, and it lives entirely on premium video. A Studio user ($49, 3,800 credits) who spends the whole allowance on Veo 3.1 clips gets 3,800 ÷ 400 = 9.5 clips = $19 COGS → margin still 61%. But push utilization to 100% across the base and blended gross margin drops from ~76% to ~65%; a cohort that consistently over-burns premium video can push an individual account underwater for a month.

How the design protects margin:

  1. Metered credits, no "unlimited." Every generation is priced to its own COGS — the opposite of Freepik/Magnific's 2,600-credit-video-on-an-unlimited-plan trap.
  2. Premium models cost proportionally more (Veo = 400 credits), so heavy premium use drains the allowance fast and pushes the user to top-ups at full 75% margin — over-consumption becomes our best revenue, not a loss.
  3. Rollover capped at 1× allowance prevents a banked-credit COGS spike.
  4. Trial excludes premium video and is capped at $0.50 COGS, so acquisition can't be gamed into free Veo.
  5. Per-model fair-use rate limits as a backstop for abuse.

Bottom line: the credit unit is a margin governor, not just a billing gimmick. Sticker margin is 75%; realized subscription margin lands mid-60s to mid-70s depending on breakage; the metering plus capped rollover plus full-price top-ups keep the tail of power users from inverting the economics — the failure mode that "unlimited" resellers walk into.
