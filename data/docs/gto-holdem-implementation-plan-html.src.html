GTO Holdem Build Plan

01

The plan on one page

The whole plan rests on six ideas.

The library is the product

Drills need instant answers, but a solve takes minutes. So the trainer serves answers that were solved ahead of time and stored. The solver is just the machine that produces them.

GTO Wizard works the same way. Its stock libraries are pre-computed with standard solvers to ≤0.3% of pot, and rivers are re-solved live. Checked

A license firewall

The best free solvers are AGPL, a "share-alike" license. We run them only offline, on machines you control. The website serves only their output files, which the AGPL doesn't cover (AGPL §2, FSF FAQ). Checked

The paid app itself uses only permissively licensed code (MIT, Apache, BSD) or our own.

postflop-solver, not TexasSolver

b-inary's postflop-solver was about 5× faster in its author's benchmark: 12–15 s versus 67–96 s, with a quarter to half the RAM. Checked

TexasSolver slightly misweights turn and river cards. That bug has been open since 2022, and a fix was only proposed on 6 Oct 2026. It stays on as a second-opinion cross-check. Checked

Short stacks first

Push/fold and ICM are exact, cheap and checkable against published tables. They also serve three of your four formats. Deep-stack preflop charts need the hardest piece in the plan, a full-game preflop solver, so they come later. Short stacks also become the first paid product.

This reverses what I told you earlier, that cash would come first. The research showed deep-stack cash depends on the hardest component.

Spins are mostly chip-EV

On GGPoker's Spin & Gold 3-max table, 97.6% of games are winner-take-all. Checked In those, ICM equals your share of the chips, so cash-style chip-EV solutions are exactly right.

ICM only matters on the ×10 wheel and above.

Rent compute, don't buy it

Every spot is an independent job. A rented 32-core machine costs about $0.60 an hour on AWS spot pricing. Checked A solid cash library comes to roughly $600. Estimate

Your Intel MacBook is for building and testing, not for weeks of nonstop solving.

  • 3-way postflop pots. No free solver handles them. GTO Wizard says a third player multiplies complexity by about 1,000× or more, and its own 3-way solving relies on proprietary neural networks. Checked This affects limped Spin pots and multiway live pots, so we stay heads-up postflop.

Technology choices

  • Rust for the number-crunching. The free poker engines worth borrowing from are already Rust: postflop-solver, robopoker, poker-cfr and rs-poker. Rust can also compile to WebAssembly, so our own solver could later run inside a customer's browser at no server cost. Rust isn't installed on your Mac yet. Checked
  • TypeScript and React for the web app. Node 26 is already installed. Checked Version 1 is a local web page that reads the library files and needs no server. Accounts, payments and hosting come in Phase 8.
  • Store flop strategies only. That's about 0.1–0.3 MB per solved flop Estimate. A full saved tree including turn and river runs to gigabytes Checked. Turn and river come later through on-demand re-solving in Phase 7.
  • The library's master copy lives in Cloudflare R2, which is cloud file storage. Rented solving machines upload straight to it, and later the paid app serves files from it.

Phase 4

Preflop solver for deep stacks

XLBiggest technical risk

You getYour own preflop charts for cash at 6-max, 7- and 9-handed (100bb); MTTs at 7-, 8- and 9-handed (MTT stack depths, big-blind ante); and Spin, 3-handed at 25bb and below. Every postflop library needs these as its starting ranges.

  • How paid tools did it. GTO Wizard's original cash preflop came from MonkerSolver on a very large server. PeakGTO's preflop comes from HRC or MonkerSolver. Checked
  • Our approach is the same family as Monker and Pluribus:
    • Monte Carlo CFR over the whole hand Verified
    • Hand bucketing on later streets; Monker's guide recommends 15–30 buckets per street Checked
    • A small bet menu
  • Table sizes (decided 6 Oct 2026): cash at 6-max, 7- and 9-handed; MTTs at 7-, 8- and 9-handed. Each table size is its own preflop solve. The postflop solver doesn't change, because almost every postflop pot is heads-up whatever the table size. Table size only changes the preflop ranges going into the pot.
  • Order:
    1. Heads-up 100bb first, because with two players we can measure exploitability properly.
    2. 6-max 100bb cash.
    3. MTTs at 8-max, the first MTT table size, at the MTT stack depths.
    4. Spin, 3-handed at 25bb.
    5. Next step, once 6-max is proven: 7- and 9-handed cash, then 7- and 9-handed MTTs. This needs its own budget approval.
  • Test to run here: can late seats be shared? The cutoff, button and blinds have the same number of players left to act at any table size, so their ranges should be close. Estimate If the 6-max and 9-handed solves confirm that within our accuracy target, those postflop spots get reused, and 7- and 9-handed only add early-seat spots. The stakes are real: there are 15 single-raised-pot seat matchups at 6-max, 21 at 7-handed and 36 at 9-handed.
  • Compute: rented big-memory servers for days. The Pluribus 6-max blueprint took 12,400 core-hours and under 512 GB of RAM. Verified At today's ~$0.019 per core-hour that's about $235 a run, and expect several runs while tuning. Estimate Big-memory machines cost more than the compute machines priced below, and that price hasn't been researched yet.
  • Your part: create an AWS account with a payment method before this phase. It's the first time we rent machines, and only you can create the account.
  • Caveat: with 3+ players there is no guarantee of "unexploitable". Verified Multi-way charts are judged by stability and sanity checks, not by one exploitability number.

Done when

  1. The heads-up solution's measured exploitability falls steadily as iterations increase.
  2. 6-max charts are stable across two independent runs. They also pass sanity rules: ranges widen from UTG to BTN, and the blinds defend sensibly.
  3. You spot-check them by eye against charts you trust. We compare only and import nothing.

We build these charts ourselves. Licensing outside preflop charts is ruled out (your decision, 6 Oct 2026). If quality stalls, we spend more compute, use finer hand buckets, and carry over the settings that tested well heads-up. Meanwhile, personal study keeps using hand-entered ranges.

Our own terms, modeled on GTO Wizard's

We borrow each protection and write it in our own words; we don't copy their text. A lawyer reviews it before launch. Sources are GTO Wizard's terms and Pio's license page. Checked

  1. No commercial use of our content (from their §7.5). Users can't sell, republish or build products from our charts, ranges, trees or solutions. That includes doing it through third-party apps.
  2. Covers every tier, free included (from §4.1). The terms protect the whole service, so there's no free-tier loophole.
  3. No scraping or automation (from §7.7). No scripts, bots, bulk downloading or automated requests.
  4. Study only, never during play (from §7.1). GGPoker and PokerStars require the same for study tools to be allowed. Checked
  5. Protections they don't spell out, but we should:
    • one person per account, with no sharing;
    • no reselling or redistributing exports;
    • no reverse-engineering our library files;
    • we can suspend accounts that break the rules.
  6. Decide our stance on coaching use. Pio's personal license lets users put results in coaching materials, books and videos, but not into on-demand services. Checked We need to choose whether students and coaches can show our charts in videos.

Why this matters more for us than for GTO Wizard:

  • GTO Wizard is in the EU, where database law protects its library even without a contract. Checked
  • The US has no equivalent database right, and machine-generated solver numbers get weak copyright protection. The copyright part is checked. The database-right point is general legal background for the lawyer to confirm.
  • So our terms of service are our main legal shield.

Technical add-on: fingerprints. Plant a few harmless, unique quirks in rarely viewed spots. If someone copies our data, we can prove it, the same way GTO Wizard's own quirks would expose anyone copying them. Every quirk stays inside the accuracy target, so drills never teach a worse play and the Phase 5 re-solve check still passes. This is an idea to check with the lawyer. Algorithm cheat sheet

CFR (counterfactual regret minimization)
The solver plays the spot against itself millions of times. Each time it leans toward the actions it "regrets" not taking. Its average strategy settles on the equilibrium.
CFR+
A faster version that never lets regrets go negative. It's typically 10× or more faster than plain CFR. Verified
DCFR (Discounted CFR)
Gives early, bad iterations less weight. It matched or beat CFR+ everywhere tested, usually reaching 2–3× lower exploitability. Verified This is the right algorithm for single postflop spots, and postflop-solver uses it with its own tweaks.
MCCFR (Monte Carlo CFR)
Samples parts of the game instead of walking every branch. It's needed for whole-game and preflop solves, and Pluribus used it. Verified
Abstraction and bucketing
NLHE has about 10165 game states Verified. Whole-game solvers therefore group similar hands into buckets and limit bet sizes. Single-spot postflop solvers don't need hand buckets.
Bet-size mapping
When a user picks a size that isn't in the tree, it maps to the nearest solved sizes with the pseudoharmonic mapping Pluribus used. Verified
Exploitability
How much a perfect opponent could win against the strategy. Whole games measure it in mbb per hand, where about 1 means essentially solved. Verified Single spots use % of pot. The practical target is 0.3–0.5%, and GTO Wizard's libraries are at 0.3% or better. Checked
ICM (Malmuth-Harville)
Your chance of finishing 1st equals your share of the chips. The same rule then applies to who's left for 2nd, and so on. Each finish is multiplied by its prize and the results are added up. It's slightly off from reality: one study found an average error of $0.37 per $100 prize pool. Checked
FGS (future game simulation)
Looks a hand or two ahead instead of paying out now. It only matters short-handed under about 10bb. Checked
Bubble factor
$ lost when you lose ÷ $ gained when you win. Required equity = BF ÷ (BF + 1). Checked
