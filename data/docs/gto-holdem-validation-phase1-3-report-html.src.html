PHASE 1 · DRILL

Graded by EV lost

The drill deals a random stack depth, seat and hand, weighted the way real cards are dealt. You shove, call or fold, and it grades you by how much EV the choice gives up compared with the solver:

  • Best, or Mixed: fine: when the solver plays your action at least sometimes.
  • Inaccuracy: under 0.1bb lost.
  • Mistake: under 0.5bb lost.
  • Blunder: 0.5bb or more.

In the screenshot sent with this page, calling a 7.5bb shove with 75s is graded a blunder. The call is worth −1.62bb against −1.00bb for folding, so it loses 0.62bb. Running totals (hands, % correct, bb lost) are kept on your device.

PHASE 3

Postflop study on your Mac

The Postflop screen works end to end:

  1. Set up a spot: ranges, board, pot, stack and bet sizes.
  2. Solve it on your Mac.
  3. Click through checks, bets and dealt cards.
  4. Open any hand to see each combo's strategy, EV and equity.

In the screenshot (T♦9♦6♥Q♣, a turn spot), A♦K♦ is the only AK that ever bets, 12% of the time. On this two-diamond board it holds a flush draw: 47% equity against 33% for the other AKs. That's the kind of detail a study tool should show.

What the one cross-check shows

  • Same value: both solvers put the out-of-position player's share of the pot at about 28.2–28.3 chips. The gap is 0.06 chips, inside the margin of error.
  • Different mix: TexasSolver checks 46% and makes the small bet 53%; ours checks 52.5% and makes it 45%. The average hand differs by about 8%. The biggest gaps are AKs (checks 76% vs 60%), 65s, 75s, 85s and 98s.
  • Likely reading, not yet proven: when two strategies are worth the same, both can be correct. The hands are indifferent between checking and betting small. TexasSolver's known turn/river bug (#95) could also play a part.
  • The test that settles it: force our solver to use TexasSolver's first-move mix and measure the EV lost. If the loss is tiny, both mixes are equally good.

What this means for the plan

  • Your Mac solves one study spot at a time comfortably, at about 11 minutes and 5 GB per flop. With your apps open, about 5 GB is the practical limit; bigger trees need half-memory mode or a quiet Mac.
  • The library still belongs on rented machines, as planned. A 32-core machine should take roughly 3 minutes per flop of this kind (estimate), which is what the cost table assumed.
