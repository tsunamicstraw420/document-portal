The music · built on the idea

Grandma's lullaby

The story has its own tune: a simple music-box melody, like one a grandmother would hum at bedtime.

  • Storybook part: only the music box, small and tinkly.
  • Pull-back: the same tune now sounds like it's coming from the TV speaker, because we're in the room. I do this in the edit for free, the same trick as the monitor sound in ad 05.
  • Barnaby gives the moon back: the same melody blooms into warm strings and piano. The music grows when the story is shared, which is what the narrator is saying at that moment.
  • Quiet beat: the music stops dead. You hear only the fire.
  • The hug and end card: the melody comes back full and warm, and ends on the last music-box note.

It's one Lyria 3.5 track, so the tune is the same in every part. That's what made Dani's hum work in ad 05. I won't use the Avoid box, which is what made Lyria 3.5 fail before.

The words on screen · per the Motion Text Playbook

A picture book that comes alive

The ad is about a story told out loud becoming a film. So all the text is a picture book. It starts as the book's own printed words, and it ends with the book closing.

  • In Barnaby's world: the narrator's words appear in the painted sky as storybook type. A big gold drop-cap "O" for "Once" draws itself in ink. Then the words rise in one by one in a storybook serif. The words "glow brighter" glow gold. Each new page comes with a soft page-turn sound.
  • The pull-back: the book text shrinks into the TV along with the picture. That's how viewers learn they're watching a screen.
  • In the living room: handwritten notes, like the names inside a family book, drawn on by a pen next to each face (never on it, measured with the face detector). "Grandma Rose · told it for 40 years". "Theo, 19 · made the film."
  • End card: the last frame shrinks onto an open book page. The facing page writes "Every family has a story." in ink, then "Film yours." lands in gold with a bell. The book closes (a real book-close sound from your library), and its cover shows the course lines.

One change from the playbook: it puts a hard impact hit under every slam. That fits a comedy, but here it would break the mood. Every entrance still moves and still has a sound, but the sounds are soft ones: page turns, a magic-bell shimmer and the book closing. Your library has all of them (Household and Bell folders).
