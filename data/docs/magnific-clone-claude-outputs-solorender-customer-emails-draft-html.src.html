Option D: low-balance warning on the site, and automatic refill

The warning on the site. A thin bar in the Studio when the balance is below the warning level: "380 credits left · about 5 clips · Top up". It costs nothing to run. It matches email 2, so the customer sees the same warning in both places.

Automatic refill. An off-by-default switch on Credits & billing: "Add a $25 pack whenever I drop below my warning level." When it fires, Stripe charges the card on file without the customer present.

  • New for Solorender: the site has never charged a card without the customer clicking. Today, every pack is bought by hand at checkout.
  • Money: each $25 Studio pack leaves you $12.97 after the AI cost and Stripe's fee (retention page, 10-29). No published data says how many people turn it on.
  • Care needed: the customer ticks a clear "yes, charge my card automatically" box. Every refill sends a receipt email. A monthly limit stops runaway charges. Some cards, mostly in Europe, ask the bank to confirm a charge, and an automatic charge can't answer that. Those refills fail, and the customer gets an "approve this top-up" email instead.

The law, in short

I read the US Federal Trade Commission's CAN-SPAM guide for businesses on October 30.

  • A sales email must explain how to opt out and must include "your valid physical postal address". A P.O. box counts if it's registered with the Postal Service.
  • Opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days.
  • The fine can be up to $53,088 per email.
  • Emails about an account the customer already has are exempt from most rules. The guide warns not to assume every email to a customer qualifies. It doesn't mention welcome or low-balance emails by name.
  • So I'd treat all three the safe way: each one gets an "Email settings" link where the customer can turn it off, and the win-back email also gets your address.
