How far our prices can come down

Our price is always our real cost × 2.5. So the only way to lower what customers pay is to buy each picture, clip and voice for less. I checked every model we sell against every place that sells it: the makers themselves, resellers, the big clouds, startup programs, prepaid deals, and running open models on rented servers. For each source I also read the terms, to see whether an app like ours is allowed to use it.

The answer

Most of our top models can get about 30% cheaper for customers: 20–35% depending on the model, and up to 70% on a few. With the larger deposit, the cut grows to about 37%.

The free steps: Veo 3.1 Fast gets 33% cheaper and Nano Banana 16%, with no money spent.

The rest needs $25,000–$50,000 of the raise, put down as a prepaid deposit with Runware. That deposit isn't spent and gone. It turns back into credit that we sell to customers as they use it.

With the $50,000 deposit, Seedance 2.0 would cost customers less here than at OpenArt or Magnific: $1.25 against their $1.40. With $25,000 it would match them at $1.42. Veo 3.1 Fast and Seedance 2.5 would land close to rivals.

Kling 2.5 and Nano Banana stay 2–4× rivals' prices. Magnific sells Kling 2.5 Turbo at $0.14, which is below the $0.35 Kling itself charges anyone, so no supplier deal can match it.

The plan

Steps ranked by what they cut, with the cash each needs

  1. DONE 20 Oct: Google's own models bought from Google directly$0 up front

    Live for every customer since 20 October, with your yes. Every model was tested side by side with fal, on the same picture or words. Google's cost is its own token count × its price list; fal's is its bill. Prices are the Google cost × 2.5.

    • Veo 3.1 Fast (picture start, prompt only, Entities): $0.80 against fal's $1.20 for 8 seconds. Customers pay 200 credits, down from 300.
    • Nano Banana 2 and its Edit: $0.067–0.068 against $0.08. 17 credits, down from 20.
    • Nano Banana 2.1: about $0.034 against $0.047. Google's bill shows it doesn't charge for the "thinking" step. 9 credits, down from 10 (21 Oct). Its Edit: about $0.039 against $0.047. 10 credits, down from 13.
    • Nano Banana Pro: $0.137 against $0.15. 35 credits, down from 38.
    • Gemini 3.8 Voice: about $0.031 per 1,000 characters against $0.045. Google's bill showed twice the rate we first used. 8 credits since 21 Oct, down from 12 on fal. Still to check: whether Google raises it again on 1 January 2027.
    • Lyria 2: $0.06 against $0.10. 15 credits, down from 25.

    How it changed from this plan: Google switches Veo off its simple developer service on 22 October, so everything runs through Google Cloud's business service instead. It has the same prices, no prepay and no monthly cap. solorender signs in through Vercel, so there's no key file. Lyria 3.5 isn't sold there, so it stays on fal.

    Bill check, 21 October: Google's billing page shows $3.42 of use, all from our tests. The free-trial credit covered it, so the bill is $0.00. Veo ($2.40), Nano Banana 2, Pro and Lyria 2 matched our own records. Two didn't match, and both prices were fixed the same day with your yes: the voice costs twice what we recorded (4 → 8 credits), and Nano Banana 2.1 costs about 13% less (10 → 9, Edit 11 → 10).

    Also done: an 18+ tick box at sign-up (Google's rule). The $2,000 Google for Startups application was sent on 20 October; Google answers in about 1–2 weeks.

  2. DONE 20 Oct: the cheap Runware swaps that need no deposit$0 up front

    All four moved to Runware on 20 October, with your yes. The numbers below are real bills from one test of each, using the same picture and words on both suppliers. Prices are the Runware bill × 2.5.

    • Ideogram Character: $0.06 on Runware against $0.15 on fal. The likeness was just as good. Price 38 → 15 credits. Proven on the live site.
    • Seedream 5 Flash Edit: $0.0193 against $0.027. Same quality, same speed. Price 7 → 5 credits. Proven on the live site.
    • Qwen Edit 2511: $0.0487 against $0.06. That's four times the $0.011 the price page suggested, because Runware bills it by computer time. fal also charges more than its page says, since it bills every started megapixel. Price 8 → 13 credits. It's slower: 77 seconds against fal's 20.
    • Qwen Image: $0.0166 against $0.02. Price stays 5 credits. It's much slower: 288 seconds against fal's 9 in the test.

    The two slow Qwen tools now run in the background, like videos. That way a slow picture never charges anyone twice. Test spend: $0.45.

  5. Runware Growth deposit$25,000 → $35,000 of credit

    This is the biggest single cut. Runware's page says, in its own words: "Deposit $25,000, start with $35,000"; "Best fit: Bootstrapped and pre-seed teams with a product in use"; "Credits do not expire"; "The deposit and the bonus credits are not refundable and not transferable."

    Since Runware sells most models at fal's price, the bonus makes nearly all of them 28.6% cheaper for us:

    • Seedance 2.0: 200 → 142 credits.
    • Kling 2.5 Turbo: 88 → 63.
    • Kling 3 Pro: 210 → 150.
    • Grok Lite: 40 → 29.
    • OmniHuman: 24 → 17 a second.

    The risk: all that money sits with one supplier. Runware also suggests running only 2–4 jobs at once per model unless we arrange guaranteed capacity with their sales team.

  6. Or Runware Scale instead of Growth$50,000 → $80,000 of credit

    37.5% off instead of 28.6%. Seedance 2.0 would be 125 credits, which is $1.25 against OpenArt and Magnific's $1.40. Pick this over Growth only when the raise has closed and our supplier bill is heading past about $5,000 a month (see the payback table).

  7. A trial of our own servers for voices and musicabout $750–3,000, only after a listening test you approve

    Open voice and music models can run on rented graphics cards for 1–2% of today's price:

    • Chatterbox voice: about $0.002 per 1,000 characters against ElevenLabs' $0.10.
    • ACE-Step music: under $0.001 per minute.
    • Kokoro voice: 95% under fal.

    Their licences (MIT and Apache) allow selling the output. These are different voices from ElevenLabs, so nothing is decided until you've heard them.

    Pay-per-use servers need no commitment. Renting one card all month (a 4090) costs about $248, or $745 for three months.

  8. ElevenLabs Scale plan, later$299 a month

    ElevenLabs' own terms (OEM Terms §2.B) only let Scale or higher plans offer their voices inside another product. It gives 20% off Multilingual v2, which pays for itself only above about 3 million characters a month. Until then, fal stays cheaper, because fal's price already includes the right to resell.

Deals I checked and don't recommend

  • fal Enterprise: about 3% off for $60,000 a year, from your September sales emails. It isn't on any public page.
  • Google's reserved capacity (Provisioned Throughput): $2,700 a month minimum, and no cheaper per Veo second than paying as we go.
  • Kling's 180-day packs: 10% off from $3,780. The Runware deposit beats them at 28.6%.
  • Kling Trial packs: 30% off, but limited to 5 buys of up to $98, and units expire in 30 days. That is fine for testing, not for a business.
  • MiniMax video packs: $1,000–6,000 for 1–20% off Hailuo, expiring every month.
  • BytePlus (ByteDance direct): its own terms say "The BytePlus Model Services are not available in the United States."
When the deposit pays back

The deposit only makes sense once customers are using it

Under the 2.5× rule, the saving goes to customers as lower prices, not to us as profit. The deposit comes back as customers use it: every $1 of Runware credit is sold to them for about $1.79.

So the question is how fast we would use up the credit. I assumed suppliers cost about 40% of our sales, and that 70% of that would run on Runware.

Ask Runware: licence on three picture models

Staying on Runware (your call, 18 Oct), because it is cheaper: 3, 4 and 1 credits, against 7, 7 and 2 on fal. The open question goes to Runware in writing.

FLUX.1 [dev], FLUX Krea and FLUX.2 Klein 9B are open models under Black Forest Labs' non-commercial licence. Its §4(a) bars use "for any commercial or production purposes".

We buy them from Runware first. Runware's terms (§2) say the model's own licence decides, and that this is our responsibility.

fal marks its copies as commercially licensed. Runware's own model pages say nothing about it either way (checked 18 Oct). So the question for Runware is whether its hosting is commercially licensed.

Fixed 18 Oct: ElevenLabs Music price

Done and live. It is now 150 credits per started minute (fal's $0.60 × 2.5): 150 for 30 s or 1 minute, 300 for 2 minutes, 450 for 3.

fal's page says, in its own words: "billed as 1 minute" for a 30-second track, because it rounds every track up to a whole minute.

We charge 3 credits a second.

  • A 30-second track earns 1.5× its cost.
  • The 1, 2 and 3-minute tracks earn 3×.

Neither matched your 2.5× rule.

How this was checked

Eight research passes ran in parallel, one per group of sources. Each checked its sources against our whole model list:

  1. Google, OpenAI, xAI and Meta.
  2. Kling, ByteDance, MiniMax and Alibaba.
  3. ElevenLabs, Black Forest Labs and ten smaller labs.
  4. fal, Runware and Replicate.
  5. Twenty other resellers.
  6. The big clouds and gateways.
  7. Running open models ourselves.
  8. Credit programs and prepaid deals.

It was all free reading. No paid run, sign-up, form or terms acceptance happened.
