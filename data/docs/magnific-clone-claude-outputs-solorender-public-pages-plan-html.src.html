Two choices for you

Everything else follows how the site already works. These two change what a stranger, including a competitor, can see.

Choice 1 · Recipes

How much of a recipe does a visitor see?

A. Just the showcase. The finished example (clip or pictures), the headline, what you bring, what you get, and the price. Nothing about how it's made.
B. Showcase plus "how it's made."Recommended Everything in A, plus the steps in plain words and the model names. For example: "a Seedream picture of your product → three Kling clips → an ElevenLabs voiceover → stitched into one film." Model names are words people search ("Kling product ad"). It also shows buyers what they're paying for. The prompts stay private.
C. Everything, prompts included. Not recommended. Anyone could paste the prompts into a cheaper tool, and the prompts are the work you've tested.
Choice 2 · Styles

Does a visitor see the style's own wording?

Each style is four example pictures plus one sentence, for example "Psychedelic cartoon illustration: exaggerated forms, bold black outlines…". That sentence is what makes the look, so anyone who reads it could use it in another tool.

A. Show the sentence. Fastest, about half a day less work. But each page is only one sentence long, and Google tends to ignore pages that thin.
B. Keep the sentence for members, and write each style a short public description.Recommended That's 2–3 sentences in our own words: what the look is, and what it's good for ("good for kids' book covers, cereal-box mascots"). It also shows a "Use @acid-pop in any prompt" button. Pages become long enough to rank, and the working text stays a member perk. I write all 197 in batches and check each against its pictures.

Help has no real choice. All 37 articles are how-tos with screenshots, none show customer data, and none are owner-only. I'd open all of them.

Do these pages show price per clip?
