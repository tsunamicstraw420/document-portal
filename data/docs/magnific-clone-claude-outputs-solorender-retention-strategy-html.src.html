Keeping customers longer, and getting them to spend more than the plan

A discussion page, not a build. It brings together Solorender's own numbers, how six rivals hold on to customers, and what published studies say. It ends with my recommendation and four choices only you can make. Nothing has been built or changed.

In one minute

  • Yearly plans are the strongest lever we have. A yearly Studio customer leaves about $179 on day one. That beats even the cautious cost to win a customer on Meta ($172). A monthly Solo customer leaves about $40 in a typical year.
  • Keeping customers longer cannot rescue Solo on its own. A monthly Solo customer would need to pay for 8 or more months in year one to cover a typical $92 ad cost. Published averages for AI apps are nowhere near that.
  • The course does not force top-ups by itself. On the course's own rules, a careful student finishes the film with about 1,260 credits, well inside one Studio month (4,450). Only a student who uses the character model and two takes everywhere goes over, by about one $25 pack. The real extra spending comes after the course, when students make a series.
  • Some customers are leaving through holes we can plug for free. In published data, a quarter to two-fifths of lost subscribers leave because a card payment failed. About half of those can be won back with tools Stripe already includes.
  • We have no customer data of our own yet. Every purchase on record is a house or test account. So step one of any plan is to start measuring from the first real customer.

How each bar is worked out. Monthly bars are the letter's middle case: Solo $40, Studio $74.1 A $25 pack on a monthly Studio plan gives 2,750 credits (packs come at the buyer's own plan rate).2 It costs $11.00 in AI if all used, plus a $1.03 card fee, so it leaves $12.97. Solo yearly: $204, minus $96 AI for 24,000 credits, minus a $7.64 card fee, leaves $100.36. Studio yearly: $408, minus $213.60 AI for 53,400 credits, minus $14.99, leaves $179.41. AI cost is $0.004 a credit (your 2.5× rule) and card fees are the letter's 3.6% + 30¢ on plans and 2.9% + 30¢ on one-off buys.

What this tells us. To cover the middle $92, a monthly Studio customer needs about $18 more in year one. That is between one and two $25 packs per customer, on average. A monthly Solo customer needs $52 more, almost four packs. A yearly customer of either plan covers the middle case on the day they pay, and a yearly Studio customer covers even the cautious case. Yearly money also stays if they never come back: in RevenueCat's data, AI apps keep about 21 in 100 yearly subscribers for a year, against about 6 in 100 on monthly plans.4

The catch with yearly. A yearly Studio customer gets all 53,400 credits up front, so they will almost never buy a pack in year one. Yearly plans and top-up packs pull against each other. That is fine: the yearly money is larger and arrives first.

Against one Studio month of 4,450 credits: the careful student has about 3,190 left over. The heavy student is short by about 810 credits, which one $25 pack covers. And because Solorender credits never expire, a student who takes two months has 8,900 credits to work with.

Where the real extra spending is. Lesson 24 plans a series: 6–8 shots an episode, starting with 3–4 episodes. At the same clip prices, that is roughly 450 to 1,120 credits an episode for one take, so 1,350 to 4,480 credits for a first run of episodes, on top of the course. That is the natural point where students run out. The course also teaches thrift on purpose ("Spend credits wisely", "Rescue a shot"), which is right for keeping students happy but works against pack sales during the course itself.

The pattern. Most rivals hold customers with loss: leave, and you lose the credits you paid for. You decided on October 16 that Solorender credits never pause or expire, and the letter sells that as an edge. I am not suggesting you change it. It does mean Solorender has to hold customers with what they lose by leaving and with reasons to stay. Today a customer who cancels loses the course, the right to buy discounted packs, extra runs at once, and storage above 5 GB. They keep every credit.

What rivals have that we don't. At least three run film contests with credit prizes (Higgsfield, Runway, OpenArt). Higgsfield sells automatic refills. Rivals give courses away free to win sign-ups. None I read puts a course behind a plan, so Solorender's course is unusual. None I read documents a pause button or a discount offer at cancel.

6The options

Six options, each with what changes for the customer and what it could mean for money. Where no study gives an effect, I say so and give the break-even point instead.

A. Plug the leaks

Part of my pick
What
Turn on Stripe's automatic payment retries, failed-payment emails and card updates (check they are on in your Stripe dashboard). In the cancel page, let customers switch to a smaller plan instead of leaving. Show a plain "come back anytime, your credits are waiting" message on the way out.
Customer sees
A payment that bounces gets retried and an email asks them to update their card. Someone cancelling sees "switch to Solo instead".
Money
If retries are not already on, the two studies imply about 12 to 21 of every 100 customers you would lose could be kept (25–40% of losses are failed cards, and about half of those are won back). Every month kept is about $11 on Solo and $20.46 on Studio.
Cost
No build needed for the Stripe part: these are settings in Stripe Billing, which your plan payments already use.10 I haven't checked whether Stripe charges extra for any of them. The site today ignores failed payments, and I can't see your Stripe settings, so I don't know yet which are on. The smaller-plan switch is a small change on our side.
Evidence
Two sources each.

B. Make yearly the first thing people see

Part of my pick
What
On /plans and /pricing, open on the yearly prices, with the dollar saving and the day-one credits spelled out. Course ads link to Studio yearly.
Customer sees
The yearly price first ($34 a month for Studio), with a switch to monthly.
Money
Each yearly Solo customer leaves $100 on day one; each yearly Studio customer $179. Unknown: how many will choose yearly. I found no published share for web apps, so we would measure it.
Cost
Small page change.
Evidence
Strong that yearly customers stay; no data on how many choose it.

C. Turn the course into a road that keeps going

Part of my pick
What
Show students a credit budget for the film, using the table in section 3. After lesson 25, start a "Series" track: a monthly episode challenge for course students, with a showcase and credit prizes. When a student's balance drops below what their next step needs, show the right pack.
Customer sees
A clear plan for what the film will cost, and a reason to come back every month once the course ends.
Money
Break-even: about 1.4 Studio packs ($25) per course customer in year one covers the middle $92 ad cost. Prizes cost you the credits ÷ 2.5, so a 10,000-credit prize costs $40. No published figure tells us how many will buy.
Cost
Medium: a few days to build.
Evidence
Rivals use contests; no study measures the effect on spending.

D. Low-balance warnings and automatic refills

Later
What
Warn when credits fall below a set level, before a run fails. Offer an optional "refill me with a $25 pack when I run low" switch, as Higgsfield does.
Money
Each $25 Studio pack leaves $12.97. No published data on how many turn it on.
Cost
Medium. Automatic charges need extra care with refunds and consent.

E. Welcome, nudge and win-back emails

Later
What
A welcome that walks new customers to their first clip in week one. A note when credits run low. A "your credits are still here" email 30 and 60 days after someone leaves.
Money
About 20 in 100 lost monthly subscribers come back within a year with no help.5 No study tells us how much an email adds.
Cost
Medium. Today the site sends only "your video is ready" emails.

F. A discount or pause at cancel

Not yet
What
Stripe's cancel page can offer a coupon (for example, a month at a lower price).10 It has no pause button; a pause would have to be built.
Money
One vendor source says about 25% of offered customers stay.11 Discounts cut the margin on the customers who would have stayed anyway.
Why not yet
Solorender customers already keep their credits after cancelling, so cancelling works much like a pause. Better to see real cancel reasons first. The cancel page already asks why.

7My recommendation

Do A, B and C together, in that order, plus the measuring page.

  1. This week, close to free: plug the leaks (A) and make yearly the first price people see (B). Both pay off from the first customer, and A has the best evidence of anything here.
  2. At the same time: a simple owner page showing renewals by plan, packs per customer, yearly vs monthly sign-ups, and course progress beside who renews. Without it, none of this can be judged.
  3. Alongside the course launch: the course budget and the Series track (C). It fits the plan in your letter, where the course is the main way you win customers for the next 60 days.
  4. Once there are 30 or more paying customers: look at the numbers and the cancel reasons, then decide on D, E and F.

Why this order: B can cover the ad cost on day one, A stops customers leaving for no reason, and C creates the extra spend your course example points to, at the moment students actually run out.

Four choices only you can make

  1. Which package first? My pick: A + B + measuring, then C. Or start with the course road (C) first.
  2. Yearly first on the price pages? Show yearly as the default, or keep monthly as the default.
  3. The course and yearly Solo. Keep the course on Studio and up only (my pick, it keeps the reason to choose Studio), or also include it with yearly Solo ($204 up front leaves $100, more than a monthly Studio customer's $74 in a typical year).
  4. Series challenge prizes. How many credits a month you are willing to give as prizes. For scale, 10,000 credits cost you $40 and 25,000 cost $100.
