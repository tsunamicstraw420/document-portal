The road from $0 to $1,000,000 a month

solorender is built and live, but it has not made money yet. This plan shows which customers $1M a month has to come from, how many of them it takes, and the four phases to get there. Each phase has a test it must pass before the next one starts.

The short answer

$1M a month is reachable, but probably not by selling $20–$80 plans to hobby creators. Every company in this market that got there sold to people who make ads: online brands, and the agencies that make their ads. To those buyers, solorender's higher price per clip doesn't matter much. They compare it with a human creator who charges $80–200 per video.

Realistic timing: late 2028 if every phase passes its test on time. Small teams that grew fast took 12–24 months to go from $1M a year to $1M a month, after they already had a product people paid for. Most solo AI-video apps never get past $10,000 a month.

The math

What $1M a month is made of

Path A: hobby creators only. At today's average plan of about $30, you'd need 33,000 paying customers.

The average AI app keeps only 6% of monthly subscribers for a full year. That works out to losing about 21% a month. So just to stand still, you'd need about 7,000 new payers every month.

Using normal conversion rates, that means roughly 900,000 visitors a month. That is OpenArt-level traffic, and OpenArt has a five-year head start.

Path B: mostly business customers. This is the mix that matches how Arcads got to $10M a year with 6,000 customers.

What we keep from each dollar

  • Every model costs customers 2.5× what we pay for it. When customers use all their credits, the model costs us 40¢ of each dollar.
  • Discounts cost us more per dollar. Super Max ($300 for 42,750 credits) costs us about $171, or 57¢ per dollar.
  • Stripe takes about 3¢ per dollar. Affiliates get 25¢ on sales they refer, for one year.
  • The result: we keep roughly 35–55¢ of each dollar, so $1M a month leaves roughly $400,000–500,000 before staff.
  • For comparison, Higgsfield says it keeps only 20–30% on models it buys from other companies, and over 80% on models it runs itself.
  • So our markup isn't the problem. The problem is that we charge more per clip than rivals. That is why the plan aims at buyers who don't shop by price per clip.

The plan

Four phases, each with a pass/fail test

Each phase ends with a test. Don't spend money on the next phase until the current one passes. This keeps to your shoestring rule: spending follows income.

PHASE 1 · NOW → 31 DEC 2026

Prove strangers will pay

$500 a month

Spend no money and build almost nothing. The job is to find 10 people you don't know who pay.

  • Send the outreach that's already written. That's 50 personal emails and 58 DMs, then the rest of the 303-person list, at 10–15 a day. Start with AI-ad and UGC creators and agency owners, not general AI hobbyists.
  • Recruit 20 affiliates. Give each 5,000 free credits. It costs us about $17–25 per person, and only if they actually use the credits.
  • Watch every new account by hand. Did they make something in the first day? Where did they get stuck? Email each one personally.
  • Close the free-credit loophole first. Stripe reports that people opening many accounts at AI startups rose 40% this year. Right now the 100 free signup credits can be farmed with throwaway emails.
  • Ask every payer one question: "What were you making, and what would you have used otherwise?" Their answers decide Phase 2.
Pass test
  • 10 paying strangers by 31 Dec.
  • At least 3 of them buy a second time.
  • At least 3 say they make ads, or content they get paid for.
If fewer than 10 pay, the problem is the offer or the first-day experience, not traffic. Fix that before spending anything.
PHASE 2 · JAN → MAR 2027

Pick the paying customer and package for them

$10,000 a month

Aim for about 100 customers. Turn what we already have into one product a brand or agency understands in five seconds.

  • "Ad Studio" package. One product photo and one brief go in. Out comes a batch of 10–20 ad videos with the same face and voice, in 9:16, 1:1 and 4:5. It is built from existing recipes, Entities and Flow.
    • Price it around $99–$299 a month.
    • The selling point is credits that never expire. Arcads' credits on its cheaper plans expire every month.
  • Make solorender's own ads with solorender. Arcads did this. Every ad we post shows the product working.
  • Post three social clips a week with the clip recorder that is already built. Use the "Which model?" and "One prompt, one shot" series that are already planned.
  • Creator Partner program. Copy only the format of Higgsfield's: a free plan in return for tagging solorender, with no posting quota. Add the 25% affiliate cut on top.
  • Launch-day speed. When a big new model comes out, add it the same day and post about it. Higgsfield grew this way repeatedly.
  • Apply for the Runware grant (up to $30k in credits, allowed for companies without investors). Your EIN now exists, so it's possible.
Pass test
  • $10,000 a month by 31 March.
  • At least 60% of revenue comes from business customers.
  • Fewer than 10% of business customers cancel each month.
  • Disputes stay under 0.5% of charges.
PHASE 3 · APR → SEP 2027

Make growth repeatable

$100,000 a month

Aim for about 1,000 customers. From here, money starts going into marketing, but only as fast as it pays back.

  • Agency plan. Seats for a team, one folder and brand kit per client, exports without our name on them, and monthly invoicing. Price it at $499–$1,500 a month. Agencies charge their clients $2,000–15,000 a month, so this is a small cost to them.
  • Cheaper suppliers. Switch to Runware, Google and the other suppliers in the switch plan you already have. Seedance alone drops from $1.52 to about $0.80 per clip. Under your rule, the savings go to customers as lower prices, which closes the price gap with rivals.
  • Paid ads, carefully. Run them only after Phase 2 shows what a customer is worth. Start at a few hundred dollars a month. Keep a channel only if its ad spend comes back as gross profit within 3 months.
  • Pages people search for. Pages like "AI product ad generator", "UGC ad maker" and "talking product video". Use OpenArt's playbook, but expect modest results.
  • First helper. A part-time contractor for support and checking quality. You can't personally check every customer's ads at 1,000 customers.
Pass test
  • $100,000 a month.
  • Each customer earns back what it cost to win them within 3 months.
  • Revenue from existing customers grows as they buy more.
  • Disputes stay under 0.5% of charges.
If you pass, a small raise becomes easy and optional. Stripe Capital, Hustle Fund and angel SAFEs all want to see exactly these numbers.
PHASE 4 · OCT 2027 → LATE 2028

Scale to $1M a month

$1,000,000 a month

Aim for about 7,800 customers. This is the mix from the table above.

  • Sales help for big accounts. One or two people who close $10k+ a year deals with agencies and brands. Arcads added this at about $6M a year.
  • Our own servers for open models. Higgsfield keeps over 80% on models it runs itself. Run the busiest open models on rented GPUs, and keep paying suppliers for the closed ones.
  • Agents as customers. List the MCP server in the Claude and ChatGPT directories and sell usage plans. One example: an agency's AI agent makes "10 new ads every Monday". No rival has shown real revenue from this yet, so treat it as a bonus, not the plan.
  • Yearly contracts for agencies, so revenue holds steady even if some customers leave.
  • Small team. Comparable companies ran at $10M a year with 8 people (Arcads) and 8–20 people (OpenArt). Plan for about 8–12 people, with AI agents doing the building.
Pass test
  • $1M a month.
  • At least half the revenue comes from business accounts.
  • Profitable, or close to it.
What could stop this

The biggest risks

We cost more per clip

We're 2–3× OpenArt, Magnific and Higgsfield per generation. Supplier switches shrink the gap. Selling finished ads to businesses makes it matter less.

Model makers go direct

Veo is free inside YouTube Shorts. Google sells 1,000 Flow credits for $19.99. Kling's own app makes about $500M a year. Hobby creators will drift to these. Agencies need a finished, organized workflow, which those apps don't offer.

Free ad makers

Amazon already gives sellers a free AI video tool. Meta is working toward making ads automatically from one product photo. Simple product ads will get cheap. Multi-scene ads with a consistent face and voice, organized per client, are the safer ground.

Customers leave fast

AI apps lose subscribers about 30% faster than other apps. Only 6% of monthly AI subscribers last a year. That's why the plan leans on business buyers, who earn money from what they make.

Fraud and chargebacks

Visa flags a seller at a 0.5% dispute rate. At $1M a month, that's only about 143 disputes. Credits that never expire and can be resold attract stolen-card buyers. Hold commissions, and add checks before the first big charge.

One founder

Today everything runs through you. Every phase above adds work you can't do alone. Hire for support and quality checking first, and sales second.

Your calls

Four decisions only you can make

  1. Who solorender is for. Keep selling to everyone, or lead with ad makers (brands and agencies) and keep creators as the second audience?
    recommend Lead with ad makers. It's the only route that worked for every comparable company. The decision isn't final until Phase 1's payers tell us what they're making.
  2. A business plan priced well above $80. Add a $99–$299 Ad Studio plan and a $499–$1,500 agency plan, both still 2.5× cost, with the same kind of plan discounts we have today?
    recommend Yes, in Phase 2. Today our top monthly plan is $80, so business buyers have nothing made for them.
  3. A marketing budget once money comes in. Today it's $0.
    recommend From Phase 3, put up to half of each month's gross profit into channels that pay back within 3 months. Spend nothing before that.
  4. Outside money. Stay self-funded, or raise once Phase 3's numbers exist?
    recommend Stay self-funded through Phase 2. Decide at $100,000 a month, when you can raise on better terms. Magnific and Arcads both got big without investors.
Keep score

Numbers to check every Monday

  • New signups, and how many made something on day one.
  • New paying customers. Split them into business and creator.
  • Monthly revenue, and how much of it came from customers who paid before.
  • Customers who cancelled or stopped buying.
  • Refunds and disputes as a % of charges. Alarm at 0.5%.
  • What we keep per dollar after supplier, Stripe and affiliate costs.

Most of these already exist on /admin. A one-page weekly scorecard there would be the only new build Phase 1 needs.
