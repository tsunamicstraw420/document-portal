What the redos taught us

Nine lessons that save credits

Test before you commit

Run one shot on two models first. One 128-credit test picked the model for every action clip that followed, and its winning clip went into the ad.

Voice before face

Approve the voice before making the talking shot. Changing Milo's voice meant paying for his talking shot twice.

Check the story, not the parts

The laser picture had everything right except the joke: no beam may touch the hamster.

Start on the ground

A clip made from a mid-air picture can't show the take-off. Make a start picture where the action begins.

Extend, don't redo

"Land and ride out the door" took 6 seconds of Extend on top of the ride we already liked, instead of gambling on a whole new take.

Ask for a hummable theme

Music was the most thrown-away stage: 105 of 120 credits. Ask for a catchy melody and judge it inside the cut, never alone.

Know what's a finishing job

A picture playing inside a screen is compositing work. Four generation tries cost 137 credits; the free editor did it properly.

Dip the music slowly

An instant 8 dB drop under the narrator sounded like a grabbed volume knob. 6 dB, eased in over 0.7 s and out over 1.2 s, sounded right, and cost nothing to fix.

Use only the good seconds

The boardroom clip is 5 seconds long. The ad uses 1.5 of them, before the extra in the back gets blocked.

How these numbers were counted

Every figure comes from solorender's credit ledger and Library for this one ad. Runs for the other course ads made the same days are left out. The cast batch was paid two pictures per run for six ads at once, so it is counted per delivered picture. Dollar prices use each plan's own credit price: Studio $40 for 4,450 credits, Max $80 for 9,450, Super Max $300 for 42,750. Times come from when each item landed in the Library and are approximate.
