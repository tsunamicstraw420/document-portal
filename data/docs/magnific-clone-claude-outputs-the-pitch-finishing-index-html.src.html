How we finished The Pitch

Every picture, voice, clip and note of music in this ad was made in solorender. The last mile was finished outside it: the cut, the speed ramp, the sound mix, the title playing inside the laptop and the animated end card. This guide shows exactly how, and how you can do the same with free tools.

Two ways to finish, same result

How The Pitch was actually made An AI assistant does the work

Claude, connected to solorender, made every asset, checked every result, cut the film with free command-line tools, and sent each version for review. The person directing only watched and gave notes.

You need: a solorender plan (connector keys come with every plan and Super Max), Claude with solorender connected from solorender.com/connect, and the free tools listed below on your computer.

Recommended if you like hands-on You edit it yourself

Make your assets in solorender, download them from your Library, and finish in DaVinci Resolve. It is free, works on Mac and Windows, and allows commercial use. Every move below has Resolve steps.

solorender's own Video editor already does five of the eight moves. Each section says what it can and can't do yet.

Setting up the free tools (Mac)
  1. Install Homebrew from brew.sh, then run brew install ffmpeg.
  2. Install Whisper's offline version: pip3 install faster-whisper. The first run downloads the small English model, then it works without internet.
  3. Install Pillow: pip3 install pillow.
  4. Connect Claude to solorender from solorender.com/connect. Then tell Claude which free tools are installed, and ask it to check every result before showing you.
Before you edit

Set up the edit the same way every time

Download everything whole

Download each clip, voice and the music from your Library at full length. Don't trim before you edit. Keep at least one second spare at each end of every shot so you can slide a cut later.

Match the clips

The Pitch is 1280×720 at 24 frames a second, because Grok Video 1.5 Lite makes 720p clips. Set your timeline to the same frame rate as your clips.

1Trim and order

Use only the best seconds of each shot

Most AI clips are 5 seconds long. The ad uses as little as 1.5 seconds of some. Cut each shot on its action: the boardroom laugh ends before an extra in the back gets hidden, and Vance's shot ends the instant the cigar leaves his fingers.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Drag your clips into the Media Pool, then onto V1 in story order.
  2. Put the playhead where a shot should end and split with Timeline › Split Clip (⌘\), or press B for the Blade tool.
  3. Delete the spare piece and close the gap.

In solorender's Video editor

Works

Drag clips onto the Main row; they snap end to end. Drag a clip's edge to trim, or use Split at the playhead.

How Claude did it

One ffmpeg command per shot: start time, length, then scale to 1280×720 at 24 fps. The shots are joined with ffmpeg's concat filter. Joining with the concat list method gave 173 audio errors.

2Speed ramp

Real speed for the stunt, fast for the getaway

The motorbike clip is 11 seconds, too long for a trailer beat. Playing it all at 1.4× made the jump feel rushed. Splitting it fixed that: the ride, ramp and jump play at normal speed, then everything after the take-off plays at 2.4×. Ten seconds of clip became 5.9 on screen.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Split the clip at the take-off frame (⌘\).
  2. Right-click the second piece › Change Clip Speed, type 240%. Tick Pitch Correction so the engine sound doesn't squeak.
  3. For a smooth ramp instead of a jump in speed, use Clip › Retime Controls (⌘R) and add speed points.

In solorender's Video editor

Works in steps

Split at the take-off, then set the second piece's Speed. The choices go 1.5×, 2× or 3×; there is no 2.4×. At 2× the getaway lasts 3.5 s, at 3× it lasts 2.3 s.

How Claude did it

ffmpeg cut the clip at 4.0 s and sped the second piece's picture and sound up 2.4× without changing the sound's pitch. That was rough cut v2, after the director asked for a ramp instead of a flat speed-up.

3Music dip under a voice

Duck the music, slowly

The first try dropped the music 8 dB in an instant when the trailer voice came in. It sounded like someone grabbed the volume knob. The fix is a smaller, slower dip. Fade 6 dB down over 0.7 seconds, starting just before the voice. Hold it there. Ease back up over 1.2 seconds after the voice ends.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. On the music clip, Option-click the volume line four times to add four keyframes.
  2. Place them at: 0.65 s before the voice (0 dB), just as the voice starts (−6 dB), 0.1 s after it ends (−6 dB), and 1.2 s later (0 dB).
  3. Or let Resolve do it: Fairlight's Ducker lowers the music track whenever the voice track plays.

In solorender's Video editor

Not smoothly yet

Each sound has one volume level. You can split the music and turn the middle piece down 6 dB, but the change is instant, which is exactly the version that sounded wrong.

How Claude did it

One ffmpeg volume curve on the music, worked out from the voice's start and length. The voice's start came from Whisper's word times. Claude measured the result and sent the smooth version for a listen before using it in the cut.

4Even loudness

Make every shot feel equally loud

Loudness is measured in LUFS; the closer to zero, the louder it feels. Phones and YouTube play videos at about −14. The raw clips for this ad ranged from −43.6 to −15.5, a 28 dB spread. Left alone, the lasers would be nearly silent and the boss would blast. Every piece was measured, then set to a target for its role.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Select the dialogue clips, right-click › Normalize Audio Levels.
  2. Pick a loudness mode (the BS.1770 / EBU R128 kind, not a Peak mode) and set the level. Choose Independent so each clip is levelled on its own.
  3. Repeat for effects at a lower level, then fine-tune by ear.

In solorender's Video editor

By ear

Each clip and sound has a Volume slider from −30 to +12 dB. There's no measuring, so play each cut and match by ear. Very quiet clips like the lasers (−43.6) can't be raised enough.

How Claude did it

ffmpeg's loudness meter read each file. Then ffmpeg's loudness levelling set each piece to its target in the same step as the trim. The finished ad measures −15.9.

5Hard cut to silence

Let the music stop dead

The joke lands when the trailer music stops mid-note and we are back in the quiet boardroom. No fade. In the soundtrack you can see the cliff at 25.4 seconds: everything before it is the trailer, everything after is a laugh dying, a cigar falling and one whisper.

In DaVinci Resolve

Split the music track at the cut frame and delete everything after it. Leave no fade. Keep the next shot's own sound.

In solorender's Video editor

Works

Split the music at the cut and delete the rest. Make sure Sound fade out is 0.

How Claude did it

The music was laid only under the trailer section, so it ends exactly where that section ends. The end-card music was built as its own piece from the last seconds of the same track.

6Picture inside a screen

Play the title on the laptop, then fill the frame

The director wanted the HAMMOND title playing on Milo's laptop while the camera pushes in, until it fills the whole frame. Four runs tried to get this straight from the generators (137 credits). A cut between two clips was visible, and a single long take froze the letters. The fix was to combine two clips: a push-in on a dark laptop screen, and the animated title on its own.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Clip A on V1, sped to 150% so the camera reaches the screen by 3 seconds. Clip B on V2, starting on the same frame.
  2. Select clip B. In the Inspector, use Transform (Zoom, Position) and Cropping to fit it exactly over the screen, then click the keyframe diamonds.
  3. Move 6–12 frames ahead, re-fit, keyframe again. Add more keyframes where the push speeds up.
  4. From 2.55 s to 3.0 s, go to Zoom 1.0 and Position 0 so the title fills the frame. Right-click the keyframes to ease them.
  5. Optional glass shine: a copy of clip A on V3, Composite Mode set to Screen, low opacity, fading out by 3.0 s.

Advanced: in the Fusion page, the Planar Tracker and Corner Positioner pin the title to the screen's corners for you. Both are in the free version.

In solorender's Video editor

Not yet

A picture on a Layer row stays where you put it for its whole length; it can't move or grow over time. Moving picture-in-picture is on the wish list.

How Claude did it

A small Python program with Pillow did it frame by frame (121 frames at 24 a second). It found the screen's edges in each frame by brightness and smoothed the box so it only grows. Once the edges left the picture, it kept the same growth curve. It pasted the title into the box with a faint glass shine, eased to full frame by 3.0 s, and ffmpeg encoded the result.

7Animated end card

Moving words over an AI backdrop

The backdrop is a generated still of two searchlights, animated with Grok. The first orange line was unreadable on the gold beams. A soft dark oval behind the words fixed it while keeping the beams at the edges. Four lines arrive one after another, each move lasting 0.4 seconds.

Font
Manrope ExtraBold: 72 px headline lines, 36 px orange course line, 40 px web address (on a 1280×720 frame)
Veil
a dark oval, about two-thirds opaque in the middle, heavily blurred, fading in over 0.4 s
Sound
narrator from 0.5 s, over the last 4.8 s of the same music track so it ends on the music's own ending

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Backdrop clip on V1.
  2. A dark oval on V2: a black Solid Color with a soft circular mask, or a ready-made dark-oval picture.
  3. Each line as its own Text+ title on V3 and up. Animate Zoom and Opacity with Inspector keyframes, or start from one of the animated Fusion Titles.

In solorender's Video editor

Mostly

Text comes in with Zoom, Pop and Rise, the same moves used here (the end card was built to copy them). A dark oval picture on a Layer row should give the veil, but we haven't tested it yet.

How Claude did it

A second Python program drew each frame: the backdrop, the veil, then each line with its own zoom, pop or rise and a soft shadow. ffmpeg mixed the narrator over the music's ending and joined picture and sound.

8Export wide and tall

One MP4 for YouTube, one for phones

The wide version is 1280×720, 24 frames a second, H.264 with AAC sound. Don't make the tall version by cropping the wide one. Every character was made in both shapes at the start, so the 9:16 cut uses real tall pictures and shots. That version is being made now.

In DaVinci Resolve

  1. Deliver page › Custom Export › Format MP4, Codec H.264. Resolution and frame rate follow the timeline. There are also YouTube and TikTok presets.
  2. For 9:16, make a second timeline. Right-click it › Timelines › Timeline Settings, untick Use Project Settings, and set 1080×1920 (or tick Use vertical resolution).

In solorender's Video editor

Works

Pick the shape when you start a project: 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 or 4:5. Export is free and lands in your Library. Make one project per shape.

How Claude did it

ffmpeg wrote the final MP4. The finished file measures 35.46 s, 1280×720, with sound, at −15.9 LUFS.

For the technical

The exact settings Claude used

You never have to type these. They are here so you, or your own AI assistant, can repeat the same finish.

ffmpeg settings, move by move 
# every shot: trim, fit to 1280×720 at 24 fps, level the sound
-ss START -t LENGTH -vf "scale=1280:720:force_original_aspect_ratio=increase,crop=1280:720,fps=24,setsar=1,format=yuv420p"
-af "aresample=48000,aformat=channel_layouts=stereo,loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5,volume=GAINdB"

# speed ramp: normal to 4.0 s, then 2.4× (picture and sound)
[0:v]trim=1:4,setpts=PTS-STARTPTS[v1];[0:v]trim=4:11,setpts=(PTS-STARTPTS)/2.4[v2]
[0:a]atrim=4:11,asetpts=PTS-STARTPTS,atempo=2.4[a2]

# music dip: −6 dB, 0.7 s down starting 0.65 s before the voice, 1.2 s back up
# VS = voice start, VL = voice length (seconds, inside the trailer section)
volume='1-0.5*clip((t-(VS-0.65))/0.7,0,1)+0.5*clip((t-(VS+VL+0.1))/1.2,0,1)':eval=frame

# mix music, voice and clip sound without automatic level changes
amix=inputs=3:duration=first:normalize=0

# join shots with the concat FILTER (the concat list method broke the sound)
[0:v][0:a][1:v][1:a]…concat=n=N:v=1:a=1[v][a]

# trailer voice one semitone deeper, same speed
asetrate=48000*0.94,aresample=48000,atempo=1.0638

# measure loudness
ffmpeg -i file.mp4 -af ebur128 -f null -
How Claude checks a clip before showing it
  1. Download it and read its length, size and whether it has sound (ffprobe).
  2. Lay out a frame every quarter second on one sheet, then look at it: does the action match the prompt, does anyone vanish or change face?
  3. Measure its loudness.
  4. Run Whisper with word times on anything that speaks. Are the words right, and are there no extra ones?
  5. For talking shots, line up mouth close-ups against the word times.
  6. Only then send it, with what was checked.
