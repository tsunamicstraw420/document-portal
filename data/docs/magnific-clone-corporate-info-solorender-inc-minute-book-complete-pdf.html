                                Bylaws of Solorender, Inc.
Solorender, Inc., a California corporation

Section numbers below refer to the California Corporations Code (the "Code").

Article I — Offices
1.1 Principal office. The principal executive office of the Corporation is at 1792 Harmony Way,
Pittsburg, CA 94565, or any other place the Board of Directors (the "Board") designates. The
Board may establish other offices.

Article II — Shareholders
2.1 Annual meeting. An annual meeting of shareholders is held each year on a date and at a time
the Board sets, to elect directors and conduct any other proper business. Meetings may be held in
person or, to the extent the Code permits, by electronic transmission, video or telephone
conference.

2.2 Special meetings. Special meetings may be called by the Board, the chair of the Board, the
President, or holders of shares entitled to cast at least 10% of the votes at the meeting (Section
600(d)).

2.3 Notice. Written notice of each meeting is given at least 10 and not more than 60 days before it
(Section 601). A shareholder may waive notice in writing, and attendance waives notice except
when the shareholder attends to object.

2.4 Quorum and voting. A majority of the shares entitled to vote, present in person or by proxy, is
a quorum. Each outstanding share has one vote. Directors are elected by a plurality of votes cast,
with cumulative voting to the extent Section 708 requires. Shareholders may vote by written
proxy.

2.5 Action without a meeting. Any action shareholders may take at a meeting may be taken
without a meeting by written consent of the holders of outstanding shares having at least the
minimum number of votes needed to take that action at a meeting where all shares were present
(Section 603(a)). Directors may be elected by written consent only if the consent is unanimous,
except to fill a vacancy the Board has not filled.

2.6 Record date. The Board may fix a record date not more than 60 nor less than 10 days before a
meeting, and not more than 60 days before any other action (Section 701).
Article III — Directors
3.1 Powers. The business and affairs of the Corporation are managed, and all corporate powers
exercised, by or under the direction of the Board.

3.2 Number. The authorized number of directors is one (1), as Section 212(a) permits while the
Corporation has only one shareholder. Before the Corporation has two or more shareholders, this
Section must be amended to provide the minimum number of directors Section 212(a) requires, by
approval of the outstanding shares.

3.3 Election and term. Directors are elected at each annual meeting and hold office until the next
annual meeting and until a successor is elected and qualified.

3.4 Vacancies, resignation and removal. A vacancy may be filled by a majority of the remaining
directors, by a sole remaining director, or by the shareholders. A director may resign by written
notice to the Corporation. Directors may be removed as provided in Section 303.

3.5 Meetings. Regular meetings may be held at times and places the Board sets. Special meetings
may be called by the chair of the Board, the President, the Secretary or any director, on four days'
notice by mail or 48 hours' notice delivered personally, by telephone or by electronic transmission
(Section 307(a)(2)). Directors may participate by conference telephone or video if all participants
can hear one another.

3.6 Quorum. A majority of the authorized number of directors is a quorum. While there is one
director, that director alone is a quorum.

3.7 Action without a meeting. Any action the Board may take may be taken without a meeting if
all directors consent in writing or by electronic transmission (Section 307(b)). Consents are filed
with the Board's minutes.

3.8 Compensation. Directors may receive compensation and expense reimbursement as the Board
decides.

Article IV — Officers
4.1 Officers. The officers are a President, a Secretary and a Chief Financial Officer. The Board
may also appoint a Chief Executive Officer, a chair of the Board, vice presidents and other
officers. Any number of offices may be held by the same person (Section 312(a)).

4.2 Appointment and removal. Officers are chosen by the Board and serve at its pleasure. The
Board may remove any officer with or without cause. An officer may resign by written notice to
the Corporation.

4.3 President and Chief Executive Officer. The President (and the Chief Executive Officer, if
one is appointed) has general supervision, direction and control of the Corporation's business and
officers, and may sign contracts, share certificates and other instruments on its behalf.

4.4 Secretary. The Secretary keeps the minute book, the share register and the record of
shareholders, and gives the notices the Bylaws and the Code require.

4.5 Chief Financial Officer. The Chief Financial Officer keeps adequate and correct books of
account, deposits the Corporation's money in the depositories the Board designates, disburses
funds as the Board directs, and reports on the Corporation's financial condition when the Board
asks.

Article V — Shares
5.1 Issuance. The Board may issue shares for money paid, labor done, services actually rendered,
debts or securities canceled, or tangible or intangible property actually received, as Section 409
permits. The Board's good-faith determination of the fair value of that consideration is conclusive.

5.2 Certificated or uncertificated shares. Shares may be certificated or uncertificated, as the
Board decides by resolution. For uncertificated shares, the Corporation sends the holder the
written statement the Code requires. Share certificates are signed as Section 416 provides; one
person holding two qualifying offices may sign in both capacities.

5.3 Transfers. Shares are transferred on the share register on proper instruction from the holder,
subject to any legend, agreement or securities-law restriction that applies to them.

5.4 Lost certificates. The Board may authorize a replacement for a lost, stolen or destroyed
certificate on terms it sets.

Article VI — Indemnification
6.1 Indemnification. The Corporation indemnifies its directors and officers against expenses,
judgments, fines, settlements and other amounts arising from their service, to the fullest extent
Section 317 permits.

6.2 Advancement of expenses. The Corporation advances expenses of defending a proceeding
before its final disposition, as Section 317(e) permits, upon receipt of an undertaking to repay
them if it is ultimately determined that the person is not entitled to indemnification.

6.3 Insurance. The Corporation may buy insurance on behalf of its directors, officers and agents.

Article VII — Records and Reports
7.1 Records. The Corporation keeps its minute book, share register and books of account at its
principal executive office, in written or electronic form.
7.2 Annual report waived. So long as the Corporation has fewer than 100 holders of record of its
shares, the annual report to shareholders described in Section 1501 is expressly waived.

7.3 Inspection. Shareholders and directors may inspect records as the Code provides.

Article VIII — General Provisions
8.1 Fiscal year. The fiscal year is the calendar year, ending December 31.

8.2 Contracts and instruments. The Board may authorize any officer or agent to sign contracts or
other instruments for the Corporation.

8.3 Electronic transmission. Notices, consents and other communications under these Bylaws
may be given by electronic transmission to the extent the Code permits.

Article IX — Amendments
These Bylaws may be adopted, amended or repealed by approval of the outstanding shares or by
the Board. After shares are issued, a bylaw fixing or changing the number of directors may be
adopted only by approval of the outstanding shares (Section 212(a)).

                          Technology Assignment Agreement
This Technology Assignment Agreement (the "Agreement") is made as of October 4, 2026 (the
"Effective Date") between Edmond E. Barna ("Assignor"), of 1792 Harmony Way, Pittsburg, CA
94565, and Solorender, Inc., a California corporation (the "Company").

Background. Assignor created and operates solorender, an AI image, video and audio studio at
solorender.com, earlier developed under the name "onda." The Company was formed to own and
run it. In exchange for shares of the Company, Assignor transfers solorender to the Company.

1. Assignment. Assignor irrevocably assigns to the Company all of Assignor's right, title and
interest worldwide in the Assigned Technology. This includes all copyrights, trade secrets,
trademark rights and goodwill, domain names and other intellectual property rights in it, and all
rights to sue for past, present and future infringement.

2. Assigned Technology. "Assigned Technology" means everything listed in Exhibit A and all
other software, content, data, brands and materials Assignor created or acquired for solorender (or
onda) before the Effective Date.

3. Accounts. For each account listed in Exhibit A, Assignor will, within 60 days after the Effective
Date, transfer the account to the Company or change its legal owner and billing to the Company,
as the provider allows. Until then Assignor holds each account for the Company's benefit. Prepaid
balances in those accounts pass to the Company.

4. Customers. From the Effective Date, the Company assumes Assignor's obligations to
solorender's existing customers, including honoring unused purchased credits and active
subscriptions.

5. Consideration. Assignor makes this assignment in exchange for the 8,000,000 shares of the
Company's common stock issued under the Founder Stock Purchase Agreement of the same date.
No other payment is owed.

6. Assignor's statements. Assignor states that:

 1. he created the Assigned Technology himself, including with AI-assisted coding tools, or
    otherwise has the right to assign it;
 2. to his knowledge, no other person or entity owns or claims any interest in it; and
 3. he has granted no license, lien or other right in it that remains in effect, other than the terms of
    service solorender offers its customers.

Third-party software, open-source components, AI models and online services that solorender uses
remain subject to their own licenses and terms. They are not assigned, except for Assignor's rights
under those licenses to the extent they can be transferred.
7. Further assurances. Assignor will sign any further documents and take any further actions the
Company reasonably requests to record or complete this assignment.

8. Moral rights. To the extent the law allows, Assignor waives any moral rights in the Assigned
Technology.

9. General. California law governs this Agreement. It is the entire agreement on its subject and
may be changed only in writing signed by both parties. It may be signed electronically and in
counterparts.
