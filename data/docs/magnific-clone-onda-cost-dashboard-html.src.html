What each AI model costs you wholesale (fal.ai) vs. what users pay in onda, and the margin on every image and video clip.

Bottom line: keep fal as the default — go direct only for a few high-volume models

For most models fal passes the provider's cost through at ~the direct rate, and several models can't be bought direct at all (open-weights, token-billed, or region-locked). fal earns its keep as one integration instead of a dozen. Three models are the real exceptions.
Keep on fal (the default)
  • Almost everything. fal ≈ direct on FLUX, GPT Image, Ideogram, Recraft, Hailuo/H3, PixVerse v5 — no meaningful savings in going direct.
  • Seedance 2.5 — fal pins 720p, so at 1080p fal is roughly half the direct price. Clear win; don't second-guess it.
  • Seedream / open-weights / GPT Image 2 — no clean direct option (region-locked, self-host, or token-billed). fal is the practical route.
Consider going direct — only if high-volume
  • Kling (Pro / Master / v3) — fal runs 2–4× Kling's own platform rate, even after matching resolution. The clearest case. Caveat: Kling charges one flat credit rate across its version tiers, and fal's higher tiers deliver higher res/fidelity — not a pure 1:1.
  • Veo 3.1 Fast — fal $0.40/s vs Google direct $0.15/s (2.7×), and it's your single priciest video model.
  • Nano Banana 2 — fal $0.15 vs Google direct $0.067/image (2.2×).
At v1 launch volumes, fal-for-everything is almost certainly right. Revisit a per-model direct integration only when one of these three becomes a large share of spend. Direct prices are Sep-2026 list-price estimates — confirm live before switching.
Pricing basis: 1 credit = $0.01 · onda price ≈ wholesale × 3 · Video credits scale linearly with clip length & round up.
