How to build a prompt library that beats Jobescape's

I collected everything Jobescape's Prompts Library shows your account, studied how its prompts are written, and checked where solorender can legally get prompts or write its own. Short version: their library is small and mostly locked. Between your earlier vflow research and what's already inside solorender, we have more reference material than they do. What's missing is a page that shows it.

1 · What Jobescape actually has

Collected from your signed-in account on 15 Oct 2026: the page data plus every scroll page and category tab, cross-checked.

Size, by category

Free (full text)Premium (locked)

By output: 160 video, 123 image, 75 text (writing and coding helpers).

How it works

  • Filters: category tabs (Cinematic, Social & Ads, Images, Work, Code), a type menu (video, image, text) and a free/premium switch.
  • Layout: 4 free prompts in a "Try for free" row, then a grid that loads 24 at a time.
  • Cards: each shows a preview picture, plus a short clip on 157 video prompts.
  • Free prompts: full text and a "Copy prompt" button.
  • Premium prompts: blurred text and "Upgrade to unlimited".
  • Daily cap: your account gets 4 copies a day, reset at midnight UTC.
  • No "Run it" button. You copy the text and take it to another tool.
  • Few models named. 50 image prompts name Nano Banana Pro. Video preview file names start with "sd25", which suggests Seedance 2.5.

How their prompts are written (from the 25 free ones)

Video · 360–600 characters

A mini short film in one paragraph:

  • The premise: who, where, what's at stake.
  • The beats in order.
  • One or two signature visual moments, such as "sand rolls like ink mist" or "the frame flashes to high-contrast black and white".

Heavy on martial arts, anime and fantasy action. They read like story pitches, not camera shot lists.

Images · 700–8,000 characters

Very long, very specific photo briefs, in two styles:

  • Photographer's spec sheet: camera body, lens, aperture, ISO, framing and skin texture.
  • JSON form: scene, subject, light, camera and an "avoid" list.

Many say "use the uploaded face, match it 100%", so they're selfie and portrait remakes. A few are fill-in templates with [SUBJECT] and [LOCATION] blanks.

Text · about 900–1,100 characters

The same recipe every time:

  • A role: "You are a recruiter who…"
  • Labelled blanks to fill in.
  • A numbered "Give me" list of outputs.
  • Honesty rules: "don't flatter me", and "write OWNER MISSING if there isn't one".

These are general office helpers (email, CVs, meeting notes, spreadsheets) with nothing to do with video.

They don't write all of it themselves. Their free "Desert Duel" video prompt is the same text as a community prompt by X creator Nim Eshed in your September vflow scrape. Jobescape shows no credit. I could only test 3 free video prompts, so one match is a small sample, but it suggests part of the library is reposted from X. Their image prompts also name real brands (Spotify, Netflix, Porsche, Nike, Prime, Bratz). solorender shouldn't copy either habit.
Where they're weak, and where solorender can win:
  • Size: 358 prompts, not "10,000+".
  • You can't try them: you copy text out to another tool. solorender can put a Try it button on every prompt that runs it on the right model.
  • Locked text: 93% is locked even for paying course members.
  • No model or price: prompts don't say which model to use or what a run costs.
  • No reuse for your own character or product: solorender's @ handles fix that.

What the law says (not legal advice):
  • Short phrases can't be copyrighted. Words like "golden hour, 35mm, slow dolly in" belong to nobody (US rule 37 CFR 202.1).
  • Long prompts are a grey area. A prompt of several sentences may count as someone's writing.
  • A site's terms can forbid copying even where copyright doesn't apply.
  • Copying a big chunk of someone's collection is riskier than borrowing single phrases.
Our rule: learn from anyone, write every prompt fresh, and copy only from CC0 or CC BY sources, with credit.

4 · How to make our own

A small, repeatable production line, so every prompt is ours and has been seen working.

Fill-in-the-blanks templates

Each library entry is built from slots, so one good template makes many tested versions:

  • Subject: or the customer's own @character / @product.
  • Action: one action per clip.
  • Scene: place, time of day, weather.
  • Camera: shot size, move, speed, final frame.
  • Light: direction and quality.
  • Style: one of our 197 built-in styles.
  • Sound (video).
  • Words in the picture (in quotes).
  • Avoid box.
  • Model and settings: so the preview can be reproduced.

The structure follows the official guides. The wording is always ours.

The production loop

1
Write. Claude drafts 3 versions per entry from the slots, following that model's official guide.
2
Run. Each version runs once on the house account, which pays the supplier's wholesale price.
3
Look. Every output gets checked against what the entry promises: full-size pictures, frame strips of clips, a listen to audio.
4
Keep the winner. Save one prompt and one preview, with the model, settings and real cost. Losers are deleted.
5
Tag and re-test. Tag by category, model, aspect, length and price. Re-run when a model updates.

Start free

Turn the 61 starters, 8 motion presets, recipe prompts and existing result pictures into the first ~100 entries. This costs almost nothing.

Mine trends, not text

Use the 8,961 vflow prompts and Jobescape's 358 titles to see which kinds of prompts people want: duels, product spins, selfie remakes, vlogs. Then write our own entries for those kinds.

Let customers share (later)

Add a "share to the library" switch with a terms update, a review step, credit to the author and the right to withdraw. Free prompts that keep growing.

5 · 40 prompt ideas to start with

Original ideas, written fresh. @product / @character means the customer's own. Several match an existing recipe; the library would hold the one-prompt version.

Cinematic shots
  • Rain-soaked arrival: a figure steps out of a taxi into neon rain. Slow push-in, puddle reflections, city hum.
  • Lighthouse keeper at dusk: wide shot, the camera cranes up as the lamp turns on and sweeps the sea.
  • Kitchen-table confession: tight two-shot under one warm bulb, a letter slides across. Held beat.
  • Desert road chase: low tracking shot beside a dusty pickup, heat shimmer, gravel spray.
Character sheets
  • Turnaround on white: @character front, three-quarter, side and back in one row.
  • Expression strip: six head shots: calm, laughing, shocked, suspicious, tired, determined.
  • Outfit rotation: @character in work, weekend and formal wear, same pose.
  • Personal kit flat-lay: the objects @character carries, neatly labelled.
Product ads
  • Floating hero spin: @product turns above a reflective plinth, rim light, dark backdrop.
  • Ingredient burst: the key ingredients fly in around @product in slow motion.
  • Morning-routine shelf: @product among everyday items on a sunlit shelf.
  • Weatherproof test: wind, rain and snow pass over @product in one shot; it stays spotless.
Social / vertical 9:16
  • POV unboxing: first-person hands open a parcel and lift out @product.
  • Before/after swipe: one smooth wipe from a messy desk to a tidy one.
  • Three-second hook: extreme close-up snaps out to reveal the whole scene.
  • Day-in-the-life: coffee, commute, desk, sunset, all in one colour grade.
Short drama
  • The wrong envelope: an office worker opens someone else's letter; slow push on their face.
  • Last train: two strangers on an empty platform, one offers an umbrella.
  • Kitchen standoff: siblings fight over a family recipe card, a pot simmering.
  • The reveal at the door: a knock, the door opens, a silhouette against hallway light.
Music video
  • Rooftop performance: blue hour, slow orbit, skyline lights blinking on.
  • Lyric in the world: one word appears on walls, mirrors and signs as the camera passes.
  • Dance in one take: an empty warehouse lit by moving window light.
  • Neon-tube portrait loop: glowing tubes pulse in time, smoky air.
Thumbnails
  • Shocked reaction + object: a big face on one side, the topic glowing on the other.
  • Versus split: two things face off across a diagonal, bold "VS".
  • Number countdown: a huge "7" surrounded by small objects.
  • Mystery box: light leaks from a crate as @character leans in.
Talking avatar / lipsync
  • Founder intro: @character at a desk explains the brand in 20 seconds.
  • Support answer: a friendly presenter answers one common question.
  • Product explainer: the presenter holds @product and names three benefits.
  • Language-switch greeting: the same welcome line in three languages.
Voiceover
  • Calm documentary narrator: warm, measured, natural pauses.
  • Hype trailer voice: rising energy, a breath before the last line.
  • Bedtime story reader: soft and slow, a whisper at the end.
  • Upbeat ad read: bright 15-second spot with a clear call to action.
Music
  • Lo-fi study loop: mellow keys, vinyl crackle, loops cleanly.
  • Cinematic trailer swell: strings to drums and brass, a hard stop for the title.
  • Upbeat product jingle: 15 seconds of bright pop with a whistle hook.
  • Suspense underscore: sparse piano over a low pulsing drone.
Also worth covering, based on what Jobescape and vflow are full of:
  • Martial-arts and anime duels.
  • "Remake my selfie as…" portraits (with the customer's own face via @character, never a celebrity).
  • Fake-brand product campaigns, e.g. an invented gaming console. Invented brands avoid trademark trouble.
  • Travel-vlog and UGC talking-head clips.
  • One-take action shots.

6 · Recommended next steps

1
Build one Prompt Library page on solorender. Every entry gets a preview, the model, the cost, and a Try it button that opens the studio with the prompt filled in. Make it searchable and sortable, with filters for category, model and price. Start with the ~100 prompts we already have.
2
Fill the gaps Jobescape shows there's demand for: cinematic and social video, product ads, and character portraits. Use the 40 ideas above and the production loop. Budget about $126 for the first 200.
3
Make every entry work with the customer's own @character or @product. Neither Jobescape nor vflow can do this.
4
Skip the office/text prompts (Jobescape's Work and Code tabs). They don't sell video, and Claude does them better.
5
Later: customer sharing with an opt-in and a terms update, plus a monthly trend pack.
