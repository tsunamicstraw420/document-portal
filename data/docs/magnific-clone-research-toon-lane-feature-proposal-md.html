Toon lane research → solorender feature proposal

Research date: 2026-09-16. Source studied: ToonBee (toonbee.ai + app.toonbee.ai), a $67-one-time "AI cartoon video maker" — its public marketing, its FastSpring checkout, and its client-side app bundles (Vite manifest + admin chunks). ToonBee is studied for product LOGIC only; solorender buys all generation wholesale from fal and copies none of their code.

The one-line finding: ToonBee is a thin orchestration wrapper over the exact same class of models solorender already resells. Their admin panel leaks the stack — OpenAI/Anthropic/Google for text, Replicate/Runware/fal for images, Alibaba (Wan 2.2 i2v) for animation, ElevenLabs for voice. solorender already owns ~70% of that pipeline. The cartoon "lane" is mostly packaging plus one genuinely new layer: audio (voiceover + music).

1. What ToonBee actually does, in plain English

One product, one promise: type a script or an idea → get a finished, narrated, multi-scene cartoon video. No timeline, no per-shot fiddling. Under the hood it's an assembly line, and every stage is a rented API call:

  1. Write. An LLM turns the idea into a script, splits it into scenes, and writes a storyboard (a prompt per scene + a narration line).
  2. Draw. A text-to-image model draws each scene as a cartoon still in a chosen style; a "character" model keeps the hero looking the same across scenes.
  3. Animate. Each still is fed to an image-to-video model that makes it move (5–8s clips).
  4. Voice + score. A text-to-speech model narrates it (55+ languages); a music model adds a background bed; captions auto-sync.
  5. Assemble. Their server stitches the clips + audio + captions into one MP4 and "cloud-renders" it.

That is the whole magic. It maps almost one-to-one onto machinery solorender already runs.

3. The genuinely new pieces, ranked

Effort scale matches the Runway proposal: small = one new tool card / preset reusing existing plumbing; medium = one new input/output type (audio) or a render-worker change; large = a new subsystem.

Piece 1 — Voiceover (text-to-speech) as a generated asset. The single most important new capability, and the render worker is already built to receive it (per-block audioUrl). fal supply (verified in the Runway doc): fal-ai/elevenlabs/tts/eleven-v3, fal-ai/elevenlabs/tts/multilingual-v2 — pennies wholesale, so ~1–3 cr per scene line at 3×, extremely margin-friendly. Effort: medium (new audio output type + a voice picker; feed the URL into the Flow "Render film" spec). Lives in: a new "Speak" node in Flow, and a voice picker inside the Toon lane.

Piece 2 — Background music bed. One track chosen by mood, mixed under the whole film. Note: the worker currently muxes audio per block (narration), so a global music bed is the one real render-worker change — add an optional spec.musicUrl mixed under the concatenated output at low gain (ducking optional later). fal supply: a music model (Lyria / MiniMax Music / Stable Audio class — pick + verify the id per DOCS.md §7 before wiring). Effort: small-medium (one fal call + ~15 lines of ffmpeg in worker.js). Lives in: Toon lane "music" toggle; spec.musicUrl in the render spec.

Piece 3 — Caption timings. The worker already burns captions; we just need to produce the [{start,end,text}]. Cheapest path: ElevenLabs TTS can return word/character timings with the audio, so narration and captions come from the same call — near-zero extra cost. Effort: small. Lives in: the Speak step emits both audioUrl and captions for that block. (ToonBee's "word-by-word animated" caption styling is a later polish on top of the plain SRT burn.)

Piece 4 — A "Toon" style pack. Curated one-click looks — 3D/Pixar-ish, classic 2D, comic book, modern flat, storybook, fine-art — each a tuned prompt preset over an existing image model (Seedream / Recraft / Nano Banana), auto-applied to every scene. Recraft especially earns its place here (native illustration/vector styles). Effort: small (pure config + preset strings; no new endpoint). Lives in: a style selector in the Toon lane; presets in config.ts.

Piece 5 — The guided "Toon flow" wrapper (the actual product). This is what makes it feel like ToonBee instead of a node editor. A one-screen recipe: paste script (or idea) → pick style → pick voice → Generate — which behind the scenes pre-builds a wired Flow graph (Story-Director → per-scene Image[style] → Animate → Speak → Render film) and runs it. It's the same "recipe / pre-wired graph" pattern already recommended as Gap 5 in the Runway proposal. Effort: medium (a template that emits a Flow graph + a guided form). Lives in: a dashboard "Make a cartoon" entry that drops into a pre-filled Flow.

Not worth copying: ToonBee's deception layer — fake "$352" anchor, "no monthly fees (today)", "unlimited" that their own Terms cap at 10k credits/yr, and the checkout upsell stack. solorender's honest credits-not-commitments model is the counter-positioning, not something to imitate.

4. What a cartoon actually costs — and the one lever that decides it

Almost the entire cost of an AI cartoon is a single line: paying to animate every second with an image-to-video model. Sixty seconds of Hailuo/Kling at wholesale ×3 is ~570–840 credits on its own — roughly 85% of a "fully animated" minute. The script, stills, voice and music together are ~150 credits, pocket change. (That animation bill is also the exact one ToonBee pays, which is why their "unlimited" is capped.)

So the real product decision is how much of the minute is truly animated — most cartoons don't need 60 seconds of real motion. Three tiers, ~1-minute explainer, at the ×3 sticker:

Two levers do all the work:

  • Don't animate what doesn't need it. A narrated, illustrated pan/zoom slideshow is a real product — it's most "cartoon story," kids, and faith channels on YouTube. It needs one small render-worker addition: accept an image block + duration and apply an ffmpeg zoompan (Ken Burns) — free motion, no model call. This single change is the biggest cost lever on the whole list, and it's what turns the default toon from "$8" into "$4" (or "$1.50" as a pure slideshow).
  • Cheaper animate model where you do animate — Hailuo 2.3 Fast (57 cr / 6s) is the floor; step up to Kling only where a shot earns it.

Default the Toon lane to Tier B; let power users pay up to Tier C. All three are honest and per-use — a Starter pack (500 cr / $5) makes ~three slideshow toons or one hybrid minute — which is the pitch ToonBee can't make: no cap, no "(today)" asterisk, no upsell wall, and you see exactly what each video costs.

5. Recommended build order

  1. Voiceover — the "Speak" node (fal-ai/elevenlabs/tts/). Unlocks narration and* caption timings in one call; the render worker already muxes and burns. Nothing else on this list matters without it.
  2. Image-block pan/zoom in the render worker (small: accept imageUrl + duration, apply ffmpeg zoompan). The biggest cost lever — it turns the default cartoon from "animate every second" (~$8) into "animate what matters" (~$4), and unlocks the $1.50 slideshow tier. Do it early; everything downstream gets cheaper.
  3. Toon style pack (config-only). Cheapest lift, and it's what makes the output look like a cartoon product rather than generic image→video. Ship alongside #1–2.
  4. The guided Toon flow wrapper (pre-wired Flow recipe + one-screen form, defaulting to the hybrid tier). Turns the parts into the product a beginner actually buys.
  5. Music bed (fal music model + the small worker.js mix step). The finishing 10% that makes a toon feel finished.
  6. Animated caption styling (word-by-word) — polish on the existing burn, only once 1–5 are landing.

Everything above rides existing plumbing: fal supply, the ×3 ledger, the Flow canvas, the Director, and a render worker that was — conveniently — already designed to take audio and captions. The cartoon lane isn't a new engine; it's a new flow over the one solorender already has.
