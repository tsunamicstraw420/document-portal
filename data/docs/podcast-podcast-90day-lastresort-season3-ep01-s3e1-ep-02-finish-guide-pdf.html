The Edit page, top to bottom
 • Media Pool (top-left) — the bin of all your clips and graphics, like a folder.
 • Preview windows (top-middle/right) — the screen where you watch your video.
 • Inspector (top-right, a button you toggle) — where you change a selected clip's size, position, and
   volume.
 • Timeline (bottom half) — the strip where your episode is laid out left-to-right in time. This is where you'll
   spend 90% of your time.
 • Toolbar (just above the timeline) — the little icons for the editing tools.

 Timeline vocabulary (memorize these five):
 • Playhead — the vertical line showing where you are in time. Drag it, or click in the ruler to move it.
 • Track — a horizontal lane. V1, V2, V3… are video lanes (higher number = on top of the picture). A1, A2… are
 audio lanes.
 • Clip — one block of video or audio sitting on a track.
 • Trim — dragging a clip's left or right edge to make it shorter/longer.
 • Snapping — a magnet that makes clips click neatly against each other. Toggle with N .

5. Fixing the timing — the main job
 PHASE: THE REAL WORK

This is the heart of the edit. The goal is simple: make each scene clip land on the words Ruby is saying
about it. When she says "May 20th, Patrick files for divorce," the Patrick-signing clip should be on screen.
You'll do this one clip at a time, and it's very repetitive once you get the rhythm.

The three tools you'll use (and how to switch)

 Tool                     Key     What it does

 Selection (arrow)          A     Move a clip, or drag its edges to trim. Your default tool.

 Blade (razor)              B     Click on a clip to cut it into two pieces at that spot.

 Snapping (magnet)          N     Not a tool — a toggle. ON = clips click together with no gaps. Keep it ON.


  After using the Blade, press A to get the arrow back. A huge number of "why isn't this working" moments are
  just having the wrong tool selected.


The core move: land a clip on the right word
This is the loop you'll repeat for every scene. Read it twice, then do it.
 1. Find the moment. Play the narration (spacebar) and listen for where a scene should appear — e.g., the
    first word of "May 20th." Stop the playhead right on that word. Nudge it frame-by-frame with the ← / →
   arrow keys to get it exact.
 2. Look at what's there. Is the correct scene clip already near here, just in the wrong spot? Usually yes.
 3. Move the clip to the playhead. With the arrow tool ( A ), drag the scene clip left or right until its left edge
   snaps to the playhead line. (Snapping makes this click into place.)
 4. Set how long it stays. Play forward to where she stops talking about it. Drag the clip's right edge to that
    point so it ends when she moves on.
 5. Close any gap. If moving the clip left a black gap on the track, right-click the gap → Ripple Delete (this
   pulls everything after it leftward to fill the hole). Or click the gap and press Delete then check nothing
   overlapped.

  Ruby is your anchor — don't move her. The Ruby clips are already synced to her voice. Leave them where
  they are. You're only sliding the scene clips around them. If a scene needs more room, trim the scene, not Ruby.


Trimming — two kinds, plain English
  • Normal trim: drag a clip's edge. This can leave a gap (if you shorten) or cover the next clip. Simple,
    visual.



                                    Reality Recaps Unfiltered — EP-02 Finish Guide                             Page 6 of 15
  • Ripple trim: hold nothing special — in Resolve, dragging an edge with snapping and then ripple-deleting
    gaps keeps everything tight. The mantra: trim, then ripple-delete any black gap.

  Black gap = a hole with no video. On export it shows as a black screen. After any move or trim, scan the V1
  track for black gaps and close them with right-click → Ripple Delete.


Dealing with the repeated placeholder clips
To fill long stretches, the rough cut sometimes repeats the same scene clip back-to-back (you'll see the
same thumbnail two or three times in a row). That's a placeholder. Two easy fixes:
  • Just want it shorter: delete the extra copies (click one, press Delete ) and ripple-delete the gap. Then
   stretch a nearby clip or a Ruby loop to cover the space.
  • Want variety: replace a repeat with a different clip. Drag another clip from the Media Pool on top of it, or
    use a Ruby reaction loop (RL-2, RL-6) as a cutaway.

Work in chunks, in this order
Don't try to do all 21 minutes at once. The episode has natural segments — fix one, save, take a breath,
move on. Use the narration to know where you are:

6. Make every clip fill the screen
 PHASE: THE REAL WORK

Your generated clips are just slightly smaller than a full 1080p screen, so without a fix you might see thin
black edges. You already turned on the global fix in Section 3, step 4. If any single clip still doesn't fill the
frame:
 1. Click the clip once to select it.
 2. Open the Inspector (button, top-right). Under Transform, nudge Zoom up slightly (e.g., 1.05) until the
    black edges disappear.
 3. To apply the same zoom to many clips: right-click the fixed clip → Copy, then select the others, right-click
    → Paste Attributes → check Zoom/Transform → OK.

  A tiny bit of softness from this zoom is normal and unavoidable — the clips were generated just under full size.
  Nobody watching will notice.



7. Add the graphics — date cards, bubbles, ratings, logo
 PHASE: DRESS IT UP

All your graphics are pre-made transparent PNG images in the overlays folder. "Transparent" means only
the graphic shows — it floats over your video with no background box. You put them on a higher video track
(V2) so they sit on top of the picture.

Import them
 1. Top menu File → Import → Media. Browse to 90Day_LastResort_Season3/overlays , select all the
   PNGs, Open. They land in the Media Pool.
 2. Do the same for still frames/RRU_logo_neon_transparent.png (your logo) and the endcard.png .

Place one
 1. If there's no V2 track yet: right-click the track-name area on the left → Add Track.
 2. Drag a graphic (say datecard_may20.png ) from the Media Pool onto V2, positioned above the moment it
   should appear.
 3. Drag its edges to control how long it's on screen (2–3 seconds is typical for a card).
 4. Reposition it on screen: select it → Inspector → Transform → change Position so it sits where you want
    (the date cards are designed for the upper-left).

8. Add music & sound effects
 PHASE: DRESS IT UP

Music sits under the narration, quiet, to set mood. You'll get tracks from Artlist (your licensed music source).
Download a few beds: something dramatic (arrest saga), something lighter (couple gossip), something warm
(Kara & Guillermo), plus an intro/outro sting.
 1. Import the music files: File → Import → Media, pick your downloaded tracks.
 2. Add an audio track for music: right-click the audio track headers → Add Track. Ruby's narration is on A1;
    put music on A2.
 3. Drag a music track onto A2 under the segment it belongs to. Trim it to the segment's length.
 4. Turn it down so Ruby stays clear. Select the music clip → Inspector → Volume, lower it until it's
    background (often around −18 to −24 dB). Rule: you should barely notice the music while she talks.
 5. Fade the edges so music doesn't start/stop abruptly: hover the top corner of the music clip — a little
    handle appears — drag it inward to create a fade in / fade out.

  "Ducking" in plain terms: making the music dip whenever Ruby speaks. The simple manual way is just
  keeping music low the whole time (above). That's completely fine for launch. Auto-ducking exists on the Fairlight
  page but skip it for now.


  Sound effects (optional): a record-scratch on "Deported," a soft whoosh on hot takes, a camera-shutter on the
  date cards, a gavel on "charges dropped." Artlist has these too. Drop them on A3 at the exact moment.



9. Captions (later, the easy way)
 PHASE: DRESS IT UP

Skip captions in Resolve. After you upload to YouTube, YouTube auto-generates captions for free, and you
pull that transcript for your show notes anyway. Don't hand-type captions here — it's hours you don't need to
spend. (If you ever want burned-in captions for Shorts later, that's a separate, optional pass.)

11. Export the finished video
 PHASE: SHIP IT

 1. Click the Deliver page (bottom bar, far right — the rocket icon).
 2. Top-left, click the YouTube preset (Resolve has a ready-made one). It auto-sets H.264, 1080p.
 3. Set Filename to EP-02_LastResort_S3E1_FINAL .
 4. Set Location — click Browse and choose your 90Day_LastResort_Season3 folder.
 5. Confirm: Resolution 1920×1080, Frame rate 24, Format MP4, Codec H.264.
 6. Click Add to Render Queue (right side).
 7. Click Render All. A progress bar runs; a 21-minute video takes a few minutes to an hour depending on
    your Mac. When it's done, the MP4 is in your folder.

  Free-version note: DaVinci Resolve free exports up to 4K — 1080p is well within that. No watermark, no limit.
  You're fine.



12. Upload to YouTube & publish
 PHASE: SHIP IT

Your podcast runs on a two-step publish: unlisted first, public later. Here's why and how.

Wednesday — upload as Unlisted
 1. Go to youtube.com, signed in as the Reality Recaps Unfiltered account. Click the camera "+" →
    Upload video.
 2. Choose your EP-02…FINAL.mp4 .
 3. Title, description (your standard show blurb), thumbnail. Set visibility to Unlisted (not Private, not Public)
    → Save.

  Why unlisted first? Once it's on YouTube, YouTube writes an automatic transcript. Claude uses that transcript
  to draft your show notes, chapter markers, and clip captions — before the episode goes public. Unlisted = it
  exists and has a transcript, but isn't shown to the public yet.


Between — Claude writes your content
Tell Claude the video is up (unlisted) and Claude will pull the YouTube transcript and draft the description,
chapters, and Short captions.

Friday — flip to Public
 1. In YouTube Studio, open the video → change visibility from Unlisted to Public (or schedule it).
