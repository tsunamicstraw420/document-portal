Written for someone doing this for the first time. Two halves: music (long, underneath) and sound effects (short, on top). They do completely different jobs.

Part 1 — The mental model

Music is furniture. Sound effects are punctuation.

Music sits under everything and tells the viewer how to feel. They shouldn't consciously notice it. If they do, it's too loud.

Sound effects mark specific moments — a card slams on, a bubble pops, you move to a new topic. Each one is a full stop or an exclamation mark. Punctuation you can hear.

The single biggest beginner mistake is treating sound effects like decoration and sprinkling them everywhere. Every professional guide says the same thing: fewer, placed deliberately, beats more. A whoosh on every cut becomes wallpaper in ninety seconds. A whoosh on the eight moments that actually matter tells the viewer this is a new chapter.

The four kinds of sound you'll use

KindWhat it isWhere in EP-02
StingerA short hit — impact, slam, thudWhen a date card or the 8/10 badge appears
WhooshA swish that carries you from A to BMoving from one couple's story to the next
Pop / UISmall, light, quickSpeech bubbles appearing
AmbienceQuiet room tone under a sceneCastle at night, therapy room

That's it. Four categories covers this whole show. Using the same whoosh every time is a feature, not laziness — it's how a viewer learns your show's grammar.

The rule that overrides all the others

Never put a sound effect on a serious moment. EP-02 has an arrest, a domestic battery charge, and someone being told to leave the country. A comic boing over any of that reads as making light of it. The date cards work because they're factual — a timeline, not a punchline. But when Ruby is sitting with something heavy, let it be quiet. Silence is a sound design choice.

Part 2 — How loud

Numbers you can just use:

  • Ruby's voice: −6 to −12 dB. She's the star. Everything else works around her.
  • Music under Ruby: −18 to −25 dB. Roughly 20 below her. It should feel like it's in another room.
  • Music with nobody talking: −12 to −14 dB. Intro, outro, the beat after a punchline — let it breathe.
  • Sound effects: −12 to −18 dB. Audible, never startling.
  • Whole episode, exported: −14 LUFS — that's YouTube's target. Resolve has a loudness meter under the Fairlight tab.

Use ducking rather than doing this by hand. Resolve can automatically dip the music whenever Ruby speaks and lift it when she stops. That one feature does 80% of a good mix. Search "ducking" in Resolve's Fairlight page.

Tier 2 — once Tier 1 feels right

The roll call, 00:01:03 → 00:01:10. Five couples in seven seconds, one shot each. A tiny tick or soft whoosh on each name gives it rhythm. Keep them small — this is fast and busy already.

Ambience beds. Very low, under the scene, barely conscious:

  • 00:02:24 castle exterior at night — distant wind
  • 00:01:41 and 00:16:13 therapy circle — quiet room tone
  • 00:02:33 and 00:03:18 terrace — light outdoor air

Deliberate silence at 00:02:24–00:03:20. The arrest sequence. Consider pulling the music out here and letting Ruby carry it dry, then bringing it back at 00:03:20. Absence of sound is the strongest tool you have, and it costs nothing.

Part 4 — Working order

  1. Music first. Lay the beds, set levels, turn ducking on. Watch it through.
  2. Then the overlay hits. Eight sounds, exact timecodes above.
  3. Then the six transitions.
  4. Watch it start to finish without touching anything. Note where your ear snags.
  5. Ambience last, if at all. It's the least important and the easiest to overdo.

Don't start sound effects until you're sure the cut is locked. If a clip moves later, every effect after it slides out of sync.

Part 5 — Where the sounds come from

Music: Magnific, using Lyria 3 Pro. 160 credits flat for anything up to 3 minutes, so always ask for the full 180 seconds even if you only need 20 — same price, and you can trim.

Sound effects: Magnific's Sound Effects generator also costs credits and is excluded from your unlimited plan. Free alternatives worth checking first, because SFX are generic and there's no reason to pay:

  • freesound.org — huge, free, check each licence
  • Pixabay Sound Effects — free, no attribution required
  • YouTube Audio Library — free, built for exactly this

You need about six unique sounds total for Tier 1: one stamp, one pop, one whoosh, one impact, one shimmer, one transition whoosh. Everything else is reuse.
