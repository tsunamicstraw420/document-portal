Shorts — research findings & in-house build plan

Researched July 24 2026. Bottom line: we can build a Shorts factory in-house that costs $0 per clip and is actually better-suited to our animated show than the paid tools. Here's why, and the plan.

Part 1 — What the paid tools actually do

OpusClip (~$15/mo, the market leader) does three things:

  1. ClipAnything — scans the long video and picks the segments most likely to go viral, scoring each 0–99.
  2. ReframeAnything — crops landscape → vertical 9:16 using AI subject-tracking, keeping the active speaker centered.
  3. Auto-captions — animated word-by-word captions burned in.

The open-source equivalent of the reframe piece is Google's MediaPipe AutoFlip — free, but it needs TensorFlow + a heavy install, and it's built to crop to follow a subject.

The key realization

Two of those three features don't help us, and one we get for free.

  • Auto-selection — we don't need an AI to guess viral moments. I already picked the five best clips from the actual transcript (in SHORTS_GUIDE.md), and I can pick them for every future episode because I have the script. My judgment on what's shocking beats a virality score.
  • Subject-tracking crop is wrong for us. Our footage is graphic-novel animation with deliberate wide compositions — the castle, two-shots of couples, the split panels. Cropping to a tall 9:16 slice would throw away most of the art we spent credits generating. OpusClip's reframe is built for talking-head podcasts, not animation.
  • Animated captions — we get these for free. This is the one genuinely valuable feature, and it's the thing paid tools lock behind a subscription. We have word-level timing for all 2,651 words (start/end of every word, from the lip-sync data). That's exactly what drives karaoke-style captions. We can burn them in ourselves, perfectly synced, at zero cost.

Verdict: an in-house ffmpeg pipeline beats OpusClip for our specific content, and saves ~$180/yr.

Part 2 — What the research says makes a Short work (2026)

  • The hook window is ~1 second, not 3. On a swipeable feed the keep-or-swipe decision happens almost instantly. Open on the single most provocative line — a bold claim or a curiosity gap. One sentence.
  • 4-beat spine: Hook (0–2s) → Context (2–7s) → Payoff (7–35s) → Loop.
  • Loop the ending. If the last frame flows back into the first, viewers rewatch without noticing, and the algorithm reads replays as a strong signal.
  • Motion every 2–3 seconds to reset attention — a zoom punch, an angle change, a caption pop.
  • 20–35 seconds is the sweet spot for completion rate.
  • Captions are non-negotiable and should be big, bold, and animated (word highlight), kept clear of YouTube's bottom/right UI.

Part 3 — The in-house pipeline (what I'll build)

A reusable script, make_short.py, that takes a start time, end time, and a hook line, and outputs a finished 1080×1920 MP4. Zero credits, all ffmpeg + our own assets.

Each Short is built as:

┌─────────────────────────┐  1080 wide × 1920 tall
│   LOGO  ·  HOOK LINE     │  ← top brand strip: neon logo + the one-sentence hook
├─────────────────────────┤
│                         │
│   THE 16:9 FOOTAGE      │  ← the episode clip, full width, nothing cropped.
│   (blurred echo-fill    │     Behind it, a blurred copy of the same frame fills
│    behind it)           │     the tall space so there are no dead bars.
│                         │
├─────────────────────────┤
│   BIG KARAOKE CAPTIONS   │  ← word-by-word highlight, Bangers, brand colors,
│                         │     synced to the word-level timings. Above the UI zone.
└─────────────────────────┘

Design decisions, grounded in the research:

  1. No cropping — the artwork is the point. Blurred echo-fill (the standard "stylized pillarbox") makes the vertical space feel full without losing any frame.
  2. Optional punch-in for close-ups — when a clip is a single centered subject (Ruby talking, one face), the script can zoom the footage to fill more of the frame. Toggle per clip.
  3. Karaoke captions from the word JSON — the marquee feature, free. Current word highlighted in pink/gold, the rest white. This is what makes it look professionally clipped.
  4. Hook text top, first ~2s — the bold claim, then it fades so the captions carry the rest.
  5. Subtle Ken Burns drift on the footage to satisfy "motion every few seconds" even on held shots.
  6. Loop-friendly — I'll trim each clip to end on a beat that flows back to its open where the content allows.

Cost per Short: $0. Time per Short once built: seconds of render. For a backlog of episodes, I can batch all five clips per episode in one run.

Part 4 — What I still can't do in-house (and don't need to)

  • AI subject-tracking crop — skipped on purpose (wrong for animation). If we ever shoot live talking-head segments, we'd revisit MediaPipe AutoFlip.
  • Auto-picking clips by ML virality score — replaced by my read of the transcript. Cheaper and, for a scripted recap, more reliable.
  • Trending-audio matching — a manual choice at upload; not an automation problem.
