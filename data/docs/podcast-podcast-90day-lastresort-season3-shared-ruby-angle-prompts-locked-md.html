LOGO BAKE — DONE, and how to redo it (Aug 6 2026)

Script: _SHARED/bake_ruby_logo.py (PIL, free, no generation). Edit the JOBS table at the top and rerun.

Target look = _SHARED/still frames/Ruby_neon_set_2026_LOGO.png (approved). That plate is SHARP and saturated and sits on a DARKER patch of wall. Match it.

Three passes, in this order:

  1. Darken the wall where the sign goes — a feathered round multiply to ~0.5

at centre. A real sign is the light source; the brick around it falls off. This is what makes the neon read.

  1. The sign, SHARP — _SHARED/RRU_logo_2026_transparent.png (the OPEN TUBE

version, 1128x1084), saturation x1.18, contrast x1.08. Never Gaussian-blur the logo. Not even 2 px. Blur mushes the small type and instantly reads as "the AI drew this logo" — that is exactly what got the first pass rejected. Also do NOT use still frames/RRU_logo_neon_transparent.png; that one is the solid purple disc and looks like a sticker on brick.

  1. Bloom that follows the tubes — take the logo's own RGB masked by its

alpha, Gaussian-blur it ~5.5% of the logo width, and screen it back on at ~0.55. A flat magenta disc over the whole area does the opposite of what you want: it BRIGHTENS the wall and kills the contrast the sign needs.

  1. Correct the ring to a true circle. Measured at threshold, the ring

inside RRU_logo_2026_transparent.png is 1031 px wide by 1056 px tall — 2.4% taller than wide. Scaling the file by its own canvas ratio lands a slightly tall OVAL on the wall, which Eddie spots immediately. The script applies ROUND_FIX = 1031/1056 to the height. Never remove it.

Never use squeeze for "perspective." A 0.93 horizontal squeeze on the low-left plate read as a wonky oval logo, not as a raked wall. Leave it at 1.00.

  1. Restore the foreground occluders. The sign is ON THE WALL, so anything

in front of the wall must stay in front of it. The mic boom arm crosses the logo area on the close-up and three-quarter; without this step the logo paints over the arm and reads as a sticker floating in front of the mic. The occluders list traces the arm as a thick line and restores those pixels from the clean plate — after the sign goes down but BEFORE the bloom, so the glow still spills across the arm the way real light would.

Don't try to auto-matte the arm by luminance or saturation: on this set the arm and the shadowed brick sit in the same dark purple range, and every threshold that catches the arm also eats the wall. Tracing the line by hand off a coordinate grid is faster and exact.
