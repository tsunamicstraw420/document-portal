Reality TV B-Roll Style Guide & Few-Shot Examples (v2 - Improved)

Use this document as few-shot reference material when generating B-roll suggestions. Load it into context or reference specific sections when calling the main analysis prompt.

Core Visual Language for Reality TV Recaps (2026 High-Performing Style)

1. High-Energy Drama / Argument / Blow-up Moments

Visual signature: Tense group dynamics, aggressive body language, quick reaction cutaways, dramatic lighting.

Recommended generative prompt structure: "A cinematic [DURATION]-second shot of [NUMBER] elegantly dressed women in their [AGE]s having an intense argument around a modern luxury [SETTING: dinner table / living room / yacht deck]. [SPECIFIC ACTION: One woman leans forward aggressively / points finger / stands up]. Dramatic side lighting, shallow depth of field, subtle slow push-in or handheld energy, filmic color grade with cool teal/blue tones, high production value, reality TV aesthetic."

Strong examples:

  • "Cinematic 11-second shot of five women in elegant dresses arguing heatedly at a long modern dining table at night. One woman in red leans forward aggressively while another looks visibly shocked and hurt. Dramatic side lighting from window, shallow depth of field, slow subtle push-in, cool teal color grade, reality TV production quality."
  • "Wide cinematic shot of a group of women in a modern living room during a major confrontation. Quick cutaways to individual shocked and angry faces. Tense energy, filmic lighting, subtle Dutch angle on the most heated moment."

2. Emotional / Vulnerable / Crying / Confessionals

Visual signature: Extreme close-ups on eyes/tears, soft dramatic lighting, contrast with luxury surroundings.

Recommended prompt: "Extreme close-up cinematic shot of a beautiful [ETHNICITY] woman in her [AGE]s with real tears streaming down her cheeks, raw emotional and vulnerable expression, soft dramatic key light from one side, shallow depth of field, very slow gentle push-in, high-end reality TV confessional style, emotional and cinematic."

Strong examples:

  • "Extreme close-up of a Black woman in her 40s crying during a confessional. Tears rolling down her face, smudged mascara, raw pain in her eyes. Soft dramatic lighting, shallow depth of field, slow subtle zoom, high production value."
  • "Cinematic shot of a woman sitting alone on a luxurious balcony at night, looking devastated after a betrayal reveal. Soft wind in hair, city lights in background, emotional and lonely atmosphere, filmic color grade."

3. Shade / Sarcastic / Passive-Aggressive Moments

Visual signature: Smirks, side-eyes, slow reactions, split-screen comparisons.

Prompt example: "Cinematic close-up of a woman delivering shade with a subtle smirk and side-eye. Slow zoom on her face as she raises an eyebrow. Luxurious modern home background slightly out of focus, warm but slightly cold color grade, reality TV confessional lighting."

4. "The Moment" / Shocking Reveal / Betrayal

Visual signature: Dramatic zooms, Dutch angles, quick cuts between faces, slow-motion reactions.

Prompt example: "Dramatic cinematic shot of a woman hearing devastating news at a group dinner. Her face drops in shock. Slow-motion reaction as she processes. Other women’s faces in soft focus reacting behind her. High contrast lighting, filmic teal and orange grade, intense reality TV moment."

5. Luxury Lifestyle / Vacation / Party / Fun Moments (for contrast)

Visual signature: Bright, aspirational, slightly faster energy.

Prompt example: "Beautiful cinematic B-roll of a luxury modern villa pool at golden hour. Group of women in elegant swimwear toasting with champagne. Bright warm color grade, slight handheld movement, aspirational and high-end reality TV vacation aesthetic."

6. Your Talking Head / Analysis Sections

  • Prefer Picture-in-Picture (PiP) of you reacting over full cutaways.
  • Use subtle background B-roll (blurred luxury interior, soft animated text, or slow Ken Burns on relevant screenshots).
  • Keep visuals supportive — do not distract from your commentary.

What NOT to Do (Critical for Brand Quality)

  • Never use overly generic happy/corporate stock for dramatic content.
  • Avoid long static shots — reality TV pacing is dynamic.
  • Do not copy show footage directly without heavy transformation + fair use justification.
  • Never make the recap feel cheap or low-effort with bad stock or weak generative prompts.
  • Avoid B-roll that fights the audio or makes the host’s commentary secondary.

Pro Tip: When in doubt, go more specific in your generative prompt (age, ethnicity, clothing color, exact emotion, lighting direction, camera movement). Vague prompts produce generic results.
