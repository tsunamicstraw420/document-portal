Summary

Aleph is a tool from Runway ML that Anthony describes as Hollywood-level visual effects: upload a video, describe the change you want, and Aleph produces it. He wasn't a Runway user before (other course tools did more), but Aleph won him over. Access is via a Runway ML subscription (affordable entry tier, then buy credits — ~60 credits per generation, 1,000 credits for $10).

How it works

On Runway's site: Create → Generate video → upload a video (Select Asset) → type a prompt describing the change. It currently only affects/creates 5 seconds (a longer upload only has its first 5 seconds changed). Hit Generate.

Examples shown

  • Day → night (a Switzerland hiking clip made with Kling on Fal AI): "change the scene to night with a large full moon illuminating the mountainside." Decent but one of his weaker results — turning full daylight into night is hard.
  • Engulf a T-Rex in flames (a Veo 3 ad clip): "engulf the dinosaur in flame" → incredible result, far beyond manual VFX skill.
  • New camera angles from one clip (a robot on a park bench, made with Pixverse on Fal AI): "create a new wider angle version" kept the robot, its movement, everything (only the skyline shifted slightly). He also generated a tight close-up. This is huge for filmmaking — you can cut between wide and tight shots of the same AI scene while keeping character consistency, which previously required painstaking image-gen + image-to-video work.
  • Weather changes (Anthony at a rainy window): "change the weather outside to snow" worked well; "outside the window a large fire rages" produced a near-perfect fire seen through the glass with realistic optics — the kind of shot Aleph excels at.
  • New angle for a short film: "create a new angle from directly behind the person, window centered, symmetrical, subtle push-in" → a convincing unique angle.

The intro "hand on fire" effect (step by step)

  1. Recorded himself on his phone snapping and pretending his hand caught fire.
  2. Uploaded to Aleph with the prompt "his arm and hand is on fire." Output shows the hand on fire the entire time.
  3. In basic editing (Premiere shown; CapCut/DaVinci work too): layer the AI clip over the original, cut at the finger-snap, delete the pre-snap portion so the fire ignites exactly on the snap, and fine-tune timing.
  4. Added three layered sound effects (snap, flame whoosh, campfire crackle) to sell the effect.

Takeaway

Aleph is a powerful, affordable VFX and camera-angle tool that unlocks shots (fire, weather, new angles, effects) that would otherwise require serious VFX skill — and it's only improving.
