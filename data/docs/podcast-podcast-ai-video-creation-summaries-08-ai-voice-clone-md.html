AI Voice Clone

Module: AI Video Creation · Instructor: Anthony · Length: ~7.5 min Lesson URL: https://www.contentcreator.com/products/ai-creator-course/categories/2157469701/posts/2197590892

Summary

A follow-up to the AI Voiceovers lesson, focused specifically on creating your own AI voice clone in ElevenLabs (via PromptEdit).

Why make a voice clone

  • Skip recording: type a script and get a voiceover in your own voice — useful when you don't want to be on camera or you sound off (e.g., a cold making you nasally).
  • Feed your voice into other AI tools (the way Anthony mainly uses it):
  • HeyGen: upload an image of yourself plus an audio file from your voice clone → a talking AI clone in your own voice, with no recording needed. Also great for fixing a mistake in a long video — regenerate just that segment via the clone + HeyGen instead of re-setting up cameras.
  • SeaDance 2.0 (on PromptEdit): a cinematic, Hollywood-style video generator (vs. HeyGen's more static talking-head) with strong camera control. Unlike Kling/Veo — which generate whatever voice they want — SeaDance lets you upload your own audio file, so you get consistent voice across clips.

How to create the clone

PromptEdit → Audio tab → Voice → ElevenLabs TTS → select "use your own voice." Name the clone, then either upload an audio file or record via your computer mic.

Best practices:

  • Use the best microphone you can — the clone mimics your sample, so cheap/low-quality audio produces a poor clone.
  • Use a long sample — the longer the recording, the more accurate the clone (he used an 11-minute clip pulled from a previous course video).
  • Leave "remove background noise" unchecked — that extra processing can hurt quality; better to start with a clean, quiet recording.

Using it

Once created, select your clone from the voice dropdown, paste your script, and adjust the Stability slider (lower = more expressive, higher = more consistent). Anthony brings his down because his real voice is energetic and expressive; someone calm and even-toned would raise it. Experiment, since AI clones some voices better than others. Then Generate and Download the audio to use in any video.
