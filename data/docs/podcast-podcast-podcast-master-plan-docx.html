Episode Format Blueprint

The most successful reality TV podcasts follow a reliable structure. Consistency builds habit — listeners come back because they know what to expect.

Episode Types to Rotate

  • Weekly recap – Core episode tied to that week's airing. Your bread and butter.
  • Hot takes – Short 20–30 min off-week episode: your unfiltered opinions on a topic, a controversial moment, or off-season news.
  • Fan interview – Bring in a superfan from your groups. Ask them about their experience with the show, their predictions, fan theories. They WILL share this episode.
  • Cast/producer interview – Work toward this. Start with fan-adjacent creators (fan accounts, other reality podcasters). Reach out to minor cast via Instagram DM.
  • Reunion breakdown – When reunion episodes air, record a long-form reaction. These episodes historically get 2–3x normal downloads.
  • Year-in-review / season wrap-up – Rank every cast member, best moment, worst moment, predictions for next season. Great for evergreen growth.

Section 5: Launch Strategy

Pre-Launch Checklist (Days 1–14)

  • Choose your show name and register it everywhere: Apple Podcasts, Spotify, TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Facebook Page.
  • Create cover art in Canva (3000×3000px). Don't overthink it – you can update it later.
  • Set up RSS.com account. Configure your show: name, description, categories (Comedy + TV & Film), language, explicit settings.
  • Record 3 episodes before launching. Episode 1: Who are you and what is this show. Episodes 2–3: Your first two recaps.
  • Edit all three. Write show notes with timestamps.
  • Set up Patreon with at least one tier ($5 'Fan' tier). Even if you only get 3 patrons, it's money on day one.
  • Build your episode thumbnail template in Canva.
  • Write a launch announcement for each Facebook group. Be specific about what the show is, where to find it, and what to expect.


Launch Day (Day 15)

  • Drop all 3 episodes simultaneously. More episodes = more binge potential = more follow-through rates on new listeners.
  • Post in ALL FOUR Facebook groups with links. Vary the copy slightly so it doesn't look copy-pasted.
  • Post a pinned announcement on each group page.
  • Release 2–3 short clips on TikTok and Instagram Reels on launch day. At least one clip should be your most controversial take from the first episodes.
  • Email all contacts who are reality TV fans. Text the news to close friends.
  • Ask for reviews: 'It would mean everything if you took 30 seconds to leave a review on Apple Podcasts.'


Post-Launch (Days 16–30)

  • Respond to EVERY comment and DM for the first 30 days. This is how superfans are made.
  • Share listener reactions in your stories and posts. Social proof begets more social proof.
  • Reach out to 3 other small reality podcasts for cross-promotion. Most will say yes — everyone's trying to grow.
  • Submit your podcast to directories that RSS.com may not auto-submit to: Podchaser, Listen Notes, Goodpods.
  • Optimize your episode titles for SEO: format = '[Show Name] S[Season]E[Episode]: [What Happened] – Full Recap & Hot Takes'

Affiliate Links — Start Immediately

Phase 2: Months 4–6 (1K–3K Downloads/Month)

  • Programmatic ads – Turn on ads through your RSS host (RSS.com has a built-in ad marketplace). Revenue starts at ~$15–25 per 1,000 downloads (CPM). Small but passive.

  • Merch – Teespring or Printful (no upfront cost). Start with 2–3 items: a tote bag, a mug, a t-shirt. Quotes from your show or the shows you cover.
  • Network pitches – At 5K+ downloads, you're attractive to podcast networks (Wondery, iHeart, Exactly Right). They handle sponsorships and take 30–50% revenue share. Worth it for the reach and resources.
  • Cast member partnerships – Minor cast members are often looking for content opportunities and sponsorships. Reach out via Instagram DM. A former cast member appearance can double your download numbers overnight.

Section 7: Growth & Community Strategy

Platform-by-Platform Social Media Plan

Facebook Groups (Your Home Base)

  • Post every episode as a 'discussion starter' — not just a link dump. Example: 'Episode 12 is out! What did YOU think of that season finale bombshell? 💣 Drop your take in the comments, and listen to ours here: [link]'
  • Run a weekly poll tied to current episodes. Top-voted answer becomes content in your next episode.
  • Create a pinned post in each group pointing to your podcast.
  • Never spam. One episode post per group per week, plus organic discussion engagement.


TikTok + Instagram Reels

  • These are your growth channels. One viral clip can add thousands of followers overnight.
  • Best performing clip formats: Your most extreme hot take. A moment where you react visibly. A clip where you call out a cast member. Anything controversial.
  • Post 4–6 clips per episode. Don't repost the same clip to both platforms — TikTok penalizes content with Instagram watermarks.
  • Trend-jack: When a show moment goes viral on Twitter/X, create a clip of your reaction within 24 hours.


YouTube

  • Upload the full episode as a 'watch the full podcast' video. Low extra effort since Riverside.fm records video automatically.
  • Create a '90-second recap' YouTube Short for every episode — different from your TikToks.
  • YouTube has the most discoverable long-form content of any platform. A well-titled video can surface years after you upload it.


Twitter/X

  • Live-tweet during episode airings. This is where reality TV Twitter lives.
  • Use the official show hashtag + your podcast handle. Cast members sometimes retweet fan accounts — and podcasters are seen as media.
  • 'Thoughts while watching' threads perform very well. You can turn these into episode topics.


Finding and Working With a Co-Host

The top reality TV podcasts almost all have co-hosts. Here's why: chemistry creates loyalty. Listeners come back for the relationship between hosts as much as the content.


  • Where to find one – Your Facebook groups. Look for members who write long, insightful, funny comments. They already have opinions. DM them: 'I'm launching a podcast and you seem like you'd be amazing on it — want to do a test episode?'
  • What to look for – Personality contrast (serious analyst + chaos gremlin works perfectly). Reliability. Someone who won't flake on recording days. Complementary knowledge (you know Bravo, they know 90 Day).
  • Test before committing – Record one episode together. How does your chemistry feel? Is editing it painful? Did you have fun? Trust your gut.
  • The agreement – Be clear from the start: this is a business. Agree on: time commitment, who does what tasks, how revenue is split, what happens if one person wants to leave.


Guest Booking Pipeline

  • Superfans from your groups (Month 1–2): Warmest leads, most reliable, will promote the episode.
  • Other reality TV podcasters (Month 2–4): Cross-promos. Guest on their show, they guest on yours.
  • Reality TV bloggers / fan account operators (Month 3–6): They have audiences that overlap perfectly with yours.
  • Minor cast members (Month 4–9): Reach via Instagram DM. Be professional, complimentary, specific about your audience size and show.
  • Production adjacent (Month 9+): Producers, crew, network reps. These unlock exclusive content.


Building a Community Beyond the Groups

Consider creating a Patreon-exclusive Discord server at your $10+ tier. Discord allows real-time discussion during episode watch parties and is stickier than Facebook for superfans. It also gives you a direct line to your most passionate and paying supporters.



Section 8: Legal & Operational Essentials

Copyright & Fair Use

Reality TV podcasting sits in a legal gray area — but it's well-traveled ground. Here's how to stay safe:


  • Short audio/video clips (10–20 seconds) with original commentary = generally fair use (transformative criticism).
  • Avoid playing the 'heart' of an episode — iconic scenes that are the primary value of the content.
  • Never play full episodes or long unedited segments.
  • Credit your sources when quoting from other podcasts, articles, or social media.
  • For music: ALL background music must be licensed. Epidemic Sound or Artlist subscriptions cover you for podcast use. Do not use commercial music without a license.
  • Many successful reality podcasters describe show scenes rather than playing audio — this is the safest approach.


Business Structure

Once you hit $500/month: register as a sole proprietorship or LLC (LLC recommended for liability protection). Most podcasters use their state's online business registration portal.


Taxes & Bookkeeping

  • Track all expenses from Day 1 – Microphone, software subscriptions, hosting fees, design costs are all deductible as business expenses.
  • Use Wave – Free accounting software designed for small businesses and freelancers. Connects to your bank.
  • Set aside 25–30% – Of all Patreon and podcast income for taxes. Reality TV podcast income is self-employment income — you'll owe both income and self-employment tax.
  • Home studio deduction – If you record in a dedicated space at home, part of your rent/mortgage may be deductible. Consult a tax professional when this becomes significant.


Privacy & Social Media

  • Your Facebook groups are community assets. Keep the podcast and the groups separate enough that the groups don't feel like ads. Authenticity is everything.
  • Be careful about discussing cast members' personal lives beyond what's aired. Stick to what's public.
  • Don't give legal, medical, or financial advice on air — even jokingly in ways that could be taken seriously.

Gap Analysis: Where You Can Win

Based on current competition, here are underserved angles in the Bravo/90 Day space:

  • The superfan perspective – Not a journalist. Not a former cast member. A passionate community member who knows what fans actually think and feel. This is authentically YOU.
  • Facebook group admin as host – No major podcast hosts their show primarily through their existing FB community. This is your differentiator — you ARE the community.
  • Multi-show coverage with depth – Most shows pick a lane (all Bravo OR all 90 Day). Covering both with depth, plus using your multi-group reach for promotion, is a competitive edge.
  • Local/regional angle – If you're in a specific city or region, connect the national show drama to your local reality. This micro-community feels very personal to listeners.
