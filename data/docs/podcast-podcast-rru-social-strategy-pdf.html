 New images, stills, or both?
 You asked this directly, so here is the direct answer: stills from the episodes we have already made, with
 new graphics composited on top in-house. Not new AI generation, and — importantly — not network
 footage.

 Three reasons, in order of how much they matter.
  • New generation now costs money. The Magnific unlimited allowance is exhausted, so every fresh image
    is a real charge. A frame grabbed out of an episode you already exported costs nothing and takes under a
    second.
  • The episodes are original work you own outright. This is the part that matters most in 2026. Meta
    formalised its original-content rules in March: re-uploading or lightly editing someone else's video now
    suppresses distribution across the entire Page, not just the offending post, and costs monetisation
    eligibility. Bravo and TLC are exactly the kind of rights holders with access to Meta Rights Manager, which
    matches on fingerprint and does not evaluate fair use. Your animated comic frames sit completely outside
    all of that.

 Stories
 Two a day, every day, drawn from the same asset pool — every card is rendered in a 9:16 version
 automatically. Stories are the one surface with a real fatigue signal: keep the exit rate under 5–10%. Two a
 day is the ceiling.


     The mismatch worth knowing about
     Friday is the weakest measured day on Instagram, and your highest-value social moment — air night — lands
     four days before the episode drops. The fix is not to move publish day. It is to treat air night as reaction content
     that funnels toward Friday, which is exactly what slots 1 and 2 do. Watch What Crappens runs a
     Monday-evening live for the same reason.

 Hashtags — the rules changed
 Instagram introduced a hard cap of five hashtags in December 2025. Over five and publishing is blocked or
 the excess is stripped. The cap counts whether the tags sit in the caption or the first comment, so placement is
 now a style choice rather than a tactic. Metricool's 24M-post study found posts using hashtags get 31.7%
 fewer views — that is correlation rather than causation, but it does kill the old "hashtags for reach" argument
 outright.

 Use three to five, show-level and franchise-level only. #90DayFiance #TheLastResort #RealityTVRecap
 works. #podcast #love #instagood is exactly what the cap was designed to suppress. Facebook hashtags are
 inert — use none.

 The replacement for hashtag reach is keywords in the caption. Google now indexes Instagram posts, and
 Instagram's own guidance shifted to recommending relevant keywords in captions and profiles. Write "The
 Last Resort castle intimacy session" as words, not as tags.

 Format priority
  • Instagram: Reels get 4× the interactions of a single image. Carousels get 9× the saves. Single images
    collapsed in 2026 — reach down 22%, engagement down 46% year-over-year. Lead with video and
    carousels; use stills as connective tissue.
  • Facebook: Reels are now the top organic format, overtaking everything. Text-only status posts collapsed
    from best format in 2025 to worst non-link format in 2026 (0.20% → 0.08%). Always attach an image or a
    video.

 Writing the captions
  • Instagram: the hook must land inside 125 characters, because that is all most people see. The build
    checks this on every post and warns you if a first line runs long.
  • Facebook: 40–80 characters of lead. Ask the question, then stop — do not argue your own position in the
    post. Save it for the comments, where the replies are worth ranking points.
  • Groups: every group gets a different opening line, always. Identical text fired into four groups at once is the
    textbook spam pattern, and the resulting mutes score against the Page, not just the post. The build fails if
    any two posts share text.
  • Clips: open on the most provocative sentence, never on setup. End on the hook, not the resolution —
    curiosity drives comments and comments drive reach. Avoid any clip that needs two sentences of context
    before it makes sense; that is the single most common failure mode for recap clips.

 A note on episode length
 A 15-minute episode yields roughly five to eight usable clips, which is the low end of the 5–10 that podcast-clip
 research recommends. That is not a reason to make longer episodes — it is a reason to write each episode
 with at least one extractable 45-second beat per section. It is a scripting instruction, not a post-production one.

 Every free option, explored
 You asked for all of them, so here they are with the actual 2026 limits rather than the marketing pages. Three
 findings reshape the plan before any tool comparison matters.


    1. Facebook Groups automation is dead and is not coming back
    Meta removed the Groups API from every API version on 22 April 2024. No tool — free or paid, Zapier, Make,
    Buffer, Hootsuite, dlvr.it — can auto-publish to a group any more. What survives is Facebook's own in-group
    scheduler for admins, which is free, fully automatic, and better than every third-party workaround. This kills the
    "Zap #2 → post to Facebook groups" item in CLAUDE.md.



    2. The X free API tier is gone
    As of 6 February 2026, X discontinued free API access for new developers. Posting now costs $0.015 per post
    — and $0.20 if the post contains a URL. For a podcast whose job is posting episode links, that is a
    non-starter. Combined with this genre's audience living on Facebook, skipping X is the right call.



    3. You could post to your own Page and Instagram via API for free
    Meta grants Standard Access to every app automatically, with no App Review, for accounts you have a role on.
    Since you own the app, the Page and the Instagram account, that is enough — no review, no business
    verification, no fee. This is the upgrade path if manual pasting ever gets old. It is not needed today.

 What the audit enforces
  • Every card renders at its exact target dimensions, or the build fails.
  • No season number, episode number or episode title reaches any image — exit code 1 if it does.
  • No two posts in the week carry identical text, and no two fire at the same minute on the same surface.
  • Every Instagram first line is measured against the 125-character truncation point and flagged if it overruns.
  • Hashtag counts are checked against Instagram's five-tag cap.
  • Every reel is measured against the 45–60 second window and flagged if it falls outside.
  • Every referenced asset is confirmed to exist before the queue lists it.

 Verified, not assumed
 The system was run against the real S3E4 master. It produced 21 cards and 18 scheduled posts with zero
 errors and zero warnings, and three 45–60 second reels at 1080×1920, 24fps, AAC 48kHz — frame counts
 exact, audio decoding clean end to end, and no visible seam at any of the segment joins. The evergreen
 guard was tested negatively too: putting "Season 3 · Episode 4" back into a card correctly failed the build.

 Risk — copyright and Meta's originality rules
 Not legal advice. This is practical risk guidance; talk to an IP attorney before building a business on any of it.

 What changed in 2026
 Two things, and they point the same direction. First, Meta's original content rules (March 2026) explicitly
 classify reaction video without substantive commentary, cosmetic-only edits, and stitched third-party clips as
 unoriginal — and the penalty is account-wide: reduced reach on everything the Page posts, loss of
 monetisation, removal from recommendations. Second, in Cordova v. Huneault (February 2026) a California
 court held that ripping YouTube video can violate the DMCA's anti-circumvention provision — a separate
 violation that fair use does not cure.
