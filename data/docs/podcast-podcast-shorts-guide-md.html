YouTube Shorts — how they work and how we'll make them

Researched July 2026. Applies to every episode, not just EP-02.

Part 1 — The specs

Aspect ratio9:16 vertical
Resolution1080 × 1920
Max length3 minutes (raised from 60s in late 2024)
Best length15–30 seconds — highest completion rate, strongest algorithm signal
FormatMP4, H.264
Frame rate30 or 60 fps
Thumbnail1280 × 720

Anything vertical and under 3 minutes is automatically filed as a Short — you don't tick a box.

Length is the decision that matters. You can post 3 minutes. You shouldn't. Completion rate is the metric Shorts lives on, and a 25-second clip watched to the end beats a 90-second clip abandoned at 40. Cut ruthlessly.

Keep text clear of the edges. YouTube overlays its own UI along the bottom and right — the title, channel name, like and comment buttons. Captions or graphics placed there get covered.

Part 2 — The problem with our episode

Our episode is 1920 × 1080 horizontal. A Short is 1080 × 1920 vertical. Those are opposite shapes, so something has to give. Three ways to handle it:

Crop to the middle. Take a tall slice out of the centre and throw away the sides. Works when Ruby is centred in her booth. Fails badly on the wide castle shots — you'd lose most of the picture. Also means blowing a 608-pixel-wide slice up to 1080, which softens the image.

Blurred background. Keep the whole frame, shrink it to fit the width, fill the space above and below with a blurred copy of itself. Nothing is lost, but it's the generic look every recap channel uses and it wastes half the screen on mush.

Brand frame — my recommendation. Keep the whole 16:9 frame intact in the middle. Fill above and below with our deep purple, the neon logo at the top and large captions at the bottom. Nothing is cropped, nothing is soft, and it looks like our show rather than a repurposed clip. The comic artwork survives whole, which is the entire point of the visual style.

Part 3 — What makes a good Short

The first second decides everything. Shorts autoplay in a feed where the thumb is already moving. Open on the most provocative sentence in the clip — never on setup, context or "so as I was saying".

One idea per Short. A complete thought with a beginning and an end. Not a random 30 seconds lifted from the middle.

Captions always. Even though Shorts play with sound on, captions raise completion. We already have accurate word-level timing, so this is free.

End on the hook, not the resolution. Curiosity drives comments, and comments drive reach.

Part 5 — How we'll actually make them

Once the episode is exported, I can generate these automatically. Give me a start and end timecode and I'll produce a finished 1080 × 1920 MP4 with:

  • the full 16:9 frame centred, nothing cropped
  • deep purple brand background
  • the neon logo at the top
  • burned-in captions pulled from the existing SRT — the timing is already frame-accurate, so they'll match her speech exactly
  • audio taken straight from the export, music and effects intact

Then you review, and I write the title, description and hashtags from the transcript.

Note: captions get burned in for Shorts, unlike the main episode. There's no caption toggle in the Shorts player, so the text has to be part of the picture.

Part 6 — Publishing

  • Link each Short to the full episode. YouTube has a "Related video" field on Shorts — always fill it in. That's the whole point of making them.
  • Space them out. One a day beats five at once.
  • Post the cold open first, on the same day the episode goes public.
  • Vertical thumbnails don't show in the Shorts feed, but do appear on your channel page — worth setting for the ones you expect to do well.
