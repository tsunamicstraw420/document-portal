Researched July 25 2026, for Reality Recaps Unfiltered.

First: the big picture (so the steps make sense)

Spotify for Creators is your podcast HOST. That's the key thing. When you upload an episode there, two things happen:

  1. It goes live on Spotify itself.
  2. Spotify generates your RSS feed, and that feed is what carries your show to Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music, iHeartRadio and every other podcast app. You upload once; the RSS pushes it everywhere. You do not upload separately to Apple/Amazon — you just submit your RSS link to them one time, and after that they pull each new episode automatically.

YouTube is the exception — it does not accept RSS feeds. YouTube is always a separate, manual upload (which you already do). So your real workflow is two uploads per episode: one to Spotify for Creators, one to YouTube. Everything else flows from the Spotify RSS.

Your show is hosted on Spotify/Anchor, and Spotify hides the owner email from your public RSS feed for privacy. Step 1 — Apple Podcasts (what actually worked for EP-02)

  1. Apple Podcasts Connect (podcastsconnect.apple.com), signed in with your normal Apple ID → ＋ → Add a show with an RSS feed URL → paste your RSS URL → Add. The show is created as Draft (yellow dot).
  2. Open the show → click Publish (top-right). The status flips Draft → Available (green dot) = live on Apple Podcasts. That's the whole thing — you did this July 25.
  3. The verification email never arrives (Spotify hides the owner email, above) — but you don't need it. Publishing from your own account is enough.

Fallback — only if Publish is blocked demanding ownership verification:

  • Apple Podcasts Connect → bottom of homepage → More Options → Claim Existing Show → paste your Apple show URL (…/id6794720876) → generate Claim Token (7-day).
  • Spotify → Settings → Availability → paste it into the "Apple Podcasts claim code" field → Save. Apple matches it within ~24h.
  • If Claim returns "This show already exists in your account," that means it's already yours — just Publish.

Step 2 — Amazon Music / Audible

  • Go to music.amazon.com/podcasters, sign in with your Amazon account → Add your podcast → paste the RSS URL.
  • Amazon also verifies by email to the feed owner, so it may hit the same hidden-email wall. If it does: in Spotify → Settings, temporarily make the owner email visible in the feed just long enough to receive Amazon's code, then hide it again.

Step 3 — iHeartRadio

  • Go to iheart.com/podcast/submit, sign in / create a free account → paste the RSS URL → submit. Manual review, a few days.

⚠️ Note: all three need YOU signed in

Apple, Amazon, and iHeart each require logging into your account (and Apple's token has to be pasted between two of your logged-in accounts). This part can't be automated on your behalf — it's a ~15-minute hands-on job. Everything above is the exact click-path so it's fast.

What to expect

  • You do this once. After the show is approved on each, every future episode appears automatically (usually within a few hours, up to 24h).
  • Video is Spotify-only. On Apple/Amazon/iHeart your episodes are audio-only — the RSS carries the audio track. That's fine and expected.
  • Some apps scrape public RSS and may list you without submission — normal.
  • Keep the RSS URL somewhere handy (it's in _SHARED reference or note it here): once submitted you rarely need it again.

Uploading an episode — step by step

On the web dashboard (creators.spotify.com):

  1. Click New episode (top-right).
  2. Click Select a file and choose your episode file.
  3. While it uploads, fill in the Details page:
  • Title — use the same episode title as YouTube.
  • Description — paste the same show-notes/description. Spotify supports basic formatting and clickable timestamps for chapters.
  • Content checks — answer the prompts (explicit content, etc.).
  1. Under Additional details: add the season and episode number and episode artwork if you want a per-episode thumbnail.
  2. Publish date:
  • Now — goes live immediately.
  • Schedule — pick a future date/time (shown in your local time).
  1. Publish.

Supported files: audio = MP3, M4A, WAV. Video = MP4 or MOV, 16:9. No hard size limit, but big files process slowly.

To change a scheduled episode later: click the episode → 3-dot menu → Revert to draft.

Multi-platform posting: help or hurt?

It helps. There is no meaningful downside. The 2026 consensus is unanimous: list your show everywhere. Being on Spotify, Apple, Amazon, iHeart and YouTube doesn't split or cannibalize you — it lets each new listener find you on whatever app they already use. There's no "duplicate content" penalty for podcasts across platforms; they're different ecosystems.

What each platform is actually best at, so you can lean in:

  • YouTube — discovery. The algorithm + Shorts + search bring in brand-new viewers who've never heard of you. This is your growth engine.
  • Spotify — audience insight + habit. Strong follower/notification mechanics and the best creator analytics; people subscribe and come back.
  • Apple Podcasts — the loyal core. Serious podcast listeners live here; good for retention and reviews.

The only "cost" of being everywhere is the one-time RSS submission and the separate YouTube upload — both of which are already in your workflow. So: post to all of them. For a channel playing catch-up, wider availability only helps.

One nuance for a new channel: don't spread your effort thin trying to game five algorithms. Put your creative energy into YouTube (where discovery happens) and let RSS quietly handle the rest. Presence everywhere, focus on one.

Your per-episode checklist (once you're rolling)

  1. Export the episode from Resolve twice: EP-XX_YOUTUBE.mp4 (captions burned) and EP-XX_SPOTIFY.mp4 (clean).
  2. YouTube: upload the YOUTUBE file (unlisted → public Friday) + the SRT + thumbnail + end screen. (you have guides for all of this)
  3. Spotify for Creators: New episode → upload the SPOTIFY (clean) file → paste title + description → set Season/Episode → Publish or schedule for Friday.
  4. That's it — Apple/Amazon/iHeart pull it from RSS automatically.
  5. Cut the Shorts from the YOUTUBE export; post them across the week. Post the social stills (Facebook groups + Instagram).
