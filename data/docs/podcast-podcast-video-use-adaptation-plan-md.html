video-use → Reality Recaps: Implementation Plan

Repo: https://github.com/browser-use/video-use (MIT, 17.1k stars, 18 commits, Aug 2026) Written: August 3, 2026 Scope agreed with Eddie: auto-tighten Ruby's narration + adopt the self-eval loop. Free Whisper, not paid ElevenLabs. Runs on the Mac via .command launchers.

1. What the repo actually is

A Claude Code skill — a SKILL.md of instructions plus six Python helpers that wrap ffmpeg. There is no application, no server, no model. The whole idea is one sentence:

The LLM never watches the video. It reads a word-level transcript, decides the cuts as text, and ffmpeg executes them.

That is precisely what we already do. Which is the headline finding of this research: we independently built about 70% of this repo. The plan below is therefore not "adopt video-use" — it's "take the three things they have that we don't, and skip the rest."

Defensible score: 9–10 of 12 met, 1 genuinely N/A. Not the "11 of 12, one-character fix" it looks like at first glance. Two of the three gaps are real work rather than a constant change:

  • Rule 7 is the one worth acting on. Codifying the padding into propose_cuts.py — applied to both edges, as a named constant — is a genuine improvement over a human remembering a docstring. Phase 3 does this.
  • Rule 2's violation in trim_audio.py is knowingly acceptable. Per-segment extract + -c copy concat exists to avoid double-encoding video. trim_audio.py touches audio only and encodes once to lossless PCM. Not worth refactoring; worth knowing it's there so nobody cites it as compliant later.

This is what makes a straight install wrong for us.

Consequence: their multi-take selection engine — the editor sub-agent brief, the "pick the best HOOK across 8 clips" logic — has no job here. Ruby does one read. grade.py has no job either. render.py's overlay compositing has no job, because Resolve owns the composite.

What does transfer is everything upstream of the composite: transcript → phrase view → cut decisions → clean audio spine → verification.

3. The three things we're taking

3.1 Automatic cut proposal (the actual win)

We already tighten Ruby's narration. Here is the current EP-04 code, verbatim:

# (start, end, why) — ORIGINAL timeline seconds
CUTS = [
    ( 92.82,  94.15, "M2  false start: 'because it's the position' before ..."),
    (187.15, 188.60, "M5  repeated 'one small ...' before 'one small door back in'"),
    (210.50, 212.10, "M7  'turned the room,' before 'turned to the room'"),
    ...
]

Seven cuts, hand-found by reading the raw transcript, hand-typed with hand-measured timestamps. That list is the manual labour video-use eliminates.

The architecture around it is already right and stays: source of truth is the original take, cuts are expressed on the original timeline, re-running rebuilds CLEAN from scratch with no compounding or generation loss. We are not replacing trim_audio.py. We are auto-generating its CUTS list.

New tool: propose_cuts.py

narration.m4a
  → chunk_transcribe.py            (already exists, free, cached)
  → pack_transcripts.py            (ported from video-use)
  → narration_packed.md            (phrase-level, ~12KB for a 15-min read)
  → I read it and propose cuts     (retakes, false starts, "sorry", dead air)
  → cuts_proposed.json             (start, end, reason, confidence)
  → you approve/reject each one
  → written into trim_audio.py's CUTS list
  → existing trim_audio.py runs unchanged

The step that video-use gets right and we should copy exactly: phrase-level packing with silence-gap breaks. Their format —

[092.40-094.15] Because it's the position. Sorry.
[094.30-098.72] It's because in any other position...

— makes a retake visually obvious in a way raw word JSON does not. A 15-minute read packs to roughly 12KB, which I can hold entirely in context and reason over in one pass. That's what turns hand-hunting into a single review.

Detection heuristics I'll use on the packed view (all derived at read time, not precomputed — their design principle, and it's the right one):

  • Consecutive phrases with high word overlap → retake
  • Phrase ending in "sorry", "let me", "again", "one more time" → abandoned take
  • Silence gaps ≥ 1.2s mid-paragraph → dead air
  • Repeated sentence openers → false start

Every proposal comes with the reason string, and the reason lands in trim_audio.py as a comment, exactly as the hand-written ones do now. Nothing gets cut without you seeing it first.

Honest expectation: this won't be perfect. It will over-propose. The value isn't autonomous cutting — it's turning "read 15 minutes of transcript hunting for flubs" into "review 10 flagged candidates." I'd guess it catches most real flubs and flags 2–4 false positives per episode.

3.2 timeline_view.py — the visual drill-down

This is the single best piece of code in the repo and we have no equivalent.

Given a video and a time range, it produces one PNG: a filmstrip of N frames across the top, an audio waveform ribbon below, word labels from the transcript overlaid on the waveform, and silence gaps shaded. Roughly 390 lines of PIL + numpy + ffmpeg. No API, no cost, no dependencies we don't have.

Why it matters for us specifically: I currently cannot see our episodes. When you say a cut looks wrong, I'm reasoning from filenames and JSON. This gives me an actual look at any moment of any file — a cut boundary, a shot that might be miscast, a caption that might be colliding with an overlay.

Direct application to problems we've already hit:

  • The miscast clip. SC-25_paola_russ_years is actually Guillermo — caught by you, by eye, after it shipped into a worklist. A filmstrip of every generated clip is how that gets caught before assembly.
  • Sliver shots. The ≥4s minimum is currently enforced by arithmetic. A filmstrip makes a flash cut visible.
  • Caption collisions. Whether the karaoke line is sitting under an overlay is a thing you look at, not a thing you compute.

Port it near-verbatim. Only change: our word JSON key names (§5).

3.3 The self-eval loop

Their step 7, and it directly implements the rule you already gave me — verify before handing back, never make Eddie do QA.

After any render, before you see it:

  1. timeline_view at every cut boundary, ±1.5s window
  2. Check each image for: visual jump/flash, waveform spike at the seam (a pop that slipped past the fade), caption hidden behind an overlay, overlay showing the wrong frames
  3. Sample first 2s, last 2s, 2–3 midpoints — grade consistency, caption readability
  4. ffprobe the output, confirm duration matches what the timeline predicted
  5. Fail → fix → re-render → re-check. Hard cap 3 passes, then surface what's left rather than looping

The 3-pass cap matters. Without it an agent will grind indefinitely on something it can't fix.

For us this runs on the Resolve export, not on an ffmpeg render — the check is identical either way, it just reads whatever MP4 came out.

This also catches a specific class of bug we've been bitten by: the sample-rate mislabel that produced the deep/slow "drunk" audio on EP-04 (48k body stream-copied onto a 44.1k logo clip). A duration check plus a decoded-length comparison would have caught it automatically instead of you hearing it. Note: that incident is recorded in my working notes, not in any file in this repo — so it's not something a reader can verify here.

5. The Whisper swap (and a schema bug we should fix while we're here)

video-use expects ElevenLabs Scribe JSON. We produce faster-whisper JSON. The gap is small:

ScribeOurs (chunk_transcribe.py)
words[].textwords[].word
words[].type = "word" / "spacing" / "audio_event"(absent)
words[].speaker_id(absent)
silence carried by "spacing" entries(absent — but see below)

The adapter is ~15 lines: map word→text and inject type: "word".

Two things I checked rather than assumed:

  • pack_transcripts.py reads gaps from spacing entries, which we don't have — but it has a prev_end fallback path that computes the gap from the previous word's end time. Our data works through that path unmodified. I verified against LastResort_03_08.words.json: word timings are contiguous during speech (2.21→2.21, 2.67→2.67) and only open up during real silence. Gap detection works correctly.
  • render.py's _words_in_range() filters on w.get("type") != "word" and would silently drop every word from our JSON. The type injection is not optional.

What we lose: speaker diarization and (laughter) / (applause) audio-event tags. Neither applies to a solo teleprompter read. If fan call-ins or guest interviews arrive as planned, diarization becomes worth paying for — revisit then, not now.

The schema bug worth fixing

While auditing I found two incompatible word-JSON schemas already in the repo. Full census:

SchemaCountWhere
{i, word, start, end}9EP05, EP06, EP07, EP08 lipsync/; mp3/ ×5 (02B, 05, 06, 07, 08); vps-pipeline/examples/
{w, s, e}2EP04 only — LastResort_03_04.words.json + its _CLEAN

The wrappers differ too: {duration, n_words, words, segments} vs {audio, duration_sec, model, language, text, words, segments}. So a normalizer that only rewrites word keys leaves a second incompatibility in place — fix both.

chunk_transcribe.py writes the majority schema. EP04 is the outlier, and it's self-consistent: trim_audio.py reads and writes {w,s,e}, and burn_captions.py reads {w,s,e} too. Nothing is broken today — the risk is that any new script pointed at EP04 fails, or silently reads nothing.

Recommendation: standardize on {i, word, start, end} (9:2 majority, and what our transcriber emits).

⚠️ This is a code change, not a data migration. Both EP04 consumers are hard-coded to {w,s,e}:

  • trim_audio.py — reads d["words"][i]["w"/"s"/"e"], writes the same shape
  • burn_captions.py — reads w["w"], wd["s"], ln[-1]["e"] (lines 80, 86, 103–111, 126–127)

Converting the JSON without editing both scripts produces a KeyError. Phase 0 is therefore 2 script edits + 2 JSON conversions, and the gate has to be "EP-04's caption build still produces identical output" — reached by changing all four files together, not by hoping the scripts don't notice.

6. The 12ms vs 30ms fade question

trim_audio.py uses FADE = 0.012. video-use mandates 30ms and calls it non-negotiable.

They're probably right, and here's the reasoning rather than deference: at 48kHz, 12ms is ~576 samples. That's enough to kill a hard discontinuity but leaves audible transient energy if the waveform is mid-cycle at a high amplitude — which is exactly what happens cutting between two takes of the same voice at similar loudness. 30ms (~1440 samples) covers a full cycle of anything above ~33Hz.

But it costs ramp time. trim_audio.py builds 8 keep-segments and fades both edges of each, so EP-04 actually carries 16 fades: 480ms of ramped audio at 30ms versus 192ms at 12ms. On a voice track with tight cuts that's a real, if small, softening.

Recommendation: bump to 30ms and A/B it on EP-04, which already has 7 known cuts. It's a one-line change and a five-minute listen. If the pops were never audible at 12ms, keep 12ms — I'd rather have the evidence than the rule. This is a taste call you should make with your ears, not one I should make from a README.

7. Build order

Each phase ships something usable. Nothing after Phase 0 blocks the current pipeline.

Phase 0 — Normalize (do first, ~1 hr)

Four files change together, or nothing works:

  • normalize_words.py — convert EP04's 2 JSONs to {i,word,start,end} + the standard wrapper, keep .bak
  • Edit trim_audio.py — read and write the new keys
  • Edit burn_captions.py — read the new keys (lines 80, 86, 103–111, 126–127)
  • Gate: rebuild EP-04's .ass and .srt and diff against the current ones. Must be identical. If they differ, revert from .bak and stop.

Phase 1 — Sight (highest value, lowest risk, ~2 hrs)

  • Port timeline_view.py to vps-pipeline/timeline_view.py
  • Swap Scribe key names for ours
  • Add make_timeline_view.command (prompts for file + time range)
  • Gate: produces a readable filmstrip+waveform PNG for a known EP-04 cut boundary

Nothing else depends on this, and it immediately gives me eyes on the material. Ship it first.

Phase 2 — Packed transcript (~1 hr)

  • Port pack_transcripts.py, single-file mode
  • Wire to chunk_transcribe.py output
  • Gate: EP-04's narration packs to a phrase-level MD under ~15KB, and all 7 known hand-found flubs are visible in it by eye

That gate is the honest test of the whole idea. If the flubs aren't obvious in the packed view, auto-proposal won't work either and we should stop there.

Phase 3 — Cut proposal (~3 hrs)

  • propose_cuts.py: read packed MD → cuts_proposed.json
  • Approval step — nothing auto-applies
  • Emit the approved list in trim_audio.py CUTS format, comments and all
  • Gate: run blind against EP-04's original narration. Score against the 7 known cuts: how many found, how many missed, how many false positives. Report the numbers before we trust it.

Phase 4 — Self-eval (~2 hrs)

  • self_eval.py: read a rendered MP4 + the cut list → filmstrips at every boundary + ffprobe duration check + decoded-length audio check
  • 3-pass cap
  • Gate: run against a known-good export (clean report) and a deliberately broken one (caught)

Phase 5 — Launchers (~1 hr)

  • 3_tighten_narration.command
  • 4_self_eval.command
  • Both chmod +x, both strip com.apple.quarantine on output

Total: roughly 10 hours of build. Phases 1 and 2 alone (3 hrs) deliver most of the value — and neither depends on Phase 0, so if the normalization turns out messier than expected we can ship sight and packing first and come back to it.

9. The honest summary

video-use is a well-built confirmation that our approach was right. We already follow 9–10 of their 12 production rules, and we found the cut-padding rule independently through the exact failure they describe. Where we fall short it's mostly that a rule lives in a docstring rather than in code — which the new tools fix as a side effect.

The genuinely new things are three:

  1. timeline_view.py — gives me eyes. Biggest single upgrade, ~2 hours, no dependencies, immediately useful. Would have caught the miscast Guillermo clip.
  2. Phrase-level packed transcript — turns flub-hunting from manual reading into a reviewable list.
  3. The self-eval loop with a 3-pass cap — formalizes "verify before handing back" into something that runs automatically.

Everything else in the repo is solving a problem we don't have — picking between camera takes, and color grading real footage.

If we only do one thing: Phase 1. It's the cheapest and it's the one that changes what I can do for you.
