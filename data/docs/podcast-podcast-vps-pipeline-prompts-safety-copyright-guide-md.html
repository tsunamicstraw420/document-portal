Copyright, Fair Use & Legal Guidance for B-Roll

This is one of the most important documents in the project. Please read it fully before implementing any feature that pulls or uses footage from actual TV episodes.

Core Principle

Default to safe, legal visuals. Stock footage + generative AI B-roll is almost always the better, faster, and lower-risk choice for a monetized reality TV recap podcast/YouTube channel.

Using actual clips from copyrighted TV shows (even short 10–20s segments) carries real legal and platform risk, even if you believe it qualifies as fair use.

Why Actual Episode Clips Are Risky

FactorWhy It Weighs Against You (in most cases)
Commercial useYour podcast/YouTube is monetized
Creative natureReality TV footage is highly creative/protected work
Amount usedEven 10–20 seconds can be substantial if it's the "heart" of a scene
Effect on marketClips can substitute for watching the original show
Transformative valuePurely illustrative B-roll is often not transformative enough

YouTube’s Content ID system is automated and aggressive. Copyright holders (studios, networks) routinely issue claims. Three strikes = channel termination. Even successful fair use defenses cost time and legal fees.

Tools like Playphrase.me, find-scene.com, and Twelve Labs make finding clips easy — they do NOT grant you the right to use them commercially.

Project Policy (Strictly Enforced in Code)

  1. Stock + Generative B-roll is the recommended and default path.
  2. Actual episode clip extraction is an advanced, opt-in, disabled-by-default feature.
  • Controlled by ALLOW_ACTUAL_EPISODE_CLIPS=false in .env
  • Even when enabled, the code will show prominent warnings and require explicit confirmation.
  1. If you choose to use actual clips anyway:
  • Keep them very short (< 10–15 seconds ideal)
  • Always layer substantial original value: your voiceover/analysis, text callouts, zooms/pans (Ken Burns), color grading, picture-in-picture of you reacting, memes, etc.
  • Never use a clip that could reasonably serve as a substitute for watching the original episode
  • Document your fair use reasoning for each clip (we can add a simple logging feature later)
  • Consult an intellectual property attorney familiar with YouTube/content creator cases in your jurisdiction

How This Project Will Handle It (Planned Implementation)

  • All B-roll suggestion prompts will default to stock/generative descriptions
  • The LLM prompt will include instructions like: “Prefer generic dramatic reality TV style scenes over specific show footage unless explicitly requested”
  • Actual clip module will be in a separate file (clip_extractor.py) and behind the feature flag
  • When the risky path is used, the final video metadata or a sidecar JSON will log the source and duration for your records
  • We will include example prompts that produce excellent stylized B-roll without needing the original footage

Final Recommendation

For a sustainable, growing channel:

  • Start with stock + generative B-roll only
  • Focus your energy on great commentary, personality, editing rhythm, and thumbnails
  • Many successful recap channels thrive with zero or minimal direct show footage

If your content style truly requires occasional actual clips for criticism/analysis, treat that as a small percentage of B-roll and apply heavy transformation + legal review.

When in doubt, leave it out.
