OmniRogue sells a waiting line, not unlimited video

Research report, 3 November 2026. Subject: omnirogue.com and its $399 Lifetime deal.

The short answer

OmniRogue (omnirogue.com) is run by OmniRogue Inc., a Florida company whose only listed officer is Samuel S. Blake. It does not deliver unlimited AI video for $399.

It delivers one included render at a time, at 720p and 10 seconds or less. The wait between renders is set by the company and not published. Buyers say it is 15 to 60 minutes. Anything bigger, longer, sharper or faster costs paid credits.

Your instinct is half right. True unlimited at $399 is impossible, and the numbers below show it. But the company is not delivering true unlimited, so running out of money is not the only way this ends. Its terms let it shrink the plan at any time, with no refund.

Unknown: how many people bought, and what OmniRogue really pays its suppliers.

Seven levers that make $399 survivable

1. A queue, not a meter

The fair-use page says included renders run one at a time across your account. It also says very heavy use "may take longer to start" (Fair use). Proven The wait shows as a countdown. Exact limits need not be published, per the Terms of Service. Buyers report waits of 15, 25, 30 and 60 minutes (Trustpilot). Reported by buyers

2. A small included lane

The company's live data file shows what Lifetime includes with no credits. Seedance 2.5 is up to 720p and 10 seconds. Veo 3.1 is the "fast" tier only, at 720p and 8 seconds. Kling 3 is the "standard" tier only, at 720p and 10 seconds (live policy data). Proven, read 2026-11-03 Higher resolutions and longer clips cost credits (Mirage page). The Seedance page still advertises 30-second clips and 4K. Those top specs are not in the included lane.

3. A paid exit from the wait

Skipping the wait is priced in credits. The live data says 50 credits; one buyer quoted 500, which is unresolved. Credits expire in 30 days and are non-refundable (Fair use, Terms). Proven for the policy The credit-to-dollar rate is not public. The video-edit product, Mirage, costs credits on every plan, Lifetime included. By the site's own wording it is a rebranded Seedance 2.5 edit mode (Mirage page). Proven

4. Cheap middlemen, not direct deals

The site's own code labels Seedance 2.5 as routed through "PiAPI". Kling 3.0 is PiAPI too. Other models are "via fal.ai" or "via OpenRouter" (labels read from the site's downloaded scripts). Proven as labels That does not prove which supplier served any single job. These firms resell access to other companies' AI models. PiAPI says it sells an "unofficial" Kling connection (PiAPI blog, 2024). Reported by PiAPI Nothing public shows a direct ByteDance contract.

5. A fixed price on the shelf, a wide exit in the contract

"Lifetime" is defined as the product's commercial life, not yours. The company may remove or replace models, and "excessive cost" is a listed reason (Terms of Service). Proven It may also slow, cap or credit-gate models, with no refund for that alone. The refund window closes after 14 days, and only if you made 5 videos or fewer and 5 images or fewer (Refund policy). Proven Archived copies show the refund test tightened between 4 and 29 October 2026.

6. Buyers who drift away

Many buyers will use it lightly or stop. Mobile subscription data shows only 21.1% of AI app annual subscribers remain after a year (RevenueCat). Inferred stand-in It is one dataset, and lifetime buyers are not subscribers. The effect is real but unmeasured for OmniRogue.

7. New buyers' cash

A lifetime sale pays up front for costs that arrive later. The research model shows this holds while sales stay strong and the average user is light. It fails within one to three years if sales fade and typical use is moderate. Inferred, from assumed usage and sales OmniRogue also sells credits, monthly plans, an agent product and a reseller program (Pricing, Enterprise). Proven that they are on offer Revenue is unknown.

Why the math does not work for a heavy user

After Stripe's card fee, OmniRogue keeps about $387 of each $399 (Stripe pricing). Seedance 2.5 at 720p costs about $0.085 a second at the cheapest reseller listing and about $0.473 on fal (Kie.ai, fal.ai). The middle estimate is $0.231 (second-hand BytePlus figure). What buyers report, and why it is not proof

Trustpilot shows 2.3 out of 5 from 37 reviews: 67% one star, 30% five star, 3% three star. The company has a claimed, paid profile and replies to many negative reviews (Trustpilot). Proven as what the page shows The reviews themselves are unverified allegations that Trustpilot does not fact-check.

The reports line up with each other and with the fair-use page.

  • A buyer paid $399 and found 10-second, 720p clips, one at a time, with waits up to 25 minutes.
  • Another reports a 15-minute cooldown, then a message that Unlimited was unavailable on the account, so everything needed credits.
  • Another reports a next-day refund request still unresolved and 30-minute waits.
  • Another reports a day-one refund request refused 22 days later, and a chargeback opened.
  • Another reports one-hour waits and that a group plans legal action. No filing was found.

All reported by buyers, unverified Earlier snapshots of the page showed a few vague five-star reviews first, with the one-star wave following. Inferred from review dates

What was not checked

No account was created. Nothing was bought. Nothing was generated. No checkout was opened. So the actual queue lengths are unknown. So is whether a Lifetime account really gets the 10,000 plus 1,000 monthly credits mentioned in site code. OmniRogue's wholesale prices and sales volume are unknown. No generated file was compared against a known supplier to test for model substitution, though the terms reserve the right to substitute models. The Florida filing documents, court records, complaint sites and trademark records were not searched. Meta and Google ad libraries were not checked, and no promoters were found. Official BytePlus and Kling price tables could not be read.

Conclusion

The deal is a rate-limited sampler priced as a lifetime product. The wait time, not the model list, is where the cost is controlled. Your read that the price is unsustainable holds for a heavy user. But the company has built exits that make a sudden collapse less likely than a slow narrowing of what Lifetime means.

The plan's weakest point is not cash today. It is supplier dependence, since the terms already name supplier price rises as a reason to cut models. The cheapest test for you as a competitor is public: watch the fair-use data file and the terms page. The refund and credit rules in the terms were already tightened between 4 and 29 October.
