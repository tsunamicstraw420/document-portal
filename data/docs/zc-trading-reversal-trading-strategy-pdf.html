LESSON 1: WHAT IS MEAN REVERSION?
Before learning the strategy, you need to understand what mean reversion trading actually is and
how it differs from the other main trading style.


The Core Concept
You are betting against momentum traders when they're in a weak position.


The Two Main Trading Styles
1. Momentum Trading
Momentum traders bet that the price will keep moving in the same direction. Also known as breakout
traders, they short breakdowns expecting price to continue lower.

2. Mean Reversion Trading
Mean reversion traders bet that the price will reverse direction. If price spikes up quickly, they sell
expecting it to fall back down.


Mean Reversion = Counter-Trading Momentum Traders
Every mean reversion trade is a bet against someone else. When you short a spike into resistance,
you're betting against the traders who just bought that spike. When you go long after a dump into
support, you're betting against traders who just shorted expecting continuation down.


The Key Insight
If momentum traders are weak, your mean reversion trade is more likely to work. If momentum
traders are strong, your mean reversion trade will probably fail.
LESSON 2: OPTIMAL TRADE ENVIRONMENT
Understanding that you're counter-trading momentum traders is your foundation. But you also need
to know when momentum traders are actually vulnerable.


The 4-Filter Checklist
   Filter 1: How Did Price Approach the Level?
       This is the MOST IMPORTANT filter. Analysis of 600+ trades shows:
       • Fast spikes: 70% win rate for mean reversion vs 30% for momentum
   ✓ WANT to see: Fast vertical spike into resistance (price shoots up in 1-2 large candles - shows
   exhaustion)
   ✗ DON'T want to see: Slow grinding staircase pattern (incremental progress with higher lows stacking up -
   controlled movement)

   Filter 2: What Did Volume Look Like?
       Volume tells you whether the move has real participation or exhaustion.
   ✓ WANT to see: Flat or decreasing volume as price approaches resistance (shows lack of sustained
   participation)
   ✗ DON'T want to see: Gradual increase in volume (fresh buyers arriving = fuel for momentum)

   Filter 3: Moving Average Crossovers
       This filter helps distinguish choppy markets (good for mean reversion) from trending markets (bad).
   ✓ WANT to see: Frequent crossovers + sideways moving average (indicates chop and indecision - mean
   reversion thrives here)
   ✗ DON'T want to see: Minimal crossovers + trending moving average (signals directional trend - mean
   reversion trades fail in trends)

   Filter 4: What Does the Left-Hand Side Price Action Look Like?
       Examine the previous 4-8 hours before your entry. When you trade, you're betting that left-side
       conditions will continue on the right side.
   ✓ WANT to see: Choppy sideways price action with levels being respected (not broken)
   ✗ DON'T want to see: Clean trending staircase with levels breaking consistently
LESSON 3: DRAWING LEVELS & IDENTIFYING SETUPS
Step 1: Draw Support and Resistance Levels
Support and resistance are price zones where buyers or sellers previously showed interest. These
levels represent points where significant order flow caused price to reverse.


Step 2: Await Your Entry Trigger on the 1-Minute Chart
Once you've identified your levels, switch to the 1-minute chart for precise entry timing. Your entry
trigger is: 1 candle close back inside the level after the spike.


For a Mean Reversion Short at Resistance:
    1. Price spikes up into your resistance level
    2. Price trades above the level briefly
    3. Wait for one complete 1-minute candle to close back below resistance
    4. That candle close is your entry trigger


Step 3: Align Setups with Optimal Conditions
Before entering, check all four filters. If any filter fails, reduce your risk. Only take full risk on A-grade
setups, not forcing trades in poor conditions.
LESSON 4: STOP LOSS & TAKE PROFIT LOGIC
Step 4: Stop Loss at the Previous Swing High (Minimum 1%)
A swing high is the highest wick in a local push up, followed by price moving away (failed to make
higher highs). Your stop loss goes at the most recent swing high before the spike.


The Minimum 1% Rule:
If the swing high is less than 1% away from your entry, skip it and use the next swing high up. Stops
that are too tight get hit by normal price noise.


Example:
You enter at $100. The nearest swing high is $100.50 (0.5% away). Skip it. Use the next swing high
at $101.50 (1.5% away). This becomes your stop.


Step 5: Take Profit at 1R (Equal Distance to Stop)
Your take profit target is 1R: the same distance as your stop loss, but in the profit direction. 1R means
your risk-to-reward ratio is 1:1.


Why 1R?
It's conservative and achievable. Mean reversion trades often hit 1R quickly because exhausted
moves reverse fast. You're not trying to catch the entire reversal—you're simply capturing a
high-probability piece of it.
