Why not 1-min

Koroush AK's exit-strategies article uses 1-min as a teaching timeframe because it gives beginners fast feedback. But 1-min is for full-time scalpers — high noise, decaying edge against HFT, hours of screen time per day. 4H gives cleaner signal, fewer false setups, and is compatible with checking charts twice a day.

VWAP needs ~30+ bars per session to be meaningful. Daily-anchored VWAP on a 4H chart shows only 6 bars per session — useless. Weekly fixes this.

Caveat: exit-rule numbers need re-validation

Koroush's 0.5R FTA cut, 2× time stop, and 0.9R-→-breakeven rule were EV-tested on 1-min data (10,000+ trades). The concepts port to 4H (they're about R-multiple psychology, not bar size), but the exact thresholds may differ. Our journal will re-validate each rule on 4H data before we treat the published numbers as authoritative. See Trade_Exit_Strategies.pdf.

The Three Dimensions of Trading Performance

Every trade — and every module in this system — must respect three interdependent dimensions. Weakness in any one prevents scaling.

DimensionWhat it meansSystem module
StrategyEdge identification, setup rules, asset selection, journalsrc/indicators/, src/signals/
RiskPosition sizing, stop/TP placement, daily/weekly limits, scalingsrc/risk/
PsycheDiscipline, routines, emotional management, behavioral trackingsrc/logging/, pre-trade checklist

Automation handles Strategy and Risk well. Psyche is handled through forced human confirmation (Phases 1-3) and behavioral metrics, not automation.

Two Core Strategies

Momentum (Breakout)

HypothesisStrength continues
ActionBuy above resistance / sell below support
EnvironmentStrong trends with volume
VWAP signaturePrice above VWAP, steep upward slope
Entry triggersPrice closes through level; volume ≥ 20-bar average; R:R ≥ 2:1
StopBelow recent swing low (or above swing high for shorts)

Mean Reversion (Reversal)

HypothesisPrice reverts to average
ActionShort fast spikes into resistance / long fast dumps into support
EnvironmentRange-bound, choppy
VWAP signaturePrice oscillating within ±1σ of VWAP, flat slope
Entry triggersFast spike (>2% in <5 min) into level with weakening volume; R:R ≥ 2:1
StopAbove/below spike extreme

Regime drives which strategy is active. Don't run breakouts in a chop tape; don't run reversals in a steep trend.

VWAP — the regime anchor

VWAP (volume-weighted average price) is the primary regime classifier. Implementation: src/indicators/vwap.py.

SignalReading
Position vs VWAPAbove = bullish bias. Below = bearish bias. Crossing = regime in transition.
Slope of VWAPSteep (>10° equivalent) = momentum. Flat = reversion. Reversing = caution.
Band width (±1σ)Wide = volatile. Narrow = compression — expect expansion.

Session-anchored (resets each UTC day by default) so the band reflects today's participation, not a rolling window.

Support & Resistance (ZCT Framework)

Levels by timeframe, in order of weight:

  1. Daily — primary structure; respected by most participants
  2. 4-hour — secondary structure
  3. 1-hour — intraday tradeable
  4. 15-minute — entry timing only

Classification:

  • Fresh (untested since formation) → higher probability, less noise
  • Recycled (retested ≥1 time) → more tests = stronger confirmation, but more participants watching

A trade taken against fresh daily resistance is qualitatively different from one against recycled 15-minute resistance.

Spike vs Grind

PatternDefinitionImplication
Fast spike>2% move in <5 minutesReversion candidate — fade into S/R
Slow grindSteady directional move over ≥15 minutes with rising volumeMomentum candidate — buy/short the breakout

The same level, hit by a spike vs a grind, gives opposite trades.

Trade Exits (Koroush AK's 6 rules)

Full detail: Trade_Exit_Strategies.pdf. Quick reference:

#RuleTypeTargets trap
1Cut at FTA — close beyond −0.5R → exit for −0.5RMechanicalFull hit on losers
2Time-based stop — cut at 2× avg-winner durationMechanicalFull hit on losers
3Volume taper after entry → cut earlyDiscretionaryMoney on table / full hit
4At +0.9R, move stop to +0.1RMechanicalWinner → loser
5Orderbook signals (thin book, trailing limits) → extend TPDiscretionaryMoney on table
63-pattern alignment (onside / grind / quality) → extend TPDiscretionaryMoney on table

The "3 traps" being avoided: (1) winner reverses to full stop, (2) leaving money on table, (3) full hit on losing trade despite warning signs.

Trade Management Defaults

DecisionDefault rule
Stop placementStructure-based: below swing low (momentum) or spike extreme (reversion)
Take-profit placementNext S/R level, or 2:1 R:R from entry, whichever comes first
Breakeven moveOptional: at +1R, move stop to break-even (or apply Rule 4 above for partials)
TrailingOnly in strong trends with confirming orderflow
NeverTrail a stop into loss

Pre-Trade Checklist (every trade)

A trade is only valid if every box is checked:

  • [ ] Strategy matches current regime (momentum strat in trending tape, reversion strat in chop)
  • [ ] Setup type is one of the documented patterns
  • [ ] S/R level identified with timeframe (daily / 4H / 1H / 15m)
  • [ ] R:R ≥ 2:1 from intended entry to intended TP
  • [ ] Position size matches risk-% rule for current account balance
  • [ ] Daily loss limit not breached
  • [ ] Weekly loss limit not breached
  • [ ] Trade context logged before entry (not after)

This checklist is the Psyche-dimension enforcement mechanism — it's the system saying "no" when discipline would otherwise slip.

Backtest Acceptance Criteria

Any new signal rule must clear all of these on out-of-sample data before going live:

MetricThreshold
Win rate≥ 50%
Profit factor≥ 1.8
Max drawdown< 15%
R:R realized≥ 2:1 average
Sample size≥ 6 months of bars

Forbidden during optimization (curve-fitting indicators): look-ahead bias, fitting to a single market regime, adding rules to explain individual losing trades, changing rules mid-test.
