LESSON 1: WHAT IS VWAP?
VWAP = Volume Weighted Average Price

Instead of treating every price move equally, it weights the price by trading volume to show you the
real average price of the day. Think of it as an anchor price that the market is gravitating around.


Why VWAP Matters
Random spikes and dumps can trick you if no volume is behind them. VWAP filters that noise and
shows where the real market commitment is. For example: if 90% of trades happen at $10 but the
price briefly spikes to $12, VWAP stays near $10, reflecting the fact that the upward move was only a
quick deviation from the overall trend.

    Bands: ±1 standard deviation

3 Key Signals of VWAP
    1. Position of VWAP <> Price: Breakouts or Breakdowns?
        • Price trending above VWAP = strong uptrend = favours breakouts (momentum longs)
        • Price trending below VWAP = strong downtrend = favours breakdowns (momentum shorts)
  2. The Slope of the VWAP: Is Price Trending or Ranging?
      • Steeper slope = price trending = favours momentum trades
      • Flat slope = price ranging = favours reversal trades

  3. VWAP Bands: Is Continuation or Reversal More Likely?
      • Wide bands = strength behind the move = favours continuation = trade momentum
      • Tight bands = weakness behind the move = favours reversals = trade mean reversion

LESSON 2: VWAP FOR BREAKOUT STRATEGIES
When you take a momentum trade, you are essentially betting on a continuation of the trend. In a
breakout strategy, that's a continued uptrend. In a breakdown strategy, that's a continued downtrend.


Directional Bias
    Price trending above VWAP = strong uptrend = favours breakouts (momentum longs)
    Price trending below VWAP = strong downtrend = favours breakdowns (momentum shorts)


Strategy Bias
    Steeper VWAP slope = price trending = favours momentum trades
    Wide VWAP bands = strength behind the move = favours continuation


Key Takeaways
    ✓ Momentum trades = betting on trend continuation
    ✓ Look to VWAP for signs of trend strength before entering
    ✓ Two key signals that favour continuation: steeper slope and wide bands
    ✓ Price position confirms direction: above VWAP for longs, below for shorts
LESSON 3: VWAP FOR REVERSAL STRATEGIES
When you take a reversal trade, you are basically betting on a reversal of the trend. In VWAP, we
want to see evidence of weakening of the trend.


Strategy Bias for Reversals
    Flat slope = price ranging = favours reversal trades
    Tight bands = weakness behind the move = favours reversals = trade mean reversion


Identifying Reversal Opportunities
VWAP tells you when the market is choppy, and this is where reversal trades perform best. Look for
tight bands forming a close range with an almost entirely flat slope.


Key Takeaways
    ✓ Reversal trades = betting on a reversal of the trend, not continuation
    ✓ Look to VWAP for signs of trend weakness before entering
    ✓ Two key signals that favour reversal: flat slope and tight bands
    ✓ Multiple VWAP crossovers with price can signal additional weakness
